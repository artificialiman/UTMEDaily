Engineering · Shared Core, Year 2

Engineering Economics

Money received on different dates cannot be added until it is moved to one date. Every method in this course does that move, then compares the results. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

Move, compare, decide

A cash flow has an amount and a date. Interest factors carry amounts to a common date, so that alternatives can be compared by present worth, annual worth or rate of return. Depreciation, tax, inflation and risk then adjust the cash flows before the comparison is made.

CHAPTER 1

The Ten Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · Interest and rates

Simple, compound, nominal, effective

F = P(1 + i)ⁿ
Rule: Convert to the rate of the period before using it. Compounding more often raises the effective rate.
Block 2 · Equivalence

Move each amount to one date

P = F/(1 + i)ⁿ
Rule: Present and future values of one stream are linked by one factor. Do not average two rates.
Block 3 · Annuities and gradients

Equal payments over time

P = A(P/A, i, n)
Rule: The present value falls one period before the first payment. An annuity due needs the extra (1 + i).
Block 4 · Comparing alternatives

Present worth, annual worth

NPV > 0
Rule: For unequal lives use annual worth. Salvage reduces cost. Capitalised cost divides by i.
Block 5 · Return and payback

IRR, payback, B/C

NPV(IRR) = 0
Rule: Payback ignores later cash. Interpolate IRR between close rates. Two sign changes can give two IRRs.
Block 6 · Depreciation

Spread cost over life

BV = C(1 − d)ⁿ
Rule: Declining balance acts on book value. Depreciation cuts tax, not cash.
Block 7 · Break-even

Contribution against fixed cost

Q = F/(p − v)
Rule: Only costs that change are relevant. The sales mix must stay fixed for a multi-product result.
Block 8 · Inflation

Real and nominal

(1 + i)/(1 + f) − 1
Rule: Use real flows with a real rate, or actual flows with a nominal rate, never a mixture.
Block 9 · Replacement and risk

Economic life, expected value

minimum AEC
Rule: Book value of the old asset is sunk. Compare market value and future costs.
Block 10 · Tax, loans, public works

After-tax view

ATCF = BTCF − t(BTCF − D)
Rule: Early loan payments are mostly interest. Interest is deductible. State the B/C convention.

Question 1

Solution

2I = P × i × n = 200 000 × 0.08 × 3 = ₦48 000.
3Total = 200 000 + 48 000 = ₦248 000.
Answerinterest ₦48 000; total ₦248 000

Question 28

Question 29

Solution

2(1.05/1.10)5 = 0.7919.
3PV = 100 000 × (1 − 0.7919)/0.05 = ₦415 059.
Answer₦415 059
Question 30
A ₦1 000 000 loan at 1.5% a month is repaid with ₦50 000 a month. Find the number of payments.

Answerabout 24 months

Questions 31–40. Present worth, annual worth and capitalised cost.

Question 31

Solution
1PW of returns = 300 000 × 3.7908 = ₦1 137 236.
2NPV = 1 137 236 − 1 000 000 = ₦137 236.
3NPV > 0 so the project is acceptable at 10%.
AnswerNPV = ₦137 236

Solution

2NPV = 300 000 × 3.3522 − 1 000 000 = ₦5 647.
Answer₦5 647
Common slips: NPV falls as the rate rises. It reaches zero at the internal rate of return.

Question 81
A machine costs ₦1 000 000. Resale value and running cost by the end of each year are: year 1, ₦700 000 and ₦100 000; year 2, ₦550 000 and ₦180 000; year 3, ₦400 000 and ₦280 000. At 10%, find the economic life.
Solution

4The lowest annual cost is at year 2.
Answereconomic life 2 years, AEC ₦452 381
Common slips: The economic life is where the equivalent annual cost is smallest, not where the machine stops working.
