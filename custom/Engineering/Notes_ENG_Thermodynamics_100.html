Engineering · Shared Core, Year 2

Thermodynamics I

Thermodynamics tracks energy as it changes form: heat into work, work into heat. Two laws govern every case, one that counts the energy and one that says which way it may flow. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

Count the energy, then check the direction

The first law balances energy: heat in minus work out equals the change in stored energy. The second law limits what that balance may do, because no engine turns all heat into work and heat never flows uphill by itself. Properties come from the gas law or from tables, and each device or cycle is a variation on the same two laws.

CHAPTER 1

The Ten Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · Basic concepts and gases

Absolute values only

pV = mRT
Rule: Add atmospheric pressure to a gauge reading and use kelvin. Real gases need Z or van der Waals.
Block 2 · Work, heat and specific heat

Area under the curve

W = ∫p dV
Rule: Work depends on the path. Heat at constant pressure needs cp; at constant volume it needs cv.
Block 3 · The first law for closed systems

Count the energy

Q − W = ΔU
Rule: Fix the signs first. An ideal gas has ΔU = mcvΔT in every process.
Block 4 · Steady-flow devices

Enthalpy in, enthalpy out

Q − W = ṁΔh
Rule: Nozzles trade enthalpy for speed. Throttles keep h constant. Turbines and compressors are adiabatic first.
Block 5 · Second law and heat engines

Limits on conversion

η = 1 − TL/TH
Rule: No engine beats Carnot. Use kelvin. A heat pump COP is the refrigerator COP plus one.
Block 6 · Entropy and efficiency

Entropy never falls overall

Δs = cp ln T − R ln p
Rule: Isentropic efficiency compares the real device with the ideal one. Exergy measures lost work.
Block 7 · Pure substances and steam

Read the tables

x = (v − vf)/vfg
Rule: Find the phase first, then the properties. A liquid barely changes with pressure.
Block 8 · Air-standard engine cycles

Compression ratio decides

η = 1 − 1/rk−1
Rule: Otto beats Diesel at the same ratio. Diesel engines use a far higher ratio.
Block 9 · Vapour and refrigeration cycles

Boiler, turbine, condenser, pump

η = wnet/qin
Rule: Reheat and feedwater heating raise efficiency. Reheat also dries the exhaust.
Block 10 · Engines, humidity, combustion and heat transfer

Practical figures

bp = Tω
Rule: Four-stroke engines fire once per two revolutions. Radiation needs kelvin to the fourth power.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 1

Basic concepts and gases

Questions 1–10. Pressure, temperature, the gas laws and real-gas corrections.

Question 1
A pressure gauge on a tank reads 250 kPa. The atmosphere is 101.3 kPa. Find the absolute pressure. Then find the absolute pressure in a vessel whose vacuum gauge reads 20 kPa.
Solution
1Absolute = gauge + atmospheric = 250 + 101.3 = 351.3 kPa.
2A vacuum reading is below atmospheric: absolute = 101.3 − 20 = 81.3 kPa.
Answer351.3 kPa; 81.3 kPa
Common slips: Property tables and gas laws need absolute pressure, never gauge.
Question 2
A U-tube mercury manometer (ρ = 13 600 kg/m3) shows a level difference of 0.35 m. Find the gauge pressure of the gas.
Solution
1p = ρgh = 13 600 × 9.81 × 0.35 = 46 696 Pa.
2= 46.7 kPa.
Answerp = 46.7 kPa (gauge)
Question 3
Convert 100 °C to kelvin and to degrees Fahrenheit.
Solution
1K = °C + 273.15 = 373.15 K.
2°F = 1.8 × 100 + 32 = 212 °F.
Answer373.15 K; 212 °F
Common slips: A temperature difference of 1 K equals 1 °C but 1.8 °F.
Question 4
2 kg of air at 300 K fills a 0.5 m3 vessel. Find the pressure. (R = 0.287 kJ/kg·K)
Solution
1pV = mRT.
2p = mRT/V = 2 × 0.287 × 300/0.5.
3= 344.4 kPa.
Answerp = 344.4 kPa
Question 5
A 0.2 m3 tank holds air at 500 kPa and 25 °C. Find the mass of air.
Solution
1T = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15 K.
2m = pV/RT = 500 × 0.2/(0.287 × 298.15).
3= 1.169 kg.
Answerm = 1.169 kg
Common slips: Use kelvin in the gas law. 25 would give a mass about twelve times too large.
Question 6
88 g of carbon dioxide (molar mass 44 g/mol) is held at 0 °C and 101.325 kPa. Find the number of moles and the volume.
Solution
1n = mass/molar mass = 88/44 = 2 mol.
2V = nRuT/p = 2 × 8.314 × 273.15/101.325 = 44.83 L.
3Check with molar volume: 2 × 22.41 = 44.83 L.
Answern = 2 mol; V = 44.83 L
Common slips: At the same temperature and pressure one mole of any ideal gas has the same volume (Avogadro).
Question 7
A mixture holds 0.5 mol of oxygen and 1.5 mol of nitrogen at a total pressure of 200 kPa. Find the partial pressures.
Solution
1Mole fractions: O2 = 0.5/2 = 0.25, N2 = 1.5/2 = 0.75.
2Partial pressure = mole fraction × total.
3p(O2) = 50 kPa; p(N2) = 150 kPa.
Answerp(O2) = 50 kPa; p(N2) = 150 kPa
Common slips: Partial pressures add to the total (Dalton).
Question 8
Find the specific gas constant of carbon dioxide (M = 44) and of helium (M = 4.003). Ru = 8.314 kJ/kmol·K.
Solution
1R = Ru/M.
2CO2: 8.314/44 = 0.1890 kJ/kg·K.
3He: 8.314/4.003 = 2.077 kJ/kg·K.
AnswerCO2 0.189; He 2.077 kJ/kg·K
Question 9
A gas with R = 0.30 kJ/kg·K is at 8 MPa and 350 K and has compressibility factor Z = 0.85. Find its specific volume and compare it with the ideal-gas value.
Solution
1Ideal: v = RT/p = 0.30 × 350/8000 = 0.013 125 m3/kg.
2Real: v = ZRT/p = 0.85 × 0.013 125 = 0.011 156 m3/kg.
3The real gas is 15% denser than the ideal-gas law predicts.
Answerv = 0.01116 m3/kg, against 0.01313 for an ideal gas
Common slips: Z below 1 means attraction between molecules dominates. Z above 1 means repulsion dominates.
Question 10
Nitrogen has van der Waals constants a = 0.137 Pa·m6/mol2 and b = 3.87 × 10−5 m3/mol. Find the pressure at 300 K when the molar volume is 0.001 m3/mol, and compare with the ideal gas.
Solution
1p = RT/(v − b) − a/v2.
2RT/(v − b) = 8.314 × 300/(0.001 − 0.0000387) = 2.5946 × 106 Pa.
3a/v2 = 0.137/10−6 = 0.137 × 106 Pa.
4p = 2.458 MPa. The ideal gas gives RT/v = 2.494 MPa.
Answerp = 2.458 MPa (ideal gas 2.494 MPa)

Work, heat and specific heat

Questions 11–20. Specific heats, boundary work, sensible and latent heat.

Question 11
A gas has R = 0.287 kJ/kg·K and k = cp/cv = 1.4. Find cp and cv.
Solution
1cp − cv = R and cp = k cv.
2cv = R/(k − 1) = 0.287/0.4 = 0.7175 kJ/kg·K.
3cp = kR/(k − 1) = 1.4 × 0.287/0.4 = 1.0045 kJ/kg·K.
Answercv = 0.7175; cp = 1.0045 kJ/kg·K
Question 12
How much heat raises 5 kg of water from 20 °C to 80 °C? (c = 4.186 kJ/kg·K)
Solution
1Q = mcΔT = 5 × 4.186 × 60.
2= 1255.8 kJ.
AnswerQ = 1255.8 kJ
Question 13
Find the heat to boil 2 kg of water at 100 °C (hfg = 2257 kJ/kg) and to melt 3 kg of ice at 0 °C (latent heat 334 kJ/kg).
Solution
1Boiling: Q = m hfg = 2 × 2257 = 4514 kJ.
2Melting: Q = 3 × 334 = 1002 kJ.
3The temperature stays constant during both.
AnswerBoiling 4514 kJ; melting 1002 kJ
Common slips: Latent heat changes the phase, not the temperature.
Question 14
A 0.5 kg copper block (c = 0.385 kJ/kg·K) at 100 °C is dropped into 1 kg of water at 20 °C in an insulated container. Find the final temperature.
Solution
1Heat lost by copper = heat gained by water.
20.5 × 0.385 × (100 − T) = 1 × 4.186 × (T − 20).
319.25 − 0.1925T = 4.186T − 83.72.
4T = 102.97/4.3785 = 23.5 °C.
AnswerT = 23.5 °C
Question 15
A gas expands at a constant pressure of 300 kPa from 0.2 m3 to 0.5 m3. Find the work done.
Solution
1W = p(V2 − V1) = 300 × 0.3.
2= 90 kJ, done by the gas.
AnswerW = 90 kJ by the gas
Question 16
1 kg of air at 300 K is compressed isothermally from 100 kPa to 500 kPa. Find the boundary work.
Solution
1W = RT ln(V2/V1) = RT ln(p1/p2).
2= 0.287 × 300 × ln(0.2).
3= 86.1 × (−1.6094) = −138.6 kJ.
4The negative sign means work is done on the gas.
AnswerW = −138.6 kJ (on the gas)
Question 17
A gas follows pV1.3 = constant. It expands from 0.1 m3 at 600 kPa to 0.4 m3. Find the final pressure and the work.
Solution
1p2 = 600 × (0.1/0.4)1.3 = 600 × 0.1649 = 98.96 kPa.
2W = (p1V1 − p2V2)/(n − 1) = (60 − 39.59)/0.3.
3= 68.0 kJ.
Answerp2 = 99.0 kPa; W = 68.0 kJ
Common slips: The polytropic work formula fails when n = 1. That case is isothermal and uses a logarithm.
Question 18
A gas expands so that its pressure falls in a straight line from 600 kPa at 0.1 m3 to 200 kPa at 0.3 m3. Find the work.
Solution
1The work is the area under the line on a p–V diagram.
2Average pressure = (600 + 200)/2 = 400 kPa.
3W = 400 × (0.3 − 0.1) = 80 kJ.
AnswerW = 80 kJ
Question 19
2 kg of air is heated from 300 K to 400 K, first at constant volume and then at constant pressure. Find the heat in each case and explain the difference.
Solution
1Constant volume: Q = mcvΔT = 2 × 0.718 × 100 = 143.6 kJ.
2Constant pressure: Q = mcpΔT = 2 × 1.005 × 100 = 201.0 kJ.
3Difference = 57.4 kJ = mRΔT, the work the gas does pushing the boundary out.
Answer143.6 kJ at constant volume; 201.0 kJ at constant pressure
Question 20
A gas goes from state 1 (100 kPa, 0.1 m3) to state 2 (300 kPa, 0.3 m3). Path A expands at 100 kPa to 0.3 m3 and then raises the pressure at constant volume. Path B raises the pressure at 0.1 m3 first and then expands at 300 kPa. Find the work on each path.
Solution
1Path A: W = 100 × (0.3 − 0.1) = 20 kJ. The constant-volume step does no work.
2Path B: W = 300 × (0.3 − 0.1) = 60 kJ.
3The end states are identical but the work differs, so work depends on the path.
AnswerPath A 20 kJ; path B 60 kJ
Common slips: Work and heat are path functions. Pressure, temperature and internal energy are point functions.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 3

The first law for closed systems

Questions 21–30. Constant-volume, constant-pressure, isothermal, adiabatic and polytropic processes.

Question 21
A system receives 50 kJ of heat and does 20 kJ of work. Find the change in internal energy. Repeat if 20 kJ of work is done on the system instead.
Solution
1First law: ΔU = Q − W, with W positive when done by the system.
2Work by the system: ΔU = 50 − 20 = 30 kJ.
3Work on the system: W = −20, so ΔU = 50 − (−20) = 70 kJ.
Answer30 kJ; 70 kJ
Common slips: Fix the sign convention first: heat in positive, work out positive.
Question 22
2 kg of air is heated in a rigid vessel from 300 K to 500 K. Find the heat and the work.
Solution
1Constant volume gives W = 0.
2Q = ΔU = mcvΔT = 2 × 0.718 × 200.
3= 287.2 kJ.
AnswerQ = 287.2 kJ; W = 0
Question 23
2 kg of air is heated at constant pressure from 300 K to 500 K. Find Q, W and ΔU.
Solution
1ΔU = mcvΔT = 2 × 0.718 × 200 = 287.2 kJ.
2W = pΔV = mRΔT = 2 × 0.287 × 200 = 114.8 kJ.
3Q = ΔU + W = 402.0 kJ, which is mcpΔT = 2 × 1.005 × 200.
AnswerQ = 402.0 kJ; W = 114.8 kJ; ΔU = 287.2 kJ
Question 24
Show that the heat added at constant pressure equals the change in enthalpy. Use 2 kg of air heated 300 K to 500 K.
Solution
1Enthalpy H = U + pV. At constant pressure, Q = ΔU + pΔV = ΔH.
2ΔH = mcpΔT = 2 × 1.005 × 200 = 402 kJ.
3This equals the heat found for the constant-pressure heating.
AnswerΔH = 402 kJ = Q
Question 25
1 kg of air at 300 K is compressed isothermally from 100 kPa to 400 kPa. Find the work and the heat.
Solution
1For an ideal gas the internal energy depends on temperature only, so ΔU = 0.
2W = RT ln(p1/p2) = 0.287 × 300 × ln(0.25) = −119.4 kJ.
3Q = W = −119.4 kJ. The heat leaves the gas.
AnswerW = −119.4 kJ; Q = −119.4 kJ
Question 26
1 kg of air at 100 kPa and 300 K is compressed adiabatically to one-eighth of its volume. Find the final temperature, final pressure and work.
Solution
1For an adiabatic process TVk−1 is constant: T2 = 300 × 80.4 = 689.2 K.
2pVk is constant: p2 = 100 × 81.4 = 1837.9 kPa.
3Q = 0, so W = −Δu = −0.718 × 389.2 = −279.5 kJ.
AnswerT2 = 689.2 K; p2 = 1838 kPa; W = −279.5 kJ
Common slips: Adiabatic means no heat transfer. The temperature still changes, because work changes the internal energy.
Question 27
Air at 100 kPa and 300 K is compressed adiabatically to 500 kPa. Find the final temperature and the work per kilogram.
Solution
1T2 = T1(p2/p1)(k−1)/k = 300 × 50.2857 = 475.1 K.
2w = R(T1 − T2)/(k − 1) = 0.287 × (−175.1)/0.4.
3= −125.7 kJ/kg.
AnswerT2 = 475.1 K; w = −125.7 kJ/kg
Question 28
1 kg of air at 100 kPa and 300 K is compressed to 600 kPa along pV1.3 = constant. Find T2, the work and the heat transfer.
Solution
1T2 = 300 × 60.3/1.3 = 300 × 1.512 = 453.6 K.
2w = R(T2 − T1)/(1 − n) = 0.287 × 153.6/(−0.3) = −146.9 kJ/kg.
3Δu = 0.718 × 153.6 = 110.3 kJ/kg.
4q = Δu + w = 110.3 − 146.9 = −36.7 kJ/kg. Heat is removed.
AnswerT2 = 453.6 K; w = −146.9 kJ/kg; q = −36.7 kJ/kg
Question 29
1 kg of air at 400 kPa fills 0.1 m3 of an insulated rigid tank. A valve opens to an evacuated 0.2 m3 tank and the air spreads into both. Find the final pressure and the temperature change.
Solution
1No heat, no work: Q = 0 and W = 0, so ΔU = 0.
2For an ideal gas ΔU = 0 means the temperature is unchanged.
3p2 = p1V1/V2 = 400 × 0.1/0.3 = 133.3 kPa.
Answerp2 = 133.3 kPa; ΔT = 0
Common slips: Free expansion into a vacuum does no work, because there is nothing to push against.
Question 30
A cycle of three processes has Q12 = +500 kJ, Q23 = −350 kJ and Q31 = +30 kJ, with W12 = +300 kJ and W23 = −100 kJ. Find the net work and W31.
Solution
1Over a cycle ΔU = 0, so net work = net heat.
2Σ Q = 500 − 350 + 30 = 180 kJ.
3W31 = 180 − 300 − (−100) = −20 kJ.
AnswerNet work 180 kJ; W31 = −20 kJ

Question 31

The second law and heat engines

Questions 41–50. Efficiency, COP and the Carnot cycle.

Question 41
An engine takes 800 kJ from a hot source and rejects 500 kJ. Find the work and the thermal efficiency.
Solution
1W = 800 − 500 = 300 kJ.
2η = W/QH = 300/800 = 0.375.
AnswerW = 300 kJ; η = 37.5%
Question 42
An engine with thermal efficiency 0.30 rejects 70 kW. Find the heat input and the power.
Solution
1QL = QH(1 − η), so 70 = 0.70 QH.
2QH = 100 kW.
3W = 100 − 70 = 30 kW.
AnswerHeat input 100 kW; power 30 kW
Question 43
A refrigerator removes 6 kW from the cold space using 2 kW of work. Find the COP and the heat rejected.
Solution
1COP = QL/W = 6/2 = 3.
2QH = QL + W = 8 kW.
AnswerCOP = 3; heat rejected 8 kW
Question 44
A heat pump gives 12 kW of heat using 3 kW of electricity. Find its COP and the COP of the same machine run as a refrigerator.
Solution
1COPHP = QH/W = 12/3 = 4.
2Heat taken from outside = 12 − 3 = 9 kW.
3COPR = 9/3 = 3 = COPHP − 1.
AnswerCOPHP = 4; COPR = 3
Question 45
Find the Carnot efficiency between 800 K and 300 K.
Solution
1η = 1 − TL/TH = 1 − 300/800.
2= 0.625.
Answerη = 62.5%
Common slips: Use absolute temperatures. Using 800 and 300 in degrees Celsius is wrong.
Question 46
A Carnot engine takes 100 kW from a source at 600 K and rejects heat at 300 K. Find the power and the heat rejected.
Solution
1η = 1 − 300/600 = 0.5.
2W = 0.5 × 100 = 50 kW.
3QL = 100 − 50 = 50 kW.
AnswerW = 50 kW; heat rejected 50 kW
Question 47
A Carnot refrigerator works between −10 °C and 30 °C. Find its COP and the power for a 5 kW cooling load.
Solution
1TL = 263.15 K; TH = 303.15 K.
2COP = TL/(TH − TL) = 263.15/40 = 6.58.
3W = 5/6.58 = 0.760 kW.
AnswerCOP = 6.58; W = 0.76 kW
Question 48
A Carnot heat pump keeps a building at 20 °C when the outside is 0 °C. Find the COP and the power for 10 kW of heating.
Solution
1TH = 293.15 K; TL = 273.15 K.
2COP = TH/(TH − TL) = 293.15/20 = 14.66.
3W = 10/14.66 = 0.682 kW.
AnswerCOP = 14.66; W = 0.68 kW

Question 49
An inventor claims an engine between 500 K and 300 K with an efficiency of 0.45. Is the claim possible?
Solution
1The Carnot efficiency is the highest possible: 1 − 300/500 = 0.40.
2No engine can exceed 0.40 between these temperatures.
30.45 is above the limit, so the claim breaks the second law.
AnswerImpossible: 0.45 exceeds the Carnot limit of 0.40
Question 50
A Carnot cycle uses 1 kg of air. Isothermal expansion at 600 K doubles the volume, and heat is rejected at 300 K. Find the heat added, the efficiency and the net work.
Solution

Entropy and efficiency

Questions 51–60. Entropy change, generation, isentropic efficiency and exergy.

Question 51
300 kJ of heat flows reversibly into a system at a constant 400 K. Find the entropy change.
Solution
1ΔS = Q/T for a reversible process at constant temperature.
2= 300/400 = 0.75 kJ/K.
AnswerΔS = 0.75 kJ/K
Question 52
1 kg of air is heated from 300 K to 600 K, first at constant volume and then at constant pressure. Find the entropy change in each case.
Solution
1Constant volume: Δs = cv ln(T2/T1) = 0.718 × ln 2 = 0.498 kJ/kg·K.
2Constant pressure: Δs = cp ln(T2/T1) = 1.005 × 0.6931 = 0.697 kJ/kg·K.
Answer0.498 kJ/kg·K at constant volume; 0.697 kJ/kg·K at constant pressure
Question 53
Air goes from 300 K and 100 kPa to 500 K and 400 kPa. Find the entropy change per kilogram.

Common slips: Resistances in series add. The material with the highest R controls the loss.
Question 100
A 2 m2 surface at 400 K loses heat to air at 360 K by convection with h = 25 W/m2·K, and by radiation to surroundings at 300 K with emissivity 0.9. Find each loss. (σ = 5.67 × 10−8 W/m2·K4)
Solution
1Convection: Q = hAΔT = 25 × 2 × 40 = 2000 W.
2Radiation: Q = εσA(Ts4 − T04) = 0.9 × 5.67 × 10−8 × 2 × (4004 − 3004).
3= 0.9 × 5.67 × 10−8 × 2 × 1.75 × 1010 = 1786 W.
4Total = 3786 W.
AnswerConvection 2000 W; radiation 1786 W; total 3786 W
Common slips: Radiation uses kelvin raised to the fourth power. A temperature in degrees Celsius gives nonsense.
