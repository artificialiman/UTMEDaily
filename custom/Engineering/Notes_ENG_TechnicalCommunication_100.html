Engineering · Shared Core, Year 2

Technical Communication

An engineering result is useful only when the reader can find it, trust it and act on it. The skills in this course are choosing the reader, writing plainly, showing numbers and data correctly, and organising the document so the answer comes first. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

Reader, message, form

Every document starts with who will read it and what they must do afterwards. The message follows from that, and the form (memo, report, procedure, slide, poster) follows from the message. Numbers, figures and sources then carry the evidence, and the order puts the decision first.

CHAPTER 1

The Ten Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · Purpose, audience, tone

Start from the reader

verb + object + outcome
Rule: Put the decision first. Remove blame from tone. Avoid idioms for readers who use English as a second language.
Block 2 · Clear sentences

One idea, active voice

actor, verb, object
Rule: Cut padding, name the actor, keep list items parallel, and attach every opening phrase to the right subject.
Block 3 · Numbers and units

SI form and honest precision

space + symbol
Rule: Multiplication keeps the fewest significant figures; addition keeps the fewest decimal places.
Block 4 · Tables and figures

Show the data truthfully

quantity (unit)
Rule: Label both axes, start bars at zero, put the caption above a table and below a figure.
Block 5 · Technical reports

Front, body, back

what / why / how / result
Rule: Results state, discussion explains, conclusions follow from evidence, recommendations ask for action.
Block 6 · Correspondence

Purpose first, one action

subject line + date
Rule: Match the closing to the greeting, state the fact, the effect and the remedy, and give a date.
Block 7 · Instructions and specifications

One action to a step

imperative
Rule: Put the warning before the step. Use shall for requirements. Give every requirement a number and a test.
Block 8 · Sources and citation

Credit and verify

quote or paraphrase, then cite
Rule: Copied words need marks as well as a citation. Rank sources by authority.
Block 9 · Proposals and planning

Specific, measured, dated

SMART
Rule: The critical path is the longest path. Contingency is a percentage of the subtotal.
Block 10 · Presentations and meetings

Time, text size, record

words = rate × minutes
Rule: Plan slides from speaking time. Keep one idea per slide. Minutes record decision, owner, date.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 1

Purpose, audience and tone

Questions 1–10. Choose the reader, the form and the level before writing.

Question 1
A report on a failed borehole pump goes to two readers: the village head and the state water engineer. State what changes between the two versions.
Solution
1The facts stay the same; the order and the depth change.
2The village head needs the cause, the cost, and what to do next, in plain words.
3The engineer needs the data, the method, the pump specification and the standard used.
4Write the village head’s version as a one-page summary and put the engineer’s detail in the body and appendices.
Answersame facts; summary for the village head, full data for the engineer
Common slips: Writing one document for both readers serves neither of them.
Question 2
You must tell your supervisor today that the test rig will be two days late. Choose the document and write the first sentence.
Solution
1One reader, one fact, urgent: an email or short memo, not a report.
2Put the fact in the first sentence: ‘The test rig will be ready on 16 March, two days later than planned.’
3Give the reason and the effect in the next two sentences.
Answershort email or memo; delay stated in the first sentence
Question 3
Write a one-sentence purpose statement for a report on testing three concrete mixes.
Solution
1A purpose statement has a verb, an object and an outcome.
2‘This report compares the compressive strength of three concrete mixes at 28 days and recommends one for the floor slab.’
AnswerThis report compares the compressive strength of three concrete mixes at 28 days and recommends one for the floor slab.
Question 4
Classify each document as informing, instructing or persuading: a data sheet, a funding proposal, an assembly manual.
Solution
1A data sheet states facts for the reader to use: informing.
2A funding proposal asks for money and must convince: persuading.
3An assembly manual tells the reader what to do: instructing.
Answerdata sheet: informing; proposal: persuading; manual: instructing
Question 5
Rewrite for tone: ‘You obviously did not read the manual and broke the pump.’
Solution
1Remove the blame and state the fact.
2Add the action.
3‘The pump failed during the test. Section 3 of the manual lists the start-up checks, and we can go through them together before the next run.’
AnswerThe pump failed during the test. Section 3 of the manual lists the start-up checks, and we can go through them together before the next run.
Common slips: Blame makes the reader defensive and hides the fix.
Question 6
A 40-page feasibility study must become a 2-page brief for the managing director. What share of the length remains, and what stays?
Solution
1Share = 2/40 = 5%.
2Keep what the director needs to decide: the recommendation, the cost, the main risk and the decision wanted.
3Leave out derivations, raw data and method detail; point to the full study.
Answer5% of the length; recommendation, cost, risk, decision needed
Question 7
Put these in the order a decision-maker needs them: background, cost, recommendation, method details, risks.
Solution
1Start with the answer: recommendation.
2Then what it costs, then what could go wrong.
3Background next, and method detail last, or in an appendix.
Answerrecommendation, cost, risks, background, method details
Common slips: Chronological order suits a diary, not a decision-maker.
Question 8
Rewrite: ‘I think the bridge is probably okay.’ The measured deflection is 12 mm and the limit is 18 mm.
Solution
1Replace opinion with evidence and the standard.
2‘The measured deflection of 12 mm is within the 18 mm limit.’
AnswerThe measured deflection of 12 mm is within the 18 mm limit.
Question 9
Replace two idioms for readers who use English as a second language: ‘we are back to square one’ and ‘a ballpark figure’.
Solution
1‘We are back to square one’ becomes ‘the design must start again’.
2‘A ballpark figure’ becomes ‘an approximate figure’.
Answerthe design must start again; an approximate figure
Common slips: Idioms translate badly and may not be understood at all.
Question 10
A reader has two minutes and reads at about 240 words a minute. How long can the document be?
Solution
1Words = 2 × 240 = 480.
2A one-page summary of about 450 words fits.
Answerabout 480 words
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 2

Clear sentences

Questions 11–20. Concise, active, parallel and unambiguous wording.

Question 11
Shorten: ‘Due to the fact that the voltage was low, the motor did not start.’ State the words saved.
Solution
1‘Due to the fact that’ means ‘because’.
2‘Because the voltage was low, the motor did not start.’
3Words: 14 down to 10, a saving of 4, or 29%.
AnswerBecause the voltage was low, the motor did not start. (4 words saved)
Question 12
Make active: ‘The beam was loaded by the technician until failure occurred.’
Solution
1Find the actor: the technician.
2Put the actor first, then the verb, then the object.
3‘The technician loaded the beam until it failed.’
AnswerThe technician loaded the beam until it failed.
Question 13
Remove the nominalisation: ‘An investigation of the cracks was conducted by the team.’
Solution
1‘Investigation’ hides the verb ‘investigate’.
2‘The team investigated the cracks.’
AnswerThe team investigated the cracks.
Question 14
Which sentence may stay passive? (a) ‘The operator closed the valve.’ (b) ‘Samples were dried at 105 °C for 24 h.’
Solution
1Use the passive when the actor does not matter and the action does.
2In (b) it does not matter who dried the samples, so the passive suits a methods section.
3In (a) the actor matters; the active is shorter and clearer.
Answer(b) stays passive; (a) should be active
Common slips: Passive is not an error. It is wrong only when it hides who did the work.
Question 15
Correct the dangling modifier: ‘Walking across the site, the crane was visible.’
Solution
1The crane cannot walk, so the opening phrase has no subject to attach to.
2Give it a subject: ‘Walking across the site, we saw the crane.’
AnswerWalking across the site, we saw the crane.
Question 16
Make parallel: ‘The student must collect data, analysing it, and a report writing.’
Solution
1List items must share a form.
2All three as verbs: ‘The student must collect data, analyse it and write a report.’
AnswerThe student must collect data, analyse it and write a report.
Question 17
Remove the ambiguity: ‘The engineer told the technician that he had to recalibrate the meter.’
Solution
1‘He’ could mean either person.
2Use a direct quotation or name the person: ‘The engineer told the technician, “You must recalibrate the meter.”’
AnswerThe engineer told the technician, “You must recalibrate the meter.”
Question 18
Explain a hydraulic conductivity of 1 × 10−5 m/s to villagers.
Solution
1Time for water to move 1 m = 1/10−5 = 100 000 s.
2100 000/3600 = 27.8 h.
3‘Water moves slowly through the soil here, about 1 metre in a little over a day.’
AnswerWater moves about 1 metre in a little over a day
Common slips: Give the lay reader a size they can picture, not a unit they cannot.
Question 19
Split this 35-word sentence: ‘The pump, which was installed in 2019 by a contractor who has since closed the business, has failed three times, and each failure was caused by dry running because the level sensor was never connected.’
Solution
1Find the separate ideas: the pump failed; the cause; who installed it.
2‘The pump has failed three times. Each failure was caused by dry running, because the level sensor was never connected. A contractor installed the pump in 2019 and has since closed the business.’
3Three sentences of 6, 14 and 13 words.
Answerthree shorter sentences, one idea each
Question 20
Cut the redundant words: past history, end result, completely eliminate, advance planning, each and every.
Solution
1History is always past; a result is always at the end; to eliminate is already complete; planning is always in advance; ‘each and every’ repeats itself.
2Use: history, result, eliminate, planning, each.
Answerhistory; result; eliminate; planning; each
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 3

Numbers, units and SI notation

Questions 21–30. Prefixes, symbols, significant figures and rounding.

Question 21
Convert 4700 W to kW and 0.0032 A to mA.
Solution
1kilo = 103: 4700/1000 = 4.7 kW.
2milli = 10−3: 0.0032 × 1000 = 3.2 mA.
Answer4.7 kW; 3.2 mA
Question 22
Convert 2.5 MPa to kPa and to pascals.
Solution
1mega = 106, kilo = 103.
22.5 MPa = 2500 kPa.
3= 2.5 × 106 Pa.
Answer2500 kPa; 2.5 × 106 Pa
Question 23
Correct the units: 25mm, 10 Kg, 5 sec.
Solution
1Leave a space between number and unit.
2Kilo is a lower-case k: kg.
3The symbol for second is s, with no full stop.
425 mm, 10 kg, 5 s.
Answer25 mm; 10 kg; 5 s
Common slips: Symbols are not abbreviations; they do not take a full stop or a plural s.
Question 24
How many significant figures are in 0.004050, 120 and 1.20 × 102?
Solution
1Leading zeros do not count; the trailing zero after the decimal point does: 0.004050 has 4.
2120 is ambiguous; it may have 2 or 3.
31.20 × 102 has 3, because the trailing zero is shown.
Answer4; ambiguous (2 or 3); 3
Question 25
Round 12.345 to 3 significant figures and 0.0856 to 2.
Solution
112.345: the third figure is 3 and the next digit is 4, so it stays: 12.3.
20.0856: the first two figures are 8 and 5, and the next digit is 6, so round up: 0.086.
Answer12.3; 0.086
Question 26
A floor measures 12.5 m by 3.2 m. State the area to a suitable precision.
Solution
112.5 × 3.2 = 40.0 m2.
2In multiplication the result has as many significant figures as the least precise factor: 3.2 has two.
3Area = 40 m2.
Answer40 m2
Common slips: Reporting 40.00 m2 claims a precision the tape measure did not give.
Question 27
Add 12.11 m + 0.3 m + 1.457 m.
Solution
1Sum = 13.867 m.
2In addition the result keeps as many decimal places as the least precise value: 0.3 has one.
3Total = 13.9 m.
Answer13.9 m
Question 28
Write 0.000 065 and 45 000 000 in scientific notation, and the second in engineering notation.
Solution
10.000 065 = 6.5 × 10−5.
245 000 000 = 4.5 × 107.
3Engineering notation uses powers that are multiples of 3: 45 × 106.
Answer6.5 × 10−5; 4.5 × 107; 45 × 106
Question 29
Convert 72 km/h to m/s.
Solution
172 km = 72 000 m and 1 h = 3600 s.
272 000/3600 = 20 m/s.
3Shortcut: divide km/h by 3.6.
Answer20 m/s
Question 30
Write 1234567.891 with digit grouping in SI style.
Solution
1Group the digits in threes, counting from the decimal point in both directions, with a thin space between groups and no commas.
21 234 567.891.
Answer1 234 567.891
Common slips: A comma may be the decimal sign in some countries, so it is not used to separate thousands in SI style.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 4

Tables and figures

Questions 31–40. Choose the display and label it correctly.

Question 31
Choose a chart for each: monthly power use over a year, and the share of a budget by category.
Solution
1A trend over time is shown by a line chart.
2A share of a whole across a few categories can be shown by a bar chart, or a pie chart when there are five or fewer slices.
Answerline chart for the trend; bar (or pie, five or fewer slices) for the share
Question 32
Label the axes of a graph titled ‘Load against extension’.
Solution
1Each axis needs the quantity and the unit.
2Horizontal: ‘Extension (mm)’. Vertical: ‘Load (kN)’.
3‘A against B’ puts A on the vertical axis and B on the horizontal axis.
Answerx: Extension (mm); y: Load (kN)
Question 33
Where do the captions go for a table and for a figure?
Solution
1The reader reads a table top to bottom, so the caption sits above it.
2A figure is viewed as a whole, and its caption sits below it.
Answertable caption above; figure caption below
Question 34
A bar chart starts its vertical axis at 95. The bars are 96 and 98. Compare the heights drawn with the true difference.
Solution
1Drawn heights above 95 are 1 and 3, so the second bar looks 3 times as tall.
2True ratio = 98/96 = 1.021, a 2.1% difference.
3Start bar charts at zero.
Answerlooks 3 times taller; truly 2.1% more
Common slips: Truncated axes exaggerate small differences.
Question 35
List the design rules for a data table of measured lengths.
Solution
1Put the unit in the column heading, not in each cell.
2Align decimals and use the same number of decimal places in a column.
3Use light horizontal rules; leave out vertical lines.
Answerunits in headings; aligned decimals; same precision; horizontal rules only
Question 36
Why must every number in a column carry the same number of decimal places?
Solution
1Different precision in a column suggests different accuracy.
2It also stops the digits lining up, so the reader cannot compare sizes at a glance.
312.50, 9.00 and 10.25 rather than 12.5, 9 and 10.25.
Answerto show equal precision and allow alignment
Question 37
Sieve analysis gives percentage passing at nine continuous sieve sizes. Bar chart or line graph?
Solution
1The sizes form a continuous scale, not separate categories.
2A line graph, usually with a log scale for size, shows the grading curve.
Answerline graph
Common slips: Bars suit separate categories such as ‘cement’, ‘sand’, ‘gravel’.
Question 38
How should the text refer to a figure?
Solution
1Introduce the figure before it appears, by number.
2Say what it shows: ‘Figure 3 shows the strength rising with curing time.’
3Do not write ‘the figure below’; the layout may change.
Answerrefer by number and state what it shows
Question 39
Data run from 0 to 87. Choose the axis range and tick spacing.
Solution
1Let the axis end just above the largest value at a round number: 100, or 90.
2Use ticks at 20, giving 0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100: six labels, easy to read.
3Avoid awkward steps such as 0, 17, 34.
Answer0 to 100, ticks every 20
Question 40
A pie chart has eight slices of similar size. What should replace it?
Solution
1Slices of similar size cannot be compared by angle.
2A bar chart sorted from largest to smallest makes the order obvious.
Answera sorted bar chart
Common slips: Use a pie chart only for a few unequal parts of one whole.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 5

Technical reports

Questions 41–50. Structure, abstracts and the content of each section.

Question 41
Put the sections of a technical report in order: results, abstract, references, introduction, discussion, conclusions, methods, contents list, title page.
Solution
1Front matter: title page, abstract, contents list.
2Body: introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusions.
3Back matter: references, then appendices.
Answertitle page, abstract, contents, introduction, methods, results, discussion, conclusions, references
Question 42
How do an abstract and an executive summary differ?
Solution
1An abstract is 150 to 250 words for readers deciding whether to read the report.
2An executive summary is for managers who may not read further, and so it states the recommendation and cost.
Answerabstract describes; executive summary advises
Question 43
Write a four-sentence abstract: three concrete mixes were tested in compression at 28 days; mix B gave 31.2 MPa, the highest; mix B costs 4% more.
Solution
1Sentence 1: purpose. Sentence 2: method. Sentence 3: main result. Sentence 4: conclusion.
2‘This study compares the strength of three concrete mixes for a floor slab. Cubes of each mix were cured and crushed at 28 days. Mix B gave the highest mean strength, 31.2 MPa. Mix B is recommended, at a cost 4% above the others.’
Answerpurpose, method, result, conclusion in four sentences
Question 44
Sort into background, problem, aim and scope: (a) ‘Boreholes supply 60% of the town’s water.’ (b) ‘Pump failures leave districts dry for days.’ (c) ‘This report finds the causes.’ (d) ‘Only the five pumps installed since 2019 are covered.’
Solution
1(a) background. (b) problem. (c) aim. (d) scope.
2An introduction moves from the general to the particular in this order.
Answer(a) background; (b) problem; (c) aim; (d) scope
Question 45
The methods section says ‘The beam was tested.’ What is missing?
Solution
1The reader must be able to repeat the work.
2Add: number of specimens, span and section, loading arrangement and rate, the equipment and its calibration, and how the readings were taken.
Answerspecimens, dimensions, loading, equipment, readings
Question 46
Classify as result or discussion: (a) ‘The mean strength was 31.2 MPa.’ (b) ‘This is above the target because the mix had a lower water–cement ratio.’
Solution
1A result states what was found.
2A discussion explains it, compares it, or gives its meaning.
3(a) result; (b) discussion.
Answer(a) result; (b) discussion
Common slips: Do not explain in the results section, and do not report new data in the discussion.
Question 47
Classify as conclusion or recommendation: (a) ‘Mix B is the strongest.’ (b) ‘Use Mix B for the slab.’
Solution
1A conclusion follows from the evidence.
2A recommendation tells someone to act.
3(a) conclusion; (b) recommendation.
Answer(a) conclusion; (b) recommendation
Question 48
What is wrong with these headings: ‘1 Introduction’, ‘1.1 Aim’, ‘2.1 Scope’?
Solution
1The numbering shows ‘Scope’ under section 2, but no heading 2 exists above it.
2It belongs under the introduction as 1.2.
3Every sub-number needs a parent, and there should never be a lone 1.1 without a 1.2.
Answerscope should be 1.2
Question 49
A 2400-word report is planned with 10% introduction, 15% methods, 30% results, 25% discussion, 10% conclusions and 10% other. Give the word budget.
Solution
1Introduction 0.10 × 2400 = 240.
2Methods 0.15 × 2400 = 360.
3Results 0.30 × 2400 = 720.
4Discussion 0.25 × 2400 = 600.
5Conclusions 240 and other 240.
6Check: 240 + 360 + 720 + 600 + 240 + 240 = 2400.
Answer240, 360, 720, 600, 240, 240
Question 50
A three-page derivation supports one result in the report. Where does it go?
Solution
1Put the derivation in an appendix.
2State the result in the main text and refer to it: ‘The derivation is given in Appendix B.’
Answerappendix, with the result and a reference in the text
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 6

Correspondence

Questions 51–60. Memos, emails and letters.

Question 51
List the parts of a memo header and write the subject line for a delay to commissioning, now due on 14 March.
Solution
1A memo header has To, From, Date and Subject.
2The subject line says what the memo is about and what is new, in under ten words.
3‘Subject: Delay to test rig commissioning, new date 14 March’.
AnswerTo, From, Date, Subject; Subject: Delay to test rig commissioning, new date 14 March
Question 52
Rewrite this email so the reader knows what to do: ‘Hi, about the thing we discussed, let me know.’
Solution
1State the purpose in the first sentence, give the detail, ask for one action with a date.
2Subject: ‘Approval needed by Friday: pump order’.
3‘Dear Mr Bello, Please approve the purchase of two pumps (quotation attached, ₦1.2 million) by Friday 14 March so delivery can begin on 17 March. Regards, Ada’.
Answerpurpose first, one action, a date, the attachment named
Question 53
A letter begins ‘Dear Mr Okafor’ and ends ‘Yours faithfully’. Correct it.
Solution
1In British practice a letter to a named person ends ‘Yours sincerely’.
2‘Yours faithfully’ is used with ‘Dear Sir or Madam’.
AnswerYours sincerely
Common slips: Match the closing to the salutation.
Question 54
Put the parts of a formal letter in order: salutation, body, sender’s address, closing, date, recipient’s address, signature, subject line.
Solution
1Sender’s address and date come first, then the recipient’s address.
2Then the salutation and the subject line.
3Then the body, the closing and the signature.
Answersender’s address, date, recipient’s address, salutation, subject line, body, closing, signature
Question 55
Write the opening of a quotation request to a supplier for 200 m of 50 mm HDPE pipe.
Solution
1Say who you are, what you need, in what quantity, and by when.
2‘We are requesting a quotation for 200 m of 50 mm HDPE pipe, PN10, delivered to our Ibadan site. Please send price, delivery time and payment terms by 20 March.’
Answerwho, what, how much, by when
Question 56
Write a complaint about a late delivery in four sentences.
Solution
1Sentence 1: what was ordered and promised.
2Sentence 2: what happened.
3Sentence 3: the effect.
4Sentence 4: the remedy and a date.
5‘Order 1187 for 40 bags of cement was promised for 3 March. It arrived on 12 March. Work on the slab stopped for nine days. Please credit the carriage charge and confirm the next delivery date by 20 March.’
Answerorder, event, effect, remedy with a date
Common slips: Keep to facts and dates; anger weakens a complaint.
Question 57
When should you use ‘Reply all’?
Solution
1Use it only if everyone on the list needs your answer.
2A ‘thank you’ or a question for one person goes to that person alone.
Answeronly when every recipient needs the reply
Question 58
Rename the file ‘Report_final_v2_FINAL.docx’.
Solution
1A file name should show the date or version, the content and the owner or project, with no ambiguity.
2‘2026-03-14_BoreholeReport_v02.docx’ sorts by date and shows the version.
Answer2026-03-14_BoreholeReport_v02.docx
Question 59
Outline a three-paragraph cover letter for an industrial-training placement.
Solution
1Paragraph 1: the position, where you saw it and who you are.
2Paragraph 2: two or three skills or projects that match the post, with evidence.
3Paragraph 3: availability, a thank-you and how to contact you.
Answerposition and identity; matching evidence; availability and contact
Question 60
Write one sentence giving bad news: the project cannot start in May because materials arrive in June.
Solution
1Give the decision, the reason and the alternative together.
2‘The project cannot start in May because the pipes arrive on 2 June; we can start on 3 June.’
AnswerThe project cannot start in May because the pipes arrive on 2 June; we can start on 3 June.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 7

Instructions and specifications

Questions 61–70. Procedures, warnings and requirements.

Question 61
Rewrite as an instruction: ‘The operator should now press the red button.’
Solution
1Instructions use the imperative.
2‘Press the red button.’
AnswerPress the red button.
Question 62
Split into single steps: ‘Open the valve, check the gauge and record the pressure.’
Solution
1One action in each step, numbered.
21. Open the valve.
32. Check the gauge.
43. Record the pressure.
Answerthree numbered steps
Common slips: If two actions share one step, the reader does one and forgets the other.
Question 63
Classify as danger, warning or caution: (a) exposed 415 V terminals that can kill; (b) a hot surface that may burn skin; (c) a switch that may jam if forced.
Solution
1Danger is an immediate hazard likely to cause death or serious injury.
2Warning is a hazard that could cause serious injury.
3Caution is a hazard that could cause minor injury or damage to equipment.
4(a) danger; (b) warning; (c) caution.
Answer(a) danger; (b) warning; (c) caution
Question 64
Where does a warning go in a list of steps?
Solution
1The reader must see the hazard before the action, not after it.
2Place the warning directly before the step it applies to, set apart in a box.
Answerimmediately before the step
Question 65
When should a procedure use a numbered list, and when a bulleted list?
Solution
1Numbers show a sequence in which order matters.
2Bullets show items with no fixed order, such as the tools required.
Answernumbers for sequence; bullets for items
Question 66
Classify the wording: (a) ‘The pump shall deliver 12 L/s.’ (b) ‘The casing should be painted.’ (c) ‘A spare filter may be supplied.’
Solution
1In a specification, shall means a requirement.
2Should means a recommendation.
3May means an option.
4(a) mandatory; (b) recommended; (c) optional.
Answer(a) mandatory; (b) recommended; (c) optional
Question 67
Make this requirement testable: ‘The bracket must be sufficiently strong.’
Solution
1A requirement needs a number and a test.
2‘The bracket shall carry a 5 kN load with a deflection below 3 mm.’
AnswerThe bracket shall carry a 5 kN load with a deflection below 3 mm.
Question 68
How do you test whether instructions for a novice are complete?
Solution
1Ask someone who has not done the task to follow the text exactly while you watch.
2Note each place where they stop, guess or ask. Each one is a missing step or an unclear word.
Answerlet a novice follow it and watch where they hesitate
Question 69
List the headings of a standard operating procedure.
Solution
1Purpose and scope.
2Hazards and protective equipment.
3Equipment and materials.
4Steps.
5Shutdown and emergency actions.
6Records to keep.
Answerpurpose, hazards, equipment, steps, shutdown and emergency, records
Question 70

Solution

CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 8

Sources, citation and plagiarism

Questions 71–80. Reference styles and honest use of sources.

Question 71
Write an IEEE reference for a book with author A. Author, title ‘Fluid Mechanics’, 3rd edition, Lagos: Example Press, 2020.
Solution
1IEEE order: author initial and surname, title in italics, edition, place: publisher, year.
2[1] A. Author, Fluid Mechanics, 3rd ed. Lagos, Nigeria: Example Press, 2020.
Answer[1] A. Author, Fluid Mechanics, 3rd ed. Lagos, Nigeria: Example Press, 2020.
Question 72
Show the author-date form of a citation in the text for Okoro, 2021, and the same source in numeric style.
Solution
1Author-date: ‘(Okoro, 2021)’.
2Numeric: ‘[3]’, where 3 is the number in the reference list.
Answer(Okoro, 2021); [3]
Question 73
Original: ‘The flow rate in a pipe falls sharply when its diameter is halved.’ Write a paraphrase with a citation.
Solution
1Change the structure and the wording; keep the meaning; cite the source.
2‘Reducing a pipe’s diameter to half its size greatly lowers the flow through it (Okoro, 2021).’
AnswerReducing a pipe’s diameter to half its size greatly lowers the flow through it (Okoro, 2021).
Common slips: Swapping a few words and keeping the structure is still copying.
Question 74
Which statements need a citation? (a) Water boils at 100 °C at sea level. (b) Nigeria’s installed grid capacity was 12 GW in 2022.
Solution
1Common knowledge that anyone can check in a basic text needs none: (a).
2A specific figure from a particular source needs one: (b).
Answer(b) needs a citation; (a) does not
Question 75
In what order are references listed in IEEE style and in author-date style?
Solution
1IEEE: in the order cited in the text, numbered.
2Author-date (Harvard): alphabetical by the first author’s surname.
AnswerIEEE: order of citation; Harvard: alphabetical
Question 76
Rank for authority: a manufacturer’s blog, a peer-reviewed journal article, a national standard, a data sheet from the supplier.
Solution
1A national standard is written under a formal consensus process: highest.
2A peer-reviewed article has been checked by independent experts.
3A supplier’s data sheet is reliable for its own product but has a commercial interest.
4A blog is unchecked: lowest.
Answerstandard, journal article, data sheet, blog
Question 77
A student copies 40 words, cites the source, but leaves out the quotation marks. Is this acceptable?
Solution
1A citation tells the reader the idea is from the source; quotation marks tell the reader the words are too.
2Without marks the 40 words pass as the student’s writing.
3Fix: put the words in quotation marks, or paraphrase them and keep the citation.
Answerno; use quotation marks or paraphrase
Question 78
A similarity report shows 18%, of which 12% is the reference list and marked quotations. How much needs checking?
Solution
1The reference list and quoted text are expected to match other documents.
2Remaining match = 18 − 12 = 6%.
3Check that 6% for unmarked copying; do not judge by the headline figure.
Answer6% needs checking
Question 79
Cite the standard BS EN 206, ‘Concrete — Specification, performance, production and conformity’, in IEEE style as reference 4.
Solution
1A standard is cited by its issuing body, number and title.
2[4] Concrete — Specification, performance, production and conformity, BS EN 206, British Standards Institution.
Answer[4] Concrete — Specification, performance, production and conformity, BS EN 206, British Standards Institution.
Question 80
You read a claim by Okoro (2015) in a paper by Bello (2021) and have not seen Okoro’s work. How do you cite it?
Solution
1Cite the paper you actually read and mention the original through it.
2‘Okoro (2015, cited in Bello, 2021)’, and list only Bello in the references.
AnswerOkoro (2015, cited in Bello, 2021); list Bello

Proposals and project planning

Questions 81–90. Objectives, schedules, budgets and risks.

Question 81
Which problem statement is stronger? (a) ‘Pumps break a lot.’ (b) ‘Of 12 pumps installed since 2019, 7 have failed from dry running, and each failure leaves a district without water for 4 days.’
Solution
1A strong statement is specific, measured and states the effect.
2(b) gives counts and consequence; (a) gives neither.
Answer(b)
Common slips: A vague problem leads to a vague solution.
Question 82
Rewrite as a SMART objective: ‘Improve the pump.’ The efficiency is now 62%.
Solution
1Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound.
2‘Raise the pump efficiency from 62% to 70% by 30 September 2026 by replacing the impeller.’
AnswerRaise the pump efficiency from 62% to 70% by 30 September 2026 by replacing the impeller.
Question 83
Task A takes 3 weeks. B (2 weeks) and C (4 weeks) follow A. D (1 week) follows both B and C. Find the project length, the critical path and the float on B.
Solution
1Path A, B, D = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 weeks.
2Path A, C, D = 3 + 4 + 1 = 8 weeks.
3The longest path sets the project length: 8 weeks, critical path A, C, D.
4B can slip by 4 − 2 = 2 weeks without delaying the end.
Answer8 weeks; A–C–D; B has 2 weeks float
Question 84
A job needs materials at ₦450 000, six workers for five days at ₦8 000 a day each, and equipment hire at ₦120 000. Add 10% contingency and find the budget.
Solution
1Labour = 6 × 5 × 8000 = ₦240 000.
2Subtotal = 450 000 + 240 000 + 120 000 = ₦810 000.
3Contingency = 0.10 × 810 000 = ₦81 000.
4Total = ₦891 000.
Answer₦891 000
Common slips: Contingency is a percentage of the subtotal, not of one item.
Question 85
A risk has likelihood 4 and impact 5 on scales of 1 to 5. Score it, and say what a score of 20 or above means if 15 to 25 is classed high.
Solution

220 is in the high band, so the risk needs an owner and an action before work starts.
Answer20: high
Question 86

2Collector, storage tank, pipework and controls, support frame, testing and commissioning.

CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 10

Presentations and meetings

Questions 91–100. Timing, slide design, agendas and minutes.

Question 91
A 15-minute slot includes 3 minutes for questions. At 1.5 minutes a slide, how many slides?
Solution
1Speaking time = 15 − 3 = 12 minutes.
2Slides = 12/1.5 = 8.
Answer8 slides
Common slips: A slide for every minute leaves no time to explain.
Question 92
Fix a slide that holds a paragraph of 80 words and a small table.
Solution
1One idea per slide.
2State the message as a headline: ‘Strength rose 12% with curing time’.
3Show one clear figure or the key three numbers; say the rest aloud.
4Keep it to about 30 words.
Answerheadline message, one figure, about 30 words
Question 93
A speaker reads at 130 words a minute. How long a script fills a 10-minute talk?
Solution
1Words = 130 × 10.
2= 1300 words.
Answer1300 words
Question 94
State minimum text sizes for slides.
Solution
1Body text should be at least 24 pt and headings about 32 pt.
2Smaller text cannot be read from the back of a room.
Answerbody 24 pt or more; headings about 32 pt
Question 95
Outline an opening that gives the audience a map.
Solution
1Say what the talk is about, why it matters to this audience, and how it is organised.
2‘Today I will show why seven pumps failed, what it costs the town, and the three changes I recommend.’
Answertopic, relevance, structure in the first minute
Question 96
You cannot answer a question. What do you say?
Solution
1Do not guess.
2‘I do not have that figure to hand. I will check and send it to you by Friday.’
3Then keep the promise.
Answersay so and promise a date
Question 97
A 60-minute meeting has agenda items of 5, 15, 20 and 10 minutes. How much time is unplanned?
Solution
1Planned = 5 + 15 + 20 + 10 = 50 minutes.
2Unplanned = 60 − 50 = 10 minutes, a sensible buffer.
Answer10 minutes
Question 98
Write the minutes line for a decision: Ada will order two pumps by 14 March.
Solution
1Minutes record decisions and actions, with an owner and a date.
2‘Decision: order two pumps. Action: Ada to place the order by 14 March.’
AnswerDecision: order two pumps. Action: Ada to place the order by 14 March.
Question 99
An A1 poster measures 594 mm by 841 mm. Find its area and give text sizes for reading at 1.5 m.
Solution
1Area = 0.594 × 0.841 = 0.50 m2.
2Title about 72 pt, headings about 36 pt, body at least 24 pt.
3Keep text under about 300 words and let figures carry the message.
Answer0.50 m2; title 72 pt, body 24 pt
Question 100
About 8% of men have red-green colour vision deficiency. In an audience of 60 men, how many are expected to be affected, and what follows for slides?
Solution
1Expected = 0.08 × 60 = 4.8, about 5.
2Do not rely on red against green to carry meaning.
3Use different line styles or labels together with colour.
Answerabout 5; use shape or labels as well as colour
