Engineering · Shared Core, Year 2

Strength of Materials I

Strength of materials asks whether a member will carry its load without breaking or bending too far. Every answer is a load divided by a section property, checked against a limit. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

Load, stress, limit

A load acts on a section. Dividing by the area, or by a section property such as I or J, gives a stress. Stress is compared with the strength of the material, and the deformation it causes is compared with what the part can tolerate. The same three steps apply to a tie, a shaft, a beam, a pressure vessel and a column.

The Ten Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · Stress, strain and material behaviour

Load over area

σ = P/A
Rule: Strain is the change in length over the original length. Proof stress is read from a line offset by 0.002.
Block 2 · Axial loading and thermal effects

Series, parallel, temperature

δ = PL/AE
Rule: Equal strain shares a load by stiffness. A prevented expansion gives a stress.
Block 3 · Design, joints and energy

Weakest mode governs

U = P²L/2AE
Rule: Check shear, bearing and tension. A falling load multiplies the stress many times.
Block 4 · Pressure vessels

Hoop is double length

σ = pd/2t
Rule: Thin formulas give a single value. A thick wall peaks at the bore.
Block 5 · Torsion and springs

Shear grows with radius

τ = Tr/J
Rule: Power gives torque by T = P/ω. Series shafts add twists; parallel shafts share torque.
Block 6 · Bending stress

Stress grows with distance

σ = My/I
Rule: The neutral axis is at the centroid. An unsymmetrical section has two different extremes.
Block 7 · Shear in beams

Q is the area beyond the cut

τ = VQ/Ib
Rule: A rectangle peaks at 1.5 times the average. Shear flow sets fastener spacing.
Block 8 · Deflection and indeterminate beams

Standard cases, superposition

δ = PL³/3EI
Rule: Fixing an end cuts moment and deflection. Superposition needs a linear elastic beam.
Block 9 · Combined stress and failure theories

Mohr's circle

σ = (σx + σy)/2 ± R
Rule: Principal stresses come from the circle. Compare with yield by Tresca or von Mises.
Block 10 · Columns, fatigue and plasticity

Slenderness decides

σ = π²E/λ²
Rule: Use Euler for long columns, Rankine otherwise. Fatigue depends on amplitude and mean.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 1

Stress, strain and material behaviour

Questions 1–10. Axial stress, shear, elastic constants and the stress–strain curve.

Question 1
A 20 mm diameter steel rod carries an axial pull of 50 kN. Find the stress.
Solution
1Area = π × 202/4 = 314.2 mm2.
2σ = P/A = 50 000/314.2 = 159.2 N/mm2.
Answerσ = 159.2 MPa
Common slips: Force in newtons and area in mm2 give MPa directly. Mixing kN with mm2 is a factor of 1000 out.
Question 2
A 500 mm bar stretches 0.25 mm under load. Find the strain and, for E = 200 GPa, the stress.
Solution
1Strain ε = extension/original length = 0.25/500 = 5 × 10−4.
2σ = Eε = 200 000 × 5 × 10−4 = 100 MPa.
Answerε = 5 × 10−4; σ = 100 MPa
Common slips: Strain has no unit. 200 GPa = 200 000 MPa.
Question 3
A 10 mm rivet in single shear carries 19.6 kN. Find the shear stress.
Solution
1Area = π × 102/4 = 78.54 mm2.
2τ = 19 600/78.54 = 249.6 MPa.
Answerτ = 249.6 MPa
Question 4
The same 10 mm pin sits in double shear and carries 31.4 kN. Find the shear stress.
Solution
1Two planes share the load, so the shearing area is 2 × 78.54 = 157.08 mm2.
2τ = 31 400/157.08 = 199.9 MPa.
Answerτ = 199.9 MPa
Common slips: Double shear doubles the area. It does not double the load.
Question 5
An aluminium alloy (E = 70 GPa) has no clear yield point. The 0.2% offset line meets the curve at a strain of 0.0075. Find the proof stress.
Solution
1The offset line starts at ε = 0.002 and runs parallel to the elastic slope.
2At the meeting point the stress is E × (0.0075 − 0.002) = 70 000 × 0.0055.
3= 385 MPa.
Answer0.2% proof stress = 385 MPa
Common slips: Draw the offset line from 0.002 on the strain axis. Drawing it from the origin only reproduces the elastic line.
Question 6
Steel yields at 250 MPa and E = 200 GPa. Find the strain energy per unit volume stored at yield (the modulus of resilience).
Solution
1u = σ2/2E = 2502/(2 × 200 000).
2= 0.156 N·mm/mm3.
3= 0.156 MJ/m3.
Answeru = 0.156 MJ/m3
Question 7
A bar is stretched so that its axial strain is 0.001 and its lateral strain is −0.0003. Find Poisson's ratio.
Solution
1ν = −(lateral strain)/(axial strain).
2= 0.0003/0.001 = 0.3.
Answerν = 0.3
Question 8
A 20 mm bar with ν = 0.3 has an axial strain of 0.001. Find the change in diameter.
Solution
1Lateral strain = −ν × 0.001 = −0.0003.
2Change in diameter = −0.0003 × 20 = −0.006 mm.
AnswerThe diameter falls by 0.006 mm
Question 9
A 12 mm diameter specimen carries 8 kN. Find the stress, and the strain if E = 80 GPa.
Solution
1Area = π × 122/4 = 113.1 mm2.
2σ = 8000/113.1 = 70.74 MPa.
3ε = 70.74/80 000 = 8.84 × 10−4.
Answerσ = 70.7 MPa; ε = 8.84 × 10−4
Question 10
A material has bulk modulus K = 160 GPa and shear modulus G = 80 GPa. Find E and ν, and the volumetric strain under a pressure of 10 MPa.
Solution
1E = 9KG/(3K + G) = 9 × 160 × 80/(480 + 80) = 205.7 GPa.
2ν = (3K − 2G)/(2(3K + G)) = (480 − 160)/(2 × 560) = 0.2857.
3Volumetric strain = −p/K = −10/160 000 = −6.25 × 10−5.
AnswerE = 205.7 GPa; ν = 0.286; volume falls by 6.25 × 10−5 (fractionally)
Common slips: Only two of the four elastic constants are independent for an isotropic material.

Question 13

Question 14

Solution

Question 15
A square concrete column 400 × 400 mm holds eight 25 mm steel bars and carries 1.5 MN. Take Es/Ec = 8. Find the stress in each material.
Solution
1Steel area = 8 × π × 252/4 = 3927 mm2. Concrete area = 160 000 − 3927 = 156 073 mm2.
2Equal strains give σs = 8σc.
3P = σc(Ac + 8As), so σc = 1.5 × 106/(156 073 + 31 416) = 8.0 MPa.
4σs = 8 × 8.0 = 64 MPa.
AnswerConcrete 8.0 MPa; steel 64 MPa
Common slips: Take the concrete area net of the bars, or the bar area is counted twice.
Question 16
A steel rod (300 mm2, E = 200 GPa) and an aluminium tube (900 mm2, E = 70 GPa) share a compressive load of 150 kN with equal shortening. Find the stress in each.
Solution
1Modular ratio n = 200/70 = 2.857, so σs = nσa.
2P = σa(nAs + Aa) = σa(857.1 + 900) = 1757.1σa.
3σa = 150 000/1757.1 = 85.4 MPa.
4σs = 2.857 × 85.4 = 243.9 MPa.
5Check: 73.2 kN in steel + 76.8 kN in aluminium = 150 kN.
AnswerSteel 243.9 MPa; aluminium 85.4 MPa
Question 17
An aluminium bar 2 m long (α = 23 × 10−6 per K) is heated by 60 K and is free to expand. Find the growth.
Solution
1δ = αLΔT = 23 × 10−6 × 2000 × 60.
2= 2.76 mm.
Answerδ = 2.76 mm
Common slips: Free expansion produces no stress.
Question 18
A steel bar (E = 200 GPa, α = 11.7 × 10−6 per K, A = 500 mm2) is held rigidly between walls and heated by 80 K. Find the stress and the thrust.
Solution
1The prevented strain is αΔT = 11.7 × 10−6 × 80 = 9.36 × 10−4.
2σ = Eα ΔT = 200 000 × 9.36 × 10−4 = 187.2 MPa, compressive.
3Thrust = 187.2 × 500 = 93.6 kN.
Answerσ = 187.2 MPa (compression); thrust 93.6 kN
Question 19
A steel bar (400 mm2, E = 200 GPa, α = 12 × 10−6) and a copper bar (600 mm2, E = 100 GPa, α = 17 × 10−6) are joined side by side, with both ends fixed to rigid plates. They are heated by 100 K. Find the stresses.
Solution
1Copper wants to grow more, so it ends in compression and steel in tension, with equal forces F.
2Compatibility: (αc − αs)ΔT = F(1/AsEs + 1/AcEc).
35 × 10−4 = F(1.25 × 10−8 + 1.667 × 10−8) = F × 2.917 × 10−8.
4F = 17 143 N.
5σs = 17 143/400 = 42.9 MPa (tension). σc = 17 143/600 = 28.6 MPa (compression).
AnswerSteel 42.9 MPa tension; copper 28.6 MPa compression
Question 20
A circular bar tapers uniformly from 20 mm to 30 mm over 1000 mm and carries 40 kN. Find the extension (E = 200 GPa).
Solution
1For a tapered round bar δ = 4PL/(πEd1d2).
2= 4 × 40 000 × 1000/(π × 200 000 × 20 × 30).
3= 0.424 mm.
Answerδ = 0.424 mm
Common slips: Using the mean diameter, 25 mm, gives 0.407 mm. It is close but not exact.

Questions 21–30. Factor of safety, rivets, bearing, strain energy and impact.

Question 21

Solution

2Area = 90 000/120 = 750 mm2.
AnswerWorking stress 120 MPa; area 750 mm2
Question 22
A 16 mm pin in double shear carries 60 kN. Find the shear stress.
Solution

2τ = 60 000/(2 × 201.1) = 149.2 MPa.
Answerτ = 149.2 MPa
Question 23
A 16 mm pin passes through a 10 mm thick plate and carries 30 kN. Find the bearing stress.
Solution
1Bearing area is the projected area, d × t = 16 × 10 = 160 mm2.
2σb = 30 000/160 = 187.5 MPa.
Answerσb = 187.5 MPa
Common slips: Bearing area is the flat projection, not the curved surface.
Question 24
A plate 10 mm thick and 80 mm wide (net) carries 120 kN. Its ultimate strength is 375 MPa. Find the stress and the factor of safety.
Solution
1σ = 120 000/(80 × 10) = 150 MPa.
2Factor of safety = 375/150 = 2.5.
Answerσ = 150 MPa; factor of safety 2.5
Question 25
A lap joint has three 16 mm rivets through 10 mm plates, 100 mm wide. Allowable stresses: shear 100 MPa, bearing 200 MPa, plate tension 120 MPa. Find the safe load.
Solution
1Shear: 3 × 100 × 201.1 = 60.3 kN.
2Bearing: 3 × 200 × 16 × 10 = 96.0 kN.
3Plate at the hole: (100 − 16) × 10 × 120 = 100.8 kN.
4The smallest governs.
AnswerSafe load = 60.3 kN (rivet shear governs)
Common slips: Check all three failure modes. The weakest sets the limit.
Question 26
A bar of area 500 mm2 and length 2 m carries 50 kN (E = 200 GPa). Find the strain energy stored.
Solution
1U = P2L/(2AE) = 50 0002 × 2000/(2 × 500 × 200 000).
2= 25 000 N·mm.
3= 25 J.
AnswerU = 25 J
Common slips: Strain energy grows with the square of the load. Doubling the load gives four times the energy.
Question 27
A 100 N weight falls 100 mm onto a collar at the end of a vertical bar 1000 mm long (A = 300 mm2, E = 200 GPa). Find the maximum stress.
Solution
1Static extension δst = 100 × 1000/(300 × 200 000) = 0.001667 mm.
2Magnification = 1 + √(1 + 2h/δst) = 1 + √(1 + 200/0.001667) = 347.4.
3Static stress = 100/300 = 0.3333 MPa.
4σmax = 347.4 × 0.3333 = 115.8 MPa.
Answerσmax = 115.8 MPa
Common slips: A load applied suddenly with no fall gives twice the static stress. A fall makes it far worse.
Question 28
The bar of problem 11 carries 60 kN and extends 1.125 mm, with the load applied gradually. Find the strain energy stored.
Solution

2= 0.5 × 60 000 × 1.125 = 33 750 N·mm.
3= 33.75 J.
AnswerU = 33.75 J
Question 29
Find the force needed to punch a 20 mm hole through 8 mm plate whose shear strength is 300 MPa.

Question 30

Question 31

Question 32

Solution

Question 34
A cylinder of 800 mm bore holds 2 MPa. The allowable stress is 100 MPa and the joint efficiency is 0.8. Find the wall thickness.
Solution

Question 35
A thick cylinder has an inner radius of 50 mm and an outer radius of 100 mm, with 40 MPa internal pressure. Find the greatest hoop stress and the hoop stress at the outside.
Solution
1At the bore σh = p(ro2 + ri2)/(ro2 − ri2) = 40 × 12 500/7500 = 66.7 MPa.

3The radial stress at the bore is −40 MPa (compression).

Question 48

Question 49

Question 50
A close-coiled helical spring has wire d = 6 mm, mean coil diameter D = 40 mm, 10 active coils, G = 80 GPa. Find the stiffness, and for a 200 N load the shear stress with Wahl correction.
