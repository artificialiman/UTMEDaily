Engineering · Foundations

Introduction to Engineering and Ethics

Two habits run through all of engineering: the duties come in a fixed order, and every number carries a unit. Learn the order and the ratios once, and each case becomes a short calculation or a short decision. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

One order, one habit

In every code of ethics, public safety comes first and everything else is placed after it. In every calculation, the unit is converted before anything is multiplied. Most mistakes in this course come from breaking one of these two rules.

CHAPTER 1

The Ten Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · The profession

Who regulates, who belongs

COREN registers; NSE represents
Rule: A degree is the first step. Registration follows training and assessment. The professional society and the regulator are different bodies.
Block 2 · Ethics: safety and interest

Safety first, interests declared

safety > client > employer
Rule: If a duty clashes with safety, safety wins. A hidden interest is declared, and the person steps out of the decision.
Block 3 · Ethics: conduct

Honest data, no gifts, own competence

report what was measured
Rule: A result is reported as measured. A gift from a bidder is refused. A signature means “I have checked this”.
Block 4 · Units

Convert before you compute

km/h ÷ 3.6 = m/s
Rule: The newton is kg·m/s². kilo is 10³; squaring a length factor squares the prefix. kWh is energy.
Block 5 · Numbers

Significant figures and powers of ten

a × 10ⁿ, 1 ≤ a < 10
Rule: A product keeps the fewest significant figures. A dimension check proves an equation wrong but not right.
Block 6 · Measurement

Error, scatter and fit

|m − t| ÷ t × 100
Rule: Zero error is subtracted. Precise but not accurate means a fixed fault. Tolerances give smallest and largest clearance.
Block 7 · Design

Define, compare, choose

FoS = failure ÷ working
Rule: A requirement must be measurable. A factor of safety of 1 is no margin at all.
Block 8 · Risk and reliability

Control the hazard, add a spare

risk = likelihood × severity
Rule: Eliminate first, protective equipment last. Parallel parts fail only when all fail.
Block 9 · Projects and cost

Longest path, full cost, steady loss

(cost − salvage) ÷ life
Rule: Parallel tasks count the longer one. Overheads apply to materials plus labour.
Block 10 · Energy

Efficiency and power

η = out ÷ in
Rule: Efficiency cannot exceed 100%. Power is work divided by time.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 1

The profession and its regulation

Questions 1–12. COREN regulates; the NSE is the society. Training comes before registration.

Question 1
Complete the statement: COREN stands for …
Solution
1COREN is the body set up by law to regulate the practice of engineering in Nigeria.
2It keeps the register of engineers, technologists, technicians and craftspeople, and it can discipline those on it.
AnswerCouncil for the Regulation of Engineering in Nigeria
Question 2
Complete the statement: The Nigerian Society of Engineers (NSE) differs from COREN because the NSE is …
Solution
1The NSE is the learned and professional society: it holds conferences, publishes work and represents members.
2The power to register and to discipline belongs to COREN.
3Product standards are set by the Standards Organisation of Nigeria (SON), and industrial training is run by the Industrial Training Fund (ITF).
Answera professional society of members, not the regulator
Question 3
Complete the statement: A new graduate wants to be legally recognised to practise as an engineer in Nigeria. The graduate must be …
Solution
1A degree is the first step, followed by approved practical training and an assessment, then registration with COREN.
2Membership of the NSE is useful but does not give the legal right to practise.
Answerregistered with COREN after training and assessment
Question 4
Complete the statement: SIWES stands for …
Solution
1The scheme places students in industry during their course so that they see real work.
2It was started in 1973 and is run by the Industrial Training Fund (ITF) with the universities and employers.
AnswerStudents Industrial Work Experience Scheme
Question 5
Complete the statement: The main purpose of SIWES is to …
Solution
1It adds practical skill to the theory in the course.
2It does not replace lectures, and the allowance paid to students is small and not a salary.
Answerlink classroom theory to real work in industry
Question 6
Complete the statement: A student’s SIWES logbook entry is acceptable when it is …
Solution
1The logbook is evidence of what the student did.
2Daily entries are more accurate, and the supervisor’s signature confirms they are true.
3Copying from another student is a form of dishonesty.
Answerwritten daily, factual, and signed by the supervisor
Question 7
Complete the statement: The design of roads, bridges and water supply belongs mainly to …
Solution
1Civil engineering deals with the structures and networks built for the public: roads, bridges, buildings, dams and water systems.
Answercivil engineering
Question 8
Complete the statement: Mechatronics is the combination of …
Solution
1A robot arm is a typical example: it has mechanical parts, sensors and motors from electronics, and a program that controls them.
2The name joins “mechanics” and “electronics”.
Answermechanical, electronic and computer control engineering
Question 9
Complete the statement: A company plans a plant that turns crude oil into petrol, diesel and kerosene by heating and separation. The discipline that leads the design of the process is …
Solution
1Processes in which materials are changed by heat, pressure and reactions are the field of chemical engineering.
2Petroleum engineers work with the oil in the ground; civil, electrical and computer engineers work on the same plant, but do not lead the process design.
Answerchemical engineering, with petroleum engineers
Question 10
Complete the statement: Science mainly asks why things happen. Engineering mainly asks …
Solution
1The engineer uses science to build a solution to a need.
2The solution has to work and it also has to meet limits of cost, time, safety and regulation.
Answerhow to build something that works
Question 11
Complete the statement: A design meets every performance target, but costs twice the budget. In engineering terms the design …
Solution
1Every real design has constraints as well as targets.
2A solution that cannot be afforded cannot be built, so it does not solve the client’s problem.
Answerfails, because cost is a constraint
Question 12
Complete the statement: An engineer is asked to choose between a heavy cheap steel part and a light costly aluminium part for a truck. The most defensible approach is to …
Solution
Answercompare both against the stated requirements and costs
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 2

Ethics: safety, conflict of interest and confidentiality

Questions 13–21. Public safety comes first. A hidden interest must be declared; confidential information is released only to prevent serious harm.

Question 13
Complete the statement: In codes of engineering ethics, the first duty of an engineer is to …
Solution
1All the other duties, to the client, the employer and colleagues, come after the safety of the public.
2The codes are written this way because the public cannot check the work for itself.
Answerpublic safety, health and welfare
Question 14
Complete the statement: A client insists on a cheaper concrete mix that is weaker than the design code requires. The engineer should …
Solution
1The code sets the minimum strength for safety and the engineer cannot lower it on the client’s request.
2If the client goes ahead anyway, the engineer reports it to the proper authority, such as the regulator.
Answerrefuse, explain the risk, and report if the client persists
Question 15
Complete the statement: A site engineer sees that scaffolding is unsafe and the contractor says the work cannot stop. The engineer should …
Solution
1A danger to workers is a danger to the public.
2The engineer’s duty is to prevent harm, not to record it afterwards, and a written record protects both the engineer and the workers.
Answerstop the work until it is made safe
Question 16
Complete the statement: A conflict of interest arises when …
Solution
1The risk is a judgement that is, or looks, unfair.
2Having a second job is not itself a conflict; the conflict is in what the two interests ask of the engineer in the same decision.
Answera personal interest could affect professional judgement
Question 17
Complete the statement: An engineer who evaluates tenders finds that the engineer’s brother owns one of the bidding firms. The engineer should …
Solution
1Even a fair score cannot be trusted if the engineer has a stake in the result.
2The relationship is declared and someone else judges.
3Scoring a relative down is also unfair, in the other direction.
Answerdeclare the relationship and step out of that evaluation
Question 18
Complete the statement: An engineer advises a client to buy equipment and is also paid a commission by the seller, which the client does not know. This is …
Solution
1The client is relying on independent advice.
2A hidden payment from the seller damages that trust whether or not the equipment is good.
3The interest must be disclosed to the client, and the client decides.
Answerunethical, because the interest was hidden
Question 19
Complete the statement: Information that a client gives an engineer about a new product should be …
Solution
1Clients share secrets because they trust the engineer.
2Using or sharing the information without permission breaks that trust and may break the law.
Answerkept private unless the client agrees
Question 20
Complete the statement: The duty of confidentiality is overridden when …
Solution
1Public safety is the first duty.
2If a hidden fault could hurt people, the engineer reports it to the proper authority, even if the client wanted it kept private.
3A payment or a change of job does not release the duty.
Answerkeeping quiet would put the public at serious risk
Question 21
Complete the statement: An engineer leaves a firm and joins a competitor. The engineer may …
Solution
1What the engineer has learnt is the engineer’s to use.
2The old firm’s drawings, prices and client lists are its property, and they stay private even after the engineer has left.
Answeruse general skills, but not the old firm’s confidential designs
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 3

Ethics: honesty, gifts, competence and intellectual property

Questions 22–33. Report results as measured, refuse gifts from bidders, work inside your competence, and credit other people’s work.

Question 22
Complete the statement: An engineer’s test report should …
Solution
1The report is a record that others will rely on to make safe decisions.
2A changed number is false, however small the change.
Answerstate the results as they were measured
Question 23
A strength test gives 29.6 MPa and the specification needs 30 MPa. The engineer rounds the result to 30 MPa so the batch passes. Is this acceptable?
Solution
1Reporting 30 is a false statement.
2The right action is to report 29.6 and let the client decide on a re-test or rejection.
Answerfalsifying a result, and unethical
Common slips: Rounding to the nearest unit would give 30, but the specification is a limit and the measured value falls below it.
Question 24
Of 20 tests, 3 fail the limit. The engineer drops the 3 results as “outliers” without a reason. Is this acceptable practice?
Solution
1A result can only be excluded for a recorded reason, such as a fault in the machine.
2Dropping results only because they fail is selecting data to get a wanted answer.
3The engineer reports all 20 and investigates why 3 failed.
Answerunethical, since failures cannot be dropped
Question 25
Complete the statement: An engineer who approves payments to a contractor receives a gift from that contractor during the tender. The engineer should …
Solution
1A gift from a bidder can look like payment for a favourable decision, whatever its size.
2Many organisations set a strict rule of no gifts during a tender.
Answerrefuse it and report the offer
Question 26
Complete the statement: A small token, such as a calendar with the supplier’s name, is given to all clients at the end of the year. Most codes treat this as …
Solution
1The test is whether the gift could influence a decision.
2A cheap, open, general gift to everyone does not.
3A gift to one person, in secret or of high value, does.
Answeracceptable, since it is open and of little value
Question 27
A contractor offers an engineer 5% of the contract value for approving an inflated valuation. What must the engineer do?
Solution
1Refusing is the first step.
2Staying silent leaves the offer to be made to someone else, so it is also reported.
Answerrefuse and report it to the authorities
Common slips: This is a bribe and the inflated valuation would be fraud on the client and the public.
Question 28
Complete the statement: An engineer should accept only work that is …
Solution
1Engineering mistakes harm people, so an engineer must know the limits of his or her skill and training.
Answerwithin the engineer’s area of competence
Question 29
Complete the statement: A registered structural engineer is asked to design an electrical installation. The proper reply is to …
Solution
1Registration shows a general standard; each design needs a competent person.
2Copying a design that has not been checked does not make the engineer competent.
Answerdecline or involve a competent electrical engineer
Question 30
Complete the statement: A senior engineer is asked to sign drawings prepared by a junior and does so without checking them. This is …
Solution
1A signature or stamp makes the engineer answerable for the work.
2If it is given without review, a person can rely on a safety check that did not take place.
Answerunethical, since a signature means checked
Question 31
Complete the statement: A patent protects …
Solution
1A patent gives the inventor the sole right to make and sell the invention for a fixed period, in exchange for describing it.
2Copyright protects the written or drawn work; a trademark protects names and logos.
Answeran invention, such as a new device
Question 32
Complete the statement: A student copies several paragraphs of a published paper into a report and gives no source. This is …
Solution
1Plagiarism is presenting another person’s work as your own.
2A paper being public does not make it free to copy without credit.
3The fix is to cite the source and put quoted material in quotation marks.
Answerplagiarism, even if the words are changed slightly
Question 33
Complete the statement: An engineer uses a short piece of code written by another person under a licence that requires credit. The engineer must …
Solution
1Open licences allow use, but with conditions.
2Giving credit is the condition here, and ignoring it breaks the licence terms.
Answergive the credit the licence asks for
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 4

SI units, prefixes and conversions

Questions 34–44. Convert before computing: prefixes, speeds, flows and energy units.

Question 34
Complete the statement: The SI base unit of mass is …
Solution
1The kilogram is the only base unit that carries a prefix in its name.
2The gram is a thousandth of it.
Answerthe kilogram
Common slips: The newton is a unit of force, not mass, and the pound is not an SI unit.
Question 35
Which of these is NOT an SI base unit?
Solution
1The newton is derived: it is built from the kilogram, metre and second.
Answernewton
Common slips: The seven base units are metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela.
Question 36
Complete the statement: The newton, written in SI base units, is …
Solution
1Force = mass × acceleration = kg × m/s².
Answerkg·m/s²
Common slips: The kg·m²/s² is the joule (energy). The kg/(m·s²) is the pascal (pressure). The kg·m/s is momentum.
Question 37
A generator is rated at 2.5 MW. Express this in kilowatts.
Solution
11 MW = 1 000 kW, so 2.5 MW = 2 500 kW.
Answer2 500 kW
Common slips: The 2.5 kW leaves the number unchanged. The 250 kW slips a decimal place. The 2 500 000 kW converts in the wrong direction by a further factor of 1 000.
Question 38
A wire has a cross-section of 0.45 mm². Express this in square metres.
Solution
11 mm = 10⁻³ m, so 1 mm² = (10⁻³)² = 10⁻⁶ m².
2Then 0.45 × 10⁻⁶ = 4.5 × 10⁻⁷ m².
Answer4.5 × 10-7 m2
Common slips: The 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ uses 10⁻³ for the area, and the 4.5 × 10⁻⁶ forgets the 0.45 is a tenth of 4.5.
Question 39
Complete the statement: The pascal, the unit of pressure, is …
Solution
1Pressure is force over area, N/m².
2One joule per second is the watt (power).
Answerone newton per square metre
Question 40
Complete the statement: The kilowatt-hour (kWh) is a unit of …
Solution
1Multiplying by hours gives energy.
21 kWh = 3.6 MJ.
3A bill is charged on the energy used, not on the power rating.
Answerenergy
Common slips: The kilowatt is power (energy per second).
Question 41
A 2 kW heater runs for 3 hours. Find the energy used, in megajoules,.
Solution
1Energy = 2 kW × 3 h = 6 kWh.
2Since 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ, this is 6 × 3.6 = 21.6 MJ.
Answer21.6 MJ
Common slips: The 6 MJ treats kWh as MJ. The 7.2 MJ is for 1 hour only (2 × 3.6). The 2.16 MJ slips a decimal place.
Question 42
Complete the statement: To change a speed from km/h to m/s, divide by …
Solution
11 km/h = 1 000 m ÷ 3 600 s = 1/3.6 m/s.
2Dividing by 60 would change km/h to km/min.
Answer3.6
Question 43
Express a speed of 90 km/h in m/s.
Solution
190 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m/s.
Answer25 m/s
Common slips: The 324 comes from multiplying by 3.6, in the wrong direction. The 1.5 divides by 60. The 0.025 divides by 3 600 and also changes the km.
Question 44
A pump delivers 36 m³ of water in one hour. Express the flow in litres per second.
Solution
136 m³ = 36 000 L and 1 hour = 3 600 s, so the flow is 36 000 ÷ 3 600 = 10 L/s.
Answer10 L/s
Common slips: The 600 divides by 60 instead of 3 600. The 0.01 forgets to turn m³ into litres. The 129.6 multiplies by 3.6.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 5

Significant figures, standard form and dimensions

Questions 45–53. Significant figures, standard form and dimension checks.

Question 45
The number 0.004050 has how many significant figures?
Solution
1Zeros at the start only show the position of the point.
2The zero between 4 and 5 counts, and so does the zero after the 5 when it is written on the end of a decimal.
3The significant figures are 4, 0, 5, 0.
Answer4
Question 46
A length is written as 1200 m with no decimal point. Find the trailing zeros.
Solution
1Without a decimal point, we cannot tell whether the writer measured to the nearest metre or the nearest hundred metres.
2Writing 1.200 × 10³ m removes the doubt.
Answernot necessarily significant
Question 47
A rectangle measures 3.2 m by 1.45 m. Find the area, with the correct number of significant figures,.
Solution
13.2 × 1.45 = 4.64.
2In a product, the answer can have no more significant figures than the least precise number, and 3.2 has 2.
3So the answer is 4.6.
Answer4.6 m2
Common slips: The 4.7 rounds the wrong way.
Question 48
Complete the statement: A number in standard form is written as …
Solution
1The first factor has one digit before the decimal point.
2This makes very large and very small numbers easy to compare by looking at the power of ten.
Answera × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ a < 10
Question 49
Write 0.00072 in standard form.
Solution
1The decimal point is moved 4 places to the right, so the power is −4.
Answer7.2 × 10-4
Common slips: The 72 × 10⁻⁵ has the right value but is not in standard form, since 72 is larger than 10.
Question 50
Find (3 × 104) × (2 × 10−2), in standard form.
Solution
1Multiply the numbers (3 × 2 = 6) and add the powers (4 + (−2) = 2), which gives 600.
Answer6 × 102
Common slips: The 6 × 10⁶ multiplies the powers. The 5 × 10² adds the numbers.
Question 51
Complete the statement: An equation is dimensionally consistent when …
Solution
1Only quantities with the same dimensions can be added or set equal.
2A check of dimensions cannot prove an equation is right, but it can prove it is wrong.
Answerevery term has the same dimensions
Question 52
Complete the statement: The dimensions of pressure are …
Solution
1Pressure = force ÷ area = MLT⁻² ÷ L² = ML⁻¹T⁻².
AnswerML⁻¹T⁻²
Common slips: The MLT⁻² is force and the ML²T⁻² is energy. The ML⁻³ is density.
Question 53
In the pendulum formula T = 2π√(L/g), what are the dimensions of L/g?
Solution
1L has dimensions of length and g of LT⁻², so L ÷ g = L ÷ (LT⁻²) = T².
2Its square root has the dimension of time, which matches T on the left.
3This is the check that the formula is consistent.
Answertime squared (T²)
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 6

Measurement: error, accuracy, uncertainty and tolerance

Questions 54–65. Error, accuracy, uncertainty and tolerance.

Question 54
Complete the statement: The percentage error of a measurement is …
Solution
1The error is the difference between the measured and the true value.
2It is compared with the true value, since that is the reference.
Answer|measured − true| ÷ true × 100
Question 55
The true length of a bar is 50.0 mm. A student measures 48.0 mm. Find the percentage error.
Solution
1(50.0 − 48.0) ÷ 50.0 × 100 = 4.0%.
Answer4.0%
Common slips: The 4.2% divides by the measured value, 48.0. The 96% is the measured value as a percentage of the true value (48 ÷ 50 × 100).
Question 56
A length is measured with an error of up to 2% on a true value of 150 mm. Find the largest reading possible.
Solution
12% of 150 = 3 mm.
Answer153 mm
Common slips: The reading can be as high as 150 + 3 = 153 mm, or as low as 147 mm. The 152 mm uses 2 mm, and the 300 mm doubles the length.
Question 57
Complete the statement: Precision describes how close …
Solution
1Accuracy is closeness to the true value.
2Precision is the scatter of repeated readings.
3A measurement can be one without the other.
Answerrepeated readings are to each other
Question 58
Three readings of a length are 10.51, 10.52 and 10.51 mm. The true length is 10.00 mm. Find the readings.
Solution
1The readings agree closely, so they are precise.
2They are all about 0.5 mm above the true length, so they are not accurate.
3A fixed fault, such as a zero error, usually causes this.
Answerprecise but not accurate
Question 59
A micrometer reads +0.03 mm when closed. It then reads 12.48 mm on a part. Find the corrected reading.
Solution
1A positive zero error means every reading is too high by 0.03 mm, so it is subtracted: 12.48 − 0.03 = 12.45 mm.
Answer12.45 mm
Common slips: The 12.51 adds it. The 12.48 does not correct it at all.
Question 60
Complete the statement: When two measured lengths are added, the uncertainty in the sum is found by adding …
Solution
1For a sum or a difference, the uncertainties in the same unit are added.
2For a product or a quotient, the percentage uncertainties are added.
Answerthe absolute uncertainties
Question 61
Complete the statement: When two measured quantities are multiplied, the uncertainty in the product is found by adding …
Solution
1A product is sensitive to the relative size of each error, so the percentages are used.
2A 1% error in each quantity gives about a 2% error in the product.
Answerthe percentage uncertainties
Question 62
A plate is 2.00 ± 0.02 m long and 1.00 ± 0.01 m wide. Find the uncertainty in its area.
Solution
1Each length has 1% uncertainty.
2For the area the percentages add: 1% + 1% = 2%.
3The area is 2.00 m², and 2% of that is 0.04 m².
4The ± 0.03 adds the absolute uncertainties (0.02 + 0.01).
Answer± 0.04 m²
Question 63
Complete the statement: The tolerance on a dimension is …
Solution
1A shaft with a size of 20.00 +0.00/−0.04 mm has a tolerance of 0.04 mm.
2No part is made to exactly the nominal size, so the tolerance sets how far from it a part may be.
Answerthe total variation allowed in it
Question 64
A hole is 20.00 +0.03/−0.00 mm and a shaft is 19.98 +0.00/−0.02 mm. Find the smallest clearance.
Solution
1Smallest clearance = smallest hole − largest shaft = 20.00 − 19.98 = 0.02 mm.
Answer0.02 mm
Common slips: The 0.07 mm is the largest clearance (20.03 − 19.96). The 0.05 mm combines the largest hole with the largest shaft.
Question 65
For the same hole and shaft (hole 20.00 to 20.03 mm, shaft 19.96 to 19.98 mm), find the largest clearance.
Solution
1Largest clearance = largest hole − smallest shaft = 20.03 − 19.96 = 0.07 mm.
Answer0.07 mm
Common slips: The 0.02 mm is the smallest clearance. The 0.05 mm uses the largest shaft by mistake. The 0.03 mm is the tolerance on the hole.

Design process, requirements and factor of safety

Questions 66–74. The design process, measurable requirements and the factor of safety.

Question 66
Complete the statement: The first step of the engineering design process is to …
Solution
1Without a clear statement of the need, the effort goes into solving the wrong problem.
2Concepts, selection, detail design, building and testing follow it.
Answerdefine the problem clearly
Question 67
Complete the statement: After several design concepts have been generated, the next step is to …
Solution
1The concepts are scored against requirements such as cost, safety, size and performance.
2The selected one is developed in detail.
3Building all of them would waste time and money.
Answercompare them with the requirements
Question 68
Complete the statement: A walkway is slippery in rain. The correct first action is to …
Solution
1Buying first is guessing, and a sign alone leaves the hazard in place.
Answerstate a measurable slip-resistance requirement
Question 69
Complete the statement: A good requirement in a specification is …
Solution
1If it cannot be measured, no one can show whether the design meets it.
2“Strong” is a wish; “carries 50 kN” is a requirement.
Answermeasurable and testable
Question 70
Which of these is a measurable requirement?
Solution
1It has a number, a unit and a condition, so a test can say yes or no.
2The others are opinions.
AnswerThe pump shall deliver 5 L/s at a head of 20 m
Question 71
Complete the statement: A client asks for “a bridge that lasts a long time”. The engineer’s best response is to …
Solution
1A vague wish is turned into numbers, such as a design life of 50 years and a traffic load per axle.
2Both sides can then check that the bridge meets it.
Answeragree a design life in years and the loads it must carry
Question 72
Complete the statement: The factor of safety is …
Solution
1It shows how many times larger the load that breaks the part is than the load it carries.
2The margin covers errors in loads, materials and workmanship.
Answerfailure load ÷ working load
Question 73
A part has a factor of safety of exactly 1. What does this mean?
Solution
1At 1 the working load equals the failure load, so a small overload or a weak batch of material causes failure.
2Real designs use values well above 1.
Answerit has no margin, and is at the point of failure
Question 74
A cable fails at 120 kN. A factor of safety of 4 is required. Find the allowable load.
Solution
1Allowable load = failure load ÷ factor of safety = 120 ÷ 4 = 30 kN.
Answer30 kN
Common slips: The 480 kN multiplies instead of dividing. The 116 kN subtracts 4. The 4.8 kN is 120 ÷ 25.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 8

Risk, controls, reliability and safety signs

Questions 75–86. Risk is likelihood × severity; control it from the top of the hierarchy; spares raise reliability.

Question 75
Complete the statement: Risk is usually estimated as …
Solution
1A hazard that is likely and severe has a high risk.
2Multiplying the two scores shows how the risks compare, so the money and effort go to the biggest ones first.
Answerlikelihood × severity
Question 76
Complete the statement: A hazard has a very low likelihood but would cause deaths. The engineer should …
Solution
1A low likelihood does not remove the risk when the outcome is catastrophic.
2Many standards set controls by severity as well as by score.
Answerstill control it, as severity is high
Question 77
A machine hazard scores likelihood 4 and severity 5 (both out of 5). After an interlocked guard is fitted, the likelihood falls to 1. Find the new risk score.
Solution
1Before: 4 × 5 = 20.
2After: 1 × 5 = 5.
3The guard reduces the likelihood but the severity of an accident is unchanged.
Answer5
Common slips: The 9 adds the two scores.
Question 78

Question 96

Solution

Answer80% and 1 kW

A pump rated at 3 kW is run for 2 hours. Find the quantity 6 kWh.
Solution

Question 100
