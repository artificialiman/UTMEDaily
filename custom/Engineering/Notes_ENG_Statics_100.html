Engineering · Shared Core, Year 1

Engineering Statics

Statics asks one question of a body that does not move: what forces must act on it. The answer follows from two sums, forces and moments, which must both come to zero. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

Force, moment, equilibrium

A force is described by its size, direction and line of action. A moment measures its tendency to turn. When a body is still, the forces add to zero and the moments about any point add to zero. The same two conditions give the reactions at supports, the shear and bending along a beam, the forces in truss members, and the limits of friction.

CHAPTER 1

The Ten Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · Forces and vectors

Resolve and add

Fx = F cos θ
Rule: Add by components. In three dimensions use direction cosines and unit vectors along the line.
Block 2 · Moments and couples

Force times perpendicular distance

M = F d
Rule: Take moments about the point that removes most unknowns. A couple has no resultant force.
Block 3 · Equilibrium and reactions

Free-body first

ΣF = 0, ΣM = 0
Rule: Count unknowns against equations. Parallel reactions cannot resist a sideways load.
Block 4 · Shear and bending moment

Diagrams from loads

dM/dx = V
Rule: Moment peaks where shear crosses zero. A couple makes the moment jump.
Block 5 · Trusses

Joints and sections

m + 3 = 2j
Rule: A cut may expose three unknowns at most. Look for zero-force members first.
Block 6 · Frames, cables and buckling

Multi-force members, tension only

H = wL²/8h
Rule: Cable pull is horizontal at the lowest point. Effective length governs buckling.
Block 7 · Friction

Limit and direction

F ≤ μN
Rule: Friction acts against impending motion. Check tipping as well as sliding.
Block 8 · Centroids

Weighted average of positions

x̄ = ΣAx/ΣA
Rule: Holes are negative areas. Pappus needs the centroid distance.
Block 9 · Moments of inertia

Distance squared times area

I = Ic + Ad²
Rule: The parallel-axis theorem begins at the centroid. Depth is cubed.
Block 10 · Virtual work and fluid statics

Energy and pressure

F = ρ g hc A
Rule: Pressure grows with depth. The resultant acts below the centroid.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 1

Forces and vectors

Questions 1–10. Components, resultants, dot and cross products, and particle equilibrium.

Question 1
A force of 500 N acts at 30° above the x-axis. Find its x and y components.
Solution
1Fx = F cos θ = 500 × cos 30° = 433.0 N.
2Fy = F sin θ = 500 × sin 30° = 250 N.
AnswerFx = 433 N; Fy = 250 N
Question 2
Forces of 300 N along x and 400 N along y act at a point. Find the resultant and its direction.
Solution
1R = √(3002 + 4002) = 500 N.
2θ = tan−1(400/300) = 53.13° above the x-axis.
Answer500 N at 53.13° above the x-axis
Question 3
Two forces of 100 N and 150 N have 60° between them. Find the resultant and its angle from the 100 N force.
Solution
1R2 = 1002 + 1502 + 2(100)(150) cos 60° = 10 000 + 22 500 + 15 000 = 47 500.
2R = 217.9 N.
3tan α = 150 sin 60°/(100 + 150 cos 60°) = 129.9/175 = 0.742, so α = 36.6°.
AnswerR = 217.9 N at 36.6° from the 100 N force
Common slips: The cosine term is plus when the angle is measured between the two forces placed tail to tail.
Question 4
Forces of 3 kN and 4 kN act at right angles on a bracket. What single force balances them?
Solution
1Resultant = √(32 + 42) = 5 kN.
2The balancing force has the same size and the opposite direction.
Answer5 kN, opposite to the resultant
Question 5
A force F = (30, −20, 50) N is projected on the line with direction vector d = (2, 1, 2). Find the component of F along the line.
Solution
1Unit vector d̂ = (2, 1, 2)/3, since |d| = √(4 + 1 + 4) = 3.
2F·d̂ = (30 × 2 − 20 × 1 + 50 × 2)/3 = 140/3.
3= 46.7 N.
Answer46.7 N
Question 6
A force F = (0, 0, 10) N acts at the point r = (2, 3, 0) m. Find the moment r × F about the origin.
Solution
1r × F = (ryFz − rzFy, rzFx − rxFz, rxFy − ryFx).
2= (3 × 10 − 0, 0 − 2 × 10, 0) = (30, −20, 0) N·m.
3Magnitude = √(900 + 400) = 36.1 N·m.
Answer(30, −20, 0) N·m; 36.1 N·m
Common slips: The cross product is not commutative: F × r reverses the sign.
Question 7
F = (60, −80, 100) N. Find its magnitude and direction cosines.
Solution
1|F| = √(3600 + 6400 + 10 000) = √20 000 = 141.4 N.
2cos θx = 60/141.4 = 0.424.
3cos θy = −80/141.4 = −0.566.
4cos θz = 100/141.4 = 0.707.
5Check: 0.18 + 0.32 + 0.50 = 1.
Answer141.4 N; (0.424, −0.566, 0.707)
Question 8
A cable runs from A(0, 0, 0) to B(3, 4, 12) m and pulls with 900 N. Write the force as a vector.
Solution
1AB = (3, 4, 12), length = √(9 + 16 + 144) = 13 m.
2Unit vector = (3, 4, 12)/13.
3F = 900 × (3, 4, 12)/13 = (207.7, 276.9, 830.8) N.
Answer(207.7, 276.9, 830.8) N
Question 9
A 1000 N weight hangs from a ring held by two cables at 30° and 60° above the horizontal on opposite sides. Find the tensions.
Solution
1Horizontal: T1 cos 30° = T2 cos 60°, so T1 = 0.5774 T2.
2Vertical: T1 sin 30° + T2 sin 60° = 1000.
3(0.2887 + 0.8660)T2 = 1000, so T2 = 866 N.
4T1 = 500 N.
AnswerT1 = 500 N; T2 = 866 N
Common slips: The steeper cable takes more of the load only when its angle is nearer the vertical.
Question 10
A 200 N block rests on a smooth 30° incline held by a rope parallel to the slope. Find the tension and the normal force.
Solution
1Along the slope: T = W sin 30° = 100 N.
2Normal to the slope: N = W cos 30° = 173.2 N.
AnswerT = 100 N; N = 173.2 N

Moments and couples

Questions 11–20. Turning effect, distributed loads and equivalent systems.

Question 11
A force of 200 N acts at a perpendicular distance of 0.4 m from a point. Find the moment about the point.
Solution
1M = F × d = 200 × 0.4 = 80 N·m.
Answer80 N·m
Question 12
A 500 N force acts on a 0.6 m spanner at 30° to the handle. Find the turning moment about the nut.
Solution
1The perpendicular distance is 0.6 sin 30° = 0.3 m.
2M = 500 × 0.3 = 150 N·m.
Answer150 N·m
Common slips: Only the component of the force perpendicular to the handle turns the nut.
Question 13
A 50 N force with components (30, 40) N acts at the point (2, 0) m. Find its moment about the origin using Varignon’s theorem.
Solution
1Moment = x Fy − y Fx.
2= 2 × 40 − 0 × 30 = 80 N·m, anticlockwise.
Answer80 N·m anticlockwise
Question 14
Two parallel forces of 150 N act in opposite directions 0.8 m apart. Find the couple.
Solution
1Couple = F × distance between lines of action.
2= 150 × 0.8 = 120 N·m.
Answer120 N·m
Common slips: A couple has the same moment about every point. It has no resultant force.
Question 15
A 400 N downward load acts 2 m from support A. Replace it with an equivalent force and couple at A.
Solution
1Force at A: 400 N downward.
2Couple at A = 400 × 2 = 800 N·m, clockwise.
Answer400 N down and 800 N·m clockwise at A
Question 16
Downward forces of 100 N at x = 0, 200 N at x = 2 m and 300 N at x = 5 m act on a bar. Find the resultant and its position.
Solution
1R = 100 + 200 + 300 = 600 N.
2R x̄ = 100 × 0 + 200 × 2 + 300 × 5 = 1900.
3x̄ = 1900/600 = 3.17 m from the first force.
Answer600 N at 3.17 m
Question 17
A force F = (0, 0, 100) N acts at r = (0.3, 0.4, 0) m. Find the moment about the x-axis.
Solution
1Mx = ryFz − rzFy.
2= 0.4 × 100 − 0 = 40 N·m.
Answer40 N·m
Question 18
A triangular load rises from 0 to 6 kN/m over 4 m. Find the resultant and where it acts.
Solution
1Resultant = area = ½ × 4 × 6 = 12 kN.
2It acts through the centroid, one-third of the base from the larger end.
3Distance from the zero end = 2 × 4/3 = 2.67 m.
Answer12 kN at 2.67 m from the zero end
Common slips: The line of action of a triangular load is at one-third of the length from the high end, not at the midpoint.
Question 19
A seesaw has a 30 kg child 2 m from the pivot. Where must a 20 kg child sit on the other side to balance?
Solution
1Take moments about the pivot: 30 g × 2 = 20 g × x.
2x = 60/20 = 3 m.
Answer3 m
Question 20
Forces of 10 N up at x = 0, 30 N down at x = 1 m and 20 N up at x = 3 m act on a bar. Find the resultant system.
Solution
1Sum of forces = 10 − 30 + 20 = 0.
2Moment about the origin = 0 − 30 × 1 + 20 × 3 = 30 N·m anticlockwise.
3The system is a pure couple of 30 N·m.
Answera couple of 30 N·m anticlockwise
Common slips: When the resultant force is zero, the moment is the same about every point.

Equilibrium and reactions

Questions 21–30. Free-body diagrams and support reactions.

Question 21
A 6 m simply supported beam carries 12 kN at 2 m from A. Find the reactions.
Solution
1Moments about A: RB × 6 = 12 × 2, so RB = 4 kN.
2RA = 12 − 4 = 8 kN.
AnswerRA = 8 kN; RB = 4 kN
Question 22
A simply supported beam of span 8 m carries 5 kN/m over the whole span. Find the reactions.
Solution
1Total load = 5 × 8 = 40 kN, at the middle.
2By symmetry each reaction is 20 kN.
Answer20 kN at each end
Question 23
A beam has supports at A (x = 0) and B (x = 6 m) and overhangs to C (x = 8 m), where it carries 10 kN downward. Find the reactions.
Solution
1Moments about A: RB × 6 = 10 × 8, so RB = 13.33 kN upward.
2RA = 10 − 13.33 = −3.33 kN.
3The negative sign means A pulls down, so A must be held down.
AnswerRB = 13.33 kN up; RA = 3.33 kN down
Question 24
A cantilever of 3 m carries 4 kN/m over its length. Find the reaction force and moment at the wall.
Solution
1Load = 4 × 3 = 12 kN, at 1.5 m from the wall.
2Reaction force = 12 kN upward.
3Moment = 12 × 1.5 = 18 kN·m.
Answer12 kN and 18 kN·m
Question 25
A beam rests on three simple supports. Is it statically determinate?
Solution
1There are three vertical reactions and two usable equations (vertical force, moment).
2Three unknowns exceed two equations, so the beam is indeterminate to the first degree.
Answerno; indeterminate to the first degree
Question 26
A plate is held by three parallel vertical links. Why is this arrangement improper?
Solution
1All three reactions are vertical and parallel.
2None can resist a horizontal force, so the plate moves sideways under any horizontal load.
3Three unknowns match three equations but the supports do not constrain the body.
Answerparallel reactions cannot resist a horizontal force
Question 27
A 5 m ladder weighing 200 N rests on a rough floor and a smooth wall. Its top is 4 m up the wall and its foot is 3 m out. Find the reactions.
Solution
1Vertical: Nfloor = 200 N.
2Moments about the foot: Nwall × 4 = 200 × 1.5 (the weight acts at the middle, 1.5 m out horizontally).
3Nwall = 75 N.
4Horizontal: friction at the floor = 75 N, towards the wall.
Answerfloor 200 N; wall 75 N; friction 75 N
Question 28
A 900 N load sits at the centroid of a triangular platform on three equal legs at the corners. Find the force in each leg.
Solution
1The load line passes through the centroid, so each corner carries an equal share.
2900/3 = 300 N each.
Answer300 N per leg
Question 29
A 4 m simply supported beam has a clockwise couple of 100 N·m at its middle. Find the reactions.
Solution
1Vertical forces: RA + RB = 0.
2Moments about A: −100 + RB × 4 = 0, so RB = 25 N upward.
3RA = 25 N downward.
AnswerRB = 25 N up; RA = 25 N down
Common slips: A couple causes reactions only because the supports must provide an equal and opposite couple.
Question 30
A 10 kN load acts at 30° from the vertical at 2 m from the pin A of a 5 m beam supported by a pin at A and a roller at B. Find the reactions.
Solution
1Vertical part = 10 cos 30° = 8.66 kN; horizontal part = 10 sin 30° = 5 kN.
2Moments about A: RB × 5 = 8.66 × 2, so RB = 3.46 kN.
3RAy = 8.66 − 3.46 = 5.20 kN.
4RAx = 5 kN, opposing the horizontal part, because the roller gives no horizontal force.
AnswerRB = 3.46 kN; RA = (5, 5.20) kN

Shear force and bending moment

Questions 31–40. Beams under point loads, uniform loads and couples.

Question 31
A 6 m beam carries 12 kN at 2 m from A (RA = 8 kN, RB = 4 kN). Find the shear along the beam and the maximum bending moment.
Solution
1From A to the load the shear is +8 kN.
2After the load it is 8 − 12 = −4 kN.
3Maximum bending moment is where the shear changes sign, at the load: M = 8 × 2 = 16 kN·m.
AnswerV = +8 then −4 kN; Mmax = 16 kN·m
Question 32
A beam of span 8 m carries 5 kN/m. Find the maximum bending moment and where it occurs.
Solution
1Mmax = wL2/8 = 5 × 64/8 = 40 kN·m.
2Shear is zero at midspan, which is where the moment peaks.
Answer40 kN·m at midspan
Question 33
A 6 m simply supported beam carries 20 kN at midspan. Find the maximum bending moment.
Solution
1Mmax = PL/4.
2= 20 × 6/4 = 30 kN·m.
Answer30 kN·m
Question 34
A 2 m cantilever carries 5 kN at its free end. Find the shear and the bending moment at the support.
Solution
1Shear = 5 kN throughout.
2M = PL = 5 × 2 = 10 kN·m, hogging.
AnswerV = 5 kN; M = 10 kN·m hogging
Question 35
A 3 m cantilever carries 4 kN/m. Find the bending moment at the support.
Solution
1M = wL2/2 = 4 × 9/2 = 18 kN·m.
Answer18 kN·m
Question 36
A beam has supports at x = 0 and x = 8 m and an overhang to x = 10 m. It carries 10 kN/m over the full 10 m. Find the reactions, the point of contraflexure and the maximum sagging moment.
Solution
1Total load = 100 kN at x = 5 m. Moments about A: RB × 8 = 100 × 5, so RB = 62.5 kN and RA = 37.5 kN.
2For x ≤ 8: M = 37.5x − 5x2.
3M = 0 at x = 7.5 m, the contraflexure point.
4M is greatest where V = 37.5 − 10x = 0, at x = 3.75 m: M = 37.5 × 3.75 − 5 × 3.752 = 70.3 kN·m.
5At B, M = 300 − 320 = −20 kN·m, the hogging moment.
AnswerRA = 37.5 kN; RB = 62.5 kN; contraflexure at 7.5 m; Mmax = 70.3 kN·m
Common slips: A point of contraflexure is where the bending moment changes sign.
Question 37
A 4 m beam carries 6 kN/m and RA = 12 kN. Find the shear 1 m from A.
Solution
1V = RA − w x.
2= 12 − 6 × 1 = 6 kN.
Answer6 kN
Question 38
A 6 m beam carries 4 kN/m and a central point load of 8 kN. Find the maximum bending moment by superposition.
Solution
1UDL alone: wL2/8 = 4 × 36/8 = 18 kN·m.
2Point load alone: PL/4 = 8 × 6/4 = 12 kN·m.
3Both peak at midspan, so Mmax = 18 + 12 = 30 kN·m.
Answer30 kN·m
Common slips: Superposition works because both maxima occur at the same section.
Question 39
A 100 N·m clockwise couple acts at the middle of a beam. How does the bending moment diagram change there?
Solution
1A concentrated couple makes the bending moment jump by its own size.
2The jump is 100 N·m, upward for a clockwise couple, while the shear is unchanged.
Answera sudden jump of 100 N·m
Question 40
Use the relation between shear and moment to find M at 2 m for the 6 m beam with 12 kN at 2 m (shear +8 kN to that point).
Solution
1dM/dx = V, so the moment is the area under the shear diagram.
2M(2) = 8 × 2 = 16 kN·m.
Answer16 kN·m
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 5

Trusses

Questions 41–50. Determinacy, joints, sections and zero-force members.

Question 41
A truss has 5 joints and 7 members. Test for determinacy with m + 3 = 2j.
Solution
1m + 3 = 7 + 3 = 10.
22j = 2 × 5 = 10.
3The two are equal, so the truss is statically determinate and just rigid.
Answerdeterminate
Common slips: More members than 2j − 3 is redundant; fewer is a mechanism.
Question 42
A triangular truss has A (0, 0) as a pin, B (4, 0) as a roller and an apex C (2, 3) loaded with 12 kN downward. Find the member forces.
Solution
1Reactions: 6 kN at each support.
2At A: member AC runs at θ = tan−1(3/2) = 56.3°. FAC sin θ = 6, so FAC = 7.21 kN.
3It pushes on the joint, so AC is in compression.
4Horizontal at A: FAB = FAC cos θ = 4.0 kN, pulling: tension.
5By symmetry BC = 7.21 kN compression.
AnswerAC = BC = 7.21 kN compression; AB = 4.0 kN tension
Question 43
At joint D three members meet. DC and DE are in one straight line and DB is at an angle. No load acts at D. Find the force in DB.
Solution
1Resolve perpendicular to the line DC–DE.
2Only DB has a component in that direction.
3Therefore FDB = 0.
Answerzero-force member
Common slips: A zero-force member still keeps the truss stable and carries load if the loading changes.
Question 44
A Warren truss has bottom joints at x = 0, 4, 8 and 12 m and top joints at x = 2, 6 and 10 m, 3 m high, with 12 kN downward at each top joint. Cut the members T1T2 (top chord), B1T2 (diagonal) and B1B2 (bottom chord) between x = 4 and x = 6. Find their forces.
Solution
1Reactions are 18 kN at each end.
2Take moments about the bottom joint at x = 4 m for the left part: 18 × 4 − 12 × 2 = 48, so FT1T2 × 3 = 48 and T1T2 = 16 kN, compression.
3Take moments about T2 (x = 6, top) for the left part: bottom chord FB1B2 × 3 = 18 × 6 − 12 × 4 = 60, so B1B2 = 20 kN, tension.
4Vertical balance on the left part: 18 − 12 = 6 = FB1T2 × 3/3.606, so B1T2 = 7.21 kN, compression.
AnswerT1T2 = 16 kN C; B1B2 = 20 kN T; B1T2 = 7.21 kN C
Question 45
In the last problem, which members are in tension and which in compression, and why does the top chord compress?
Solution
1The bottom chord B1B2 pulls on the joints: tension.
2The top chord T1T2 pushes: compression.
3Under downward load the truss bends like a beam, with the top squeezed and the bottom stretched.
Answerbottom chord tension; top chord compression
Question 46
Classify as simple, compound or complex: (a) a triangle with new joints added in pairs of members; (b) two simple trusses joined by three bars; (c) a truss that cannot be built by either method.
Solution
1A simple truss grows from a triangle by adding two members and one joint at a time.
2A compound truss joins simple trusses.
3A complex truss fits neither.
4(a) simple; (b) compound; (c) complex.
Answer(a) simple; (b) compound; (c) complex
Question 47
A tetrahedron has 4 joints and 6 members, with 6 support reactions. Is the space truss determinate?
Solution
1Condition: m + r = 3j.
26 + 6 = 12 and 3 × 4 = 12.
3The two agree, so it is determinate.
Answerdeterminate
Question 48
In the Warren truss problem the loading is symmetric. What does symmetry tell you about members B0T1 and B3T3?
Solution
1Symmetric members under symmetric loads carry equal forces.
2Here each is 21.63 kN in compression.
3Only half the truss need be analysed.
Answerequal forces of 21.63 kN compression
Question 49
A cut passes through four members of a truss, none of them parallel and none meeting at a point. Can they all be found from that cut alone?
Solution
1The cut gives three equations: two forces and a moment.
2Four unknown forces exceed the three equations.
3Choose a cut through at most three, or find one of the four first from another section.
Answerno; three equations for four unknowns
Question 50
In the triangular truss a horizontal force of 8 kN pushes C to the right. Find the reactions and the member forces.
Solution
1Moments about A: By × 4 = 8 × 3, so By = 6 kN up.
2Ay = 6 kN down and Ax = 8 kN to the left.
3At B the vertical balance gives BC = 7.21 kN compression, and AB = 4.0 kN tension.
4AC = 7.21 kN tension.
AnswerBy = 6 kN; A = (−8, −6) kN; BC 7.21 C; AC 7.21 T; AB 4.0 T

Friction

Questions 61–70. Dry friction, wedges, screws, belts and rolling.

Question 61
A 50 kg block rests on a floor with μs = 0.4. Find the largest friction force available.
Solution
1N = 50 × 9.81 = 490.5 N.
2Fmax = μsN = 0.4 × 490.5 = 196.2 N.
Answer196.2 N
Question 62
A horizontal push of 150 N is applied to that block. Does it move? What is the friction?
Solution
1150 N is less than 196.2 N.
2The block stays still, and friction equals the applied force: 150 N.
Answerit does not move; friction = 150 N
Common slips: Friction equals μN only when sliding is about to start, not at every load.
Question 63
A block on a plane with μs = 0.5 is about to slide when the plane is tilted. Find the angle.
Solution
1At impending motion tan θ = μs.
2θ = tan−1 0.5 = 26.6°.
Answer26.6°
Question 64
Find the angle of friction when μ = 0.3.
Solution
1φ = tan−1 μ.
2= tan−1 0.3 = 16.7°.
Answer16.7°
Question 65
A 100 kg crate is on a 20° slope with μs = 0.3. Does it slide without help? Find the force parallel to the slope to start it moving up.
Solution
1W = 981 N. Down-slope component = 981 sin 20° = 335.5 N.
2Maximum friction = 0.3 × 981 cos 20° = 276.5 N.
3335.5 > 276.5, so it slides down on its own.
4To start it moving up: P = W(sin θ + μ cos θ) = 981 × (0.342 + 0.282) = 612 N.
Answerit slides down; 612 N to push it up
Common slips: Up the slope, friction acts down the slope and adds to the weight component.
Question 66
A crate of 100 kg is 0.8 m wide and 1.6 m high, with μs = 0.5. A horizontal push acts at the top. Does it slide or tip first?
Solution
1Sliding needs P = μW = 0.5 × 981 = 490 N.
2Tipping about the lower edge: P × 1.6 = 981 × 0.4, so P = 245 N.
3245 N < 490 N, so it tips first.
Answerit tips at 245 N, before it slides at 490 N
Question 67
A screw jack lifts 10 kN. Mean diameter 40 mm, pitch 8 mm single start, μ = 0.15. Find the torque to raise the load.
Solution
1Helix angle α = tan−1(8/(π × 40)) = 3.64°.
2Friction angle φ = tan−1 0.15 = 8.53°.
3T = W r tan(α + φ) = 10 000 × 0.02 × tan 12.17°.
4= 200 × 0.2156 = 43.1 N·m.
5Since φ > α the screw is self-locking.
Answer43.1 N·m; self-locking
Question 68
A rope wraps 180° round a drum with μ = 0.3. The slack side is 200 N. Find the tight-side tension at the point of slip.
Solution
1T1/T2 = eμθ, with θ = π radians.
2e0.3π = 2.566.
3T1 = 200 × 2.566 = 513 N.
Answer513 N
Common slips: The angle of wrap must be in radians.
Question 69
A rope is wrapped twice (4π rad) round a bollard, μ = 0.25. A ship pulls with 10 kN. Find the holding force needed.
Solution
1T1/T2 = e0.25 × 4π = eπ = 23.1.
2T2 = 10 000/23.1 = 432 N.
Answer432 N
Question 70
A wheel of radius 0.3 m carries 2000 N and the coefficient of rolling resistance is 2 mm. Find the horizontal force to keep it rolling.
Solution
1P = W a/r.
2= 2000 × 0.002/0.3 = 13.3 N.
Answer13.3 N
Common slips: Rolling resistance is far smaller than sliding friction, which is why wheels are used.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 8

Centroids and centres of gravity

Questions 71–80. Composite areas, integration and Pappus.

Question 71
Where is the centroid of a 200 mm by 100 mm rectangle?
Solution
1A rectangle has two axes of symmetry.
2The centroid lies at their intersection: 100 mm from the long side ends and 50 mm from the long edge.
Answerat the centre: (100, 50) mm from a corner
Question 72
A triangle has base 6 m and height 9 m. How high is its centroid above the base?
Solution
1ȳ = h/3.
2= 9/3 = 3 m.
Answer3 m
Question 73
An L-shape is a 60 × 20 mm bar along the bottom and a 20 × 40 mm bar standing on its left end. Find the centroid from the lower left corner.
Solution
1Bar 1: A = 1200 mm2 at (30, 10). Bar 2: A = 800 mm2 at (10, 40).
2x̄ = (1200 × 30 + 800 × 10)/2000 = 22 mm.
3ȳ = (1200 × 10 + 800 × 40)/2000 = 22 mm.
Answer(22, 22) mm
Question 74
Find the centroid of a semicircle of radius 100 mm from its straight edge.
Solution
1ȳ = 4r/(3π).
2= 400/(3π) = 42.4 mm.
Answer42.4 mm
Question 75
Find the centroid of the area under y = x2 from x = 0 to 2.
Solution
1A = ∫x2dx = 8/3.
2x̄ = ∫x·x2dx/A = 4/(8/3) = 1.5.
3ȳ = ½∫x4dx/A = (½ × 32/5)/(8/3) = 1.2.
Answer(1.5, 1.2)
Question 76
A disc of radius 100 mm has a hole of radius 30 mm centred 50 mm from its centre. Find the centroid of the remaining plate.
Solution
1A = π(1002 − 302) = 28 588 mm2.
2Treat the hole as a negative area: x̄ = (0 − π × 900 × 50)/28 588.
3= −4.94 mm, so the centroid moves 4.94 mm away from the hole.
Answer4.94 mm from the centre, away from the hole
Common slips: A hole is a negative area; the centroid moves away from it.
Question 77
Use Pappus to find the volume generated when a semicircle of radius 0.1 m spins about its diameter.
Solution
1Volume = area × 2π × (distance of centroid from the axis).
2= (π × 0.12/2) × 2π × (4 × 0.1/3π).
3= 4.19 × 10−3 m3, the volume of a sphere of radius 0.1 m.
Answer4.19 × 10−3 m3
Question 78
A straight line of length 0.5 m, its end 0.3 m from the axis, spins about the axis to form a cone. Find the curved surface area by Pappus.
Solution
1The centroid of the line is at half the radius: 0.15 m.
2Area = length × 2π × 0.15 = 0.5 × 0.942 = 0.471 m2.
3It agrees with π r L = π × 0.3 × 0.5.
Answer0.471 m2
Question 79
A cone has height 0.4 m. How far is its centroid from the base?
Solution
1The centroid of a solid cone is h/4 from the base.
20.4/4 = 0.1 m.
Answer0.1 m
Question 80
A steel cube (7850 kg/m3) and an aluminium cube (2700 kg/m3) of the same size are fixed side by side, with centres 0.1 m apart. Find the centre of gravity from the steel cube’s centre.
Solution
1The volumes are equal, so weights are proportional to density.
2x = (7850 × 0 + 2700 × 0.1)/(7850 + 2700) = 0.0256 m.
3The centre of gravity is 25.6 mm from the steel cube’s centre, towards the aluminium.
Answer25.6 mm towards the aluminium
Common slips: The centroid is a geometric point; the centre of gravity depends on density.

Moments of inertia

Questions 81–90. Area and mass second moments.

Question 81
Find I about the centroidal x-axis for a rectangle 100 mm wide and 200 mm deep.
Solution
1I = bh3/12.
2= 100 × 2003/12 = 6.67 × 107 mm4.
Answer6.67 × 107 mm4
Question 82
Find I for the same rectangle about its base.
Solution

2= 6.67 × 107 + 20 000 × 1002 = 2.67 × 108 mm4.
3Check: bh3/3 = 100 × 8 × 106/3 = 2.67 × 108.

Virtual work, stability and fluid statics

Questions 91–100. Energy methods, pressure and hydrostatic force.

Question 91
A lever has a load W at 0.5 m from the pivot and an effort of 40 N at 1.5 m. Use virtual work to find W.
Solution
1Give a small rotation δθ: the work of the load is −W(0.5 δθ), and of the effort 40(1.5 δθ).
2For equilibrium the total virtual work is zero: 40 × 1.5 = W × 0.5.
3W = 120 N.
Answer120 N
Question 92
A screw jack of lead 6 mm is turned by a handle of radius 0.3 m. Ideal case: find the load lifted by a handle force of 50 N.
Solution
1One turn: the handle moves 2π × 0.3 = 1.885 m and the load rises 0.006 m.
2Virtual work: 50 × 1.885 = W × 0.006.
3W = 15.7 kN.
Answer15.7 kN
Common slips: Friction in a real jack reduces this, but it is what allows the jack to hold the load.
Question 93
A pendulum of 5 kg with its centre 0.8 m above a torsion spring of stiffness 50 N·m/rad is inverted. Is the upright position stable?
Solution
1Stable if the spring torque beats the gravity torque: kt > mgL.
2mgL = 5 × 9.81 × 0.8 = 39.2 N·m/rad.
350 > 39.2, so it is stable.
Answerstable
Question 94
A spring of stiffness 2000 N/m is compressed 0.05 m. Find the force and the stored energy.
Solution

Answer100 N; 2.5 J
Question 95

Question 98
The same 2 m wide, 3 m high gate has its top 1 m below the surface. Find the depth of the centre of pressure.
Solution
