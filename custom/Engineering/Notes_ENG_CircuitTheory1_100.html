Engineering · Circuit Analysis

Circuit Theory I

Every network problem is the same move in disguise: replace what you cannot see into with something simpler, without changing what the load sees. Add one rule for stored energy and one for AC, and the whole course follows. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

One move, many disguises

Series and parallel reduction, source transformation, Thévenin and Norton all replace a network by a simpler one. Mesh and nodal analysis solve it by writing one law many times. Capacitors and inductors add a single rule: the capacitor voltage and the inductor current cannot jump. In AC the same laws hold with impedance in place of resistance.

CHAPTER 1

The Eight Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · Laws and reduction

Reduce what you can

R = R₁R₂ / (R₁ + R₂)
Rule: Current in equals current out at a node. A load changes a divider. A balanced bridge has equal arm ratios.
Block 2 · Analysis methods

Write one law many times

ΣV = 0 round a mesh ; ΣI = 0 at a node
Rule: A shared resistor carries the difference of the mesh currents. Turn off independent sources only.
Block 3 · Theorems

Condense to V_th and R_th

P_max = V_th² / 4R_th
Rule: Short voltage sources and open current sources to find R_th. Power is not linear in the source.
Block 4 · Storage elements

Voltage and current cannot jump

i = C dv/dt ; v = L di/dt
Rule: Capacitors in series combine as product over sum. Energy is ½CV² and ½LI².
Block 5 · Transients

Start, finish, time constant

x = x_f + (x_0 − x_f)e^(−t/τ)
Rule: τ = R_th C or L ÷ R. After 5τ the change is complete. An interrupted coil makes a spike.
Block 6 · AC basics

Phasors and impedance

X_L = 2πfL ; X_C = 1 / 2πfC
Rule: RMS is peak ÷ √2. Magnitudes of phasors at an angle cannot be added directly.
Block 7 · AC circuits

Series, parallel, power

|Z| = √(R² + X²) ; P = VI cos φ
Rule: Admittances add in parallel. A lagging current means an inductive load.
Block 8 · Resonance and coupling

Tune, filter, match

f_0 = 1 / 2π√(LC)
Rule: A higher Q gives a narrower bandwidth. The load for maximum AC power is the conjugate. An ideal transformer reflects the load by the square of the turns ratio.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 1

Kirchhoff’s laws, reduction, dividers and bridges

Questions 1–15. Reduce first. Kirchhoff’s laws give the rest: current in equals current out, and voltage rises equal drops.

Question 1
Complete the statement: Kirchhoff’s current law states that, at any node, the algebraic sum of the currents is …
Solution
1Charge cannot build up at a node, so the current going in equals the current going out.
2It is written with a sign for each current, taking those entering as positive and those leaving as negative.
Answerzero
Question 2
Complete the statement: Going round a loop in the direction of the current, a resistor causes …
Solution
1Current flows from high to low potential through a resistor, so travelling with the current we move downhill.
2A battery crossed from − to + gives a rise.
3The sum of rises equals the sum of drops (Kirchhoff’s voltage law).
Answera voltage drop of IR
Question 3
At a node, currents of 5 A and 3 A enter, and 2 A leaves along one branch. Find the current in the fourth branch.
Solution
1Total in = 5 + 3 = 8 A.
2One branch takes out 2 A, so the other must take out 8 − 2 = 6 A.
Answer6 A leaving the node
Common slips: The 10 A adds the 2 A instead of subtracting it.
Question 4
Complete the statement: Two resistors R₁ and R₂ in parallel have an equivalent resistance of …
Solution
1The conductances add: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂, which gives the product over the sum.
2The result is always smaller than the smaller resistor.
3R₁ + R₂ is the series value.
AnswerR₁R₂ ÷ (R₁ + R₂)
Question 5
A 6 Ω and a 3 Ω resistor are connected in parallel. Find the equivalent resistance.
Solution
16 × 3 ÷ (6 + 3) = 2 Ω.
2A parallel combination must be smaller than 3 Ω, which rules out all three.
Answer2 Ω
Common slips: The 9 Ω is the series value. The 4.5 Ω is the average. The 18 Ω is the product alone.
Question 6
A 24 V source feeds a 4 Ω resistor in series with a 12 Ω and a 6 Ω resistor in parallel. Find the source current.
Solution
112 ∥ 6 = 12 × 6 ÷ 18 = 4 Ω.
2Total = 4 + 4 = 8 Ω.
3I = 24 ÷ 8 = 3 A.
Answer3 A
Common slips: The 1.1 A treats all three resistors as in series (22 Ω). The 4 A uses 6 Ω alone. The 6 A uses 4 Ω alone.
Question 7
Complete the statement: A voltage source V in series with a resistance R can be replaced by …
Solution
1Both give the same current into a short circuit (V/R) and the same open-circuit voltage (V).
2The resistance moves from series to parallel.
3A current source in series with a resistor is not equivalent to anything useful.
Answera current source V/R in parallel with R
Question 8
Complete the statement: In a source transformation, the arrow of the current source points …
Solution
1The current source must push current out of the same terminal that the voltage source would drive positive.
2A wrong arrow reverses the sign of the whole answer.
Answertowards the positive terminal
Question 9
A 10 V source in series with 2 Ω is replaced by its equivalent current source. Find the equivalent.
Solution
1I = V ÷ R = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 A, with the same 2 Ω in parallel.
Answer5 A in parallel with 2 Ω
Common slips: The 20 A multiplies instead of dividing. The 0.2 A divides the other way round.
Question 10
Complete the statement: For two resistors R₁ and R₂ in series across a voltage V, the voltage across R₂ is …
Solution
1The voltage divides in proportion to the resistance, and R₂ takes its share of the total.
2The same idea gives the current divider: in parallel, the branch with the smaller resistance takes the larger share of the current.
AnswerV × R₂ ÷ (R₁ + R₂)
Question 11
Complete the statement: A load is connected across the lower resistor of a voltage divider. The output voltage …
Solution
1The load in parallel with R₂ gives a smaller resistance, so R₂ takes a smaller share of the voltage.
2A divider is accurate only if the load is much larger than R₂.
Answerfalls, as the parallel resistance is smaller
Question 12
A 12 V source feeds a divider of two 6 kΩ resistors. A 6 kΩ load is connected across the lower resistor. Find the output voltage.
Solution
1The lower resistor and the load in parallel make 6 ∥ 6 = 3 kΩ.
2Output = 12 × 3 ÷ (6 + 3) = 4 V.
Answer4 V
Common slips: The 6 V is the unloaded value. The 8 V is what the lower resistor would get if the load were in parallel with the upper resistor.
Question 13
Complete the statement: A Wheatstone bridge is balanced when …
Solution
1With arms P, Q, R and S, balance means P ÷ Q = R ÷ S.
2The two mid-points then are at the same potential, so no current passes through the meter.
3The resistors do not all have to be equal.
Answerthe arm ratios are equal and the meter reads zero
Question 14
Complete the statement: A delta of three equal resistors R is changed into a star. Each arm of the star is …
Solution
1Each star resistor is the product of the two adjacent delta resistors over the sum of all three: R × R ÷ 3R = R/3.
2A star of equal arms R becomes a delta of 3R.
AnswerR ÷ 3
Question 15
A bridge balances with P = 100 Ω, Q = 400 Ω and R = 250 Ω. Using P ÷ Q = R ÷ S, find S.
Solution
1S = R × Q ÷ P = 250 × 400 ÷ 100 = 1 000 Ω.
Answer1 000 Ω
Common slips: The 62.5 Ω turns the ratio upside down. The 100 Ω repeats P. The 650 Ω adds R and Q.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 2

Mesh and nodal analysis, dependent sources and superposition

Questions 16–27. Write KVL round each mesh or KCL at each node; superposition turns off independent sources one at a time.

Question 16
Complete the statement: In mesh analysis, the unknowns are …
Solution
1One current is assigned to each mesh, usually clockwise, and KVL is written round each mesh.
2Node analysis does the opposite with node voltages and the current law.
Answerthe loop (mesh) currents, found with Kirchhoff’s voltage law
Question 17
Complete the statement: For a resistor R shared by two meshes with clockwise currents I₁ and I₂, the voltage across it is …
Solution
1The two mesh currents pass through the shared resistor in opposite directions, so the net current is their difference.
2The sign of each term in the mesh equation follows from this.
AnswerR (I₁ − I₂)
Question 18
The mesh currents I₁ and I₂ in the diagram both run clockwise. Find the current in the 4 Ω resistor, downwards.
Solution
1Mesh 1: 12 = 2I₁ + 4(I₁ − I₂), so 6I₁ − 4I₂ = 12.
2Mesh 2: 4(I₂ − I₁) + 2I₂ = −6, so −4I₁ + 6I₂ = −6.
3Solving, I₁ = 2.4 A and I₂ = 0.6 A.
4The current down the 4 Ω is I₁ − I₂ = 1.8 A.
Answer1.8 A
Common slips: The 3.0 A adds the currents. The 0.6 A and 2.4 A are the mesh currents themselves.
Question 19
Complete the statement: In nodal analysis, the equations are written by applying …
Solution
1The reference node is given 0 V.
2At every other node the currents leaving through the resistors are written in terms of the node voltages, and their sum is set equal to the source current.
Answerthe current law at every non-reference node
Question 20
A circuit has 5 nodes. Find the number of independent nodal equations.
Solution
1One node is the reference, with a known voltage of zero.
2So n − 1 = 4 equations are needed.
Answer4
Common slips: Writing an equation for it would give no new information.
Question 21
A 10 A current source feeds a node. The node is joined to ground by a 2 Ω resistor and by a 3 Ω resistor, in parallel. Find the node voltage.
Solution
1KCL at the node: V/2 + V/3 = 10, so 5V/6 = 10 and V = 12 V.
2The parallel resistance is 1.2 Ω, and 10 × 1.2 = 12 V.
Answer12 V
Common slips: The 50 V takes the resistors in series (5 Ω). The 20 V and 30 V use only one resistor each.
Question 22
Complete the statement: A dependent source differs from an independent source because its value …
Solution
1It is the model of an amplifier or transistor: the output is set by a control quantity.
2An independent source keeps its own value whatever the rest of the circuit does.
Answerdepends on a voltage or current elsewhere
Question 23
Complete the statement: When superposition is used on a circuit with a dependent source, the dependent source is …
Solution
1Only independent sources are turned off.
2A dependent source is controlled by the circuit, so it must remain in the circuit and its control value is found as part of the answer.
Answerleft in the circuit in every step
Question 24
A dependent voltage source has the value 0.5 Vx, where Vx is the voltage across a 5 Ω resistor carrying 2 A. Find the source voltage.
Solution
1Vx = 2 × 5 = 10 V, so the source is 0.5 × 10 = 5 V.
Answer5 V
Common slips: The 1 V multiplies the current by 0.5 and not the voltage. The 10 V is Vx itself.
Question 25
Complete the statement: To find the effect of one source alone, the other independent sources are …
Solution
1A voltage source has zero voltage when turned off, which is a short circuit.
2A current source has zero current, which is an open circuit.
3The results from each source are then added with their signs.
Answervoltage sources shorted and current sources opened
Question 26
Complete the statement: Superposition can be used directly to find …
Solution
1Voltage and current are linear in the sources, so they add.
2Power depends on the square of the current, so the powers from each source cannot be added.
Answercurrents and voltages, but not power
Question 27
A 4 Ω resistor carries 2 A from source A alone, and 1 A in the opposite direction from source B alone. Find the power in the resistor with both sources.
Solution
1The net current is 2 − 1 = 1 A, so P = 1² × 4 = 4 W.
Answer4 W
Common slips: The 20 W adds the separate powers (16 W + 4 W), which superposition does not allow. The 36 W adds the currents (3 A).
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 3

Thévenin, Norton, maximum power and linearity

Questions 28–39. Condense any network to a source and a resistance, and match the load to it for maximum power.

Question 28
Complete the statement: A Thévenin equivalent circuit consists of …
Solution
1Any linear network seen from two terminals behaves like one voltage source V_th and one series resistance R_th.
2The load cannot tell the difference.
Answera voltage source in series with a resistance
Question 29
Complete the statement: The Thévenin resistance of a network is found by …
Solution
1Voltage sources are shorted and current sources are opened.
2The resistance seen between the terminals is R_th.
3V_th is the open-circuit voltage.
Answerturning off the sources and looking in at the terminals
Question 30
Complete the statement: The Thévenin voltage and resistance seen at terminals a–b are …
Solution
1With a–b open, the 3 Ω carries the current and takes 24 × 3 ÷ 9 = 8 V.
2With the source shorted, 6 Ω and 3 Ω are in parallel, 6 ∥ 3 = 2 Ω.
Answer8 V and 2 Ω
Common slips: The 24 V and 9 Ω ignore the divider. The 16 V takes the share of the 6 Ω resistor.
Question 31
Complete the statement: A Norton equivalent circuit consists of …
Solution
1It is the source transformation of the Thévenin form.
2The Norton current is the short-circuit current between the terminals.
Answera current source in parallel with a resistance
Question 32
Complete the statement: The Norton resistance R_N of a network is …
Solution
1The same dead network is seen from the terminals in both cases.
2Also I_N = V_th ÷ R_th, so the two forms are linked.
Answerequal to its Thévenin resistance
Question 33
The network in the earlier diagram (24 V, 6 Ω in series, 3 Ω across the terminals) has V_th = 8 V and R_th = 2 Ω. Find the Norton current at a–b.
Solution
1I_N = V_th ÷ R_th = 8 ÷ 2 = 4 A.
2Check by shorting a–b: the 3 Ω is bypassed and the current is 24 ÷ 6 = 4 A.
Answer4 A
Common slips: The 2.7 A is 24 ÷ 9 (all resistors in series). The 12 A is 24 ÷ 2.
Question 34
Complete the statement: A source with an internal resistance R_th delivers the greatest power to a load when the load resistance is …
Solution
1With a zero load there is no voltage, so no power.
2With a very large load there is no current.
3The best match lies between these, at R_L = R_th.
Answerequal to R_th
Question 35
Complete the statement: When maximum power is transferred, the efficiency of the source is …
Solution
1The same current flows through R_th and R_L, which are equal, so half of the power is wasted inside the source.
2For power lines, where efficiency matters, the load is made much larger than the source resistance, not equal to it.
Answer50%
Question 36
A network has V_th = 8 V and R_th = 2 Ω. Find the maximum power it can deliver to a load.
Solution
1P_max = V_th² ÷ (4R_th) = 64 ÷ 8 = 8 W.
Answer8 W
Common slips: The 32 W is V_th² ÷ R_th, which forgets the 4. The 16 W is V_th² ÷ (2R_th). The 4 W is V_th² ÷ (8R_th).
Question 37
Complete the statement: A circuit of resistors and independent sources is linear, which means that …
Solution
1If all sources are multiplied by k, every voltage and current in the circuit is multiplied by k.
2This allows the unit-output method, in which the answer is found from an assumed value.
Answerthe response is in proportion to the source
Question 38
Complete the statement: A source is doubled in a linear resistive circuit. The power in one resistor …
Solution
1Voltage and current both double, and P = VI = I²R, so the power is multiplied by 2² = 4.
2Power is not linear in the source.
Answerbecomes four times larger
Question 39
A linear circuit gives an output of 3 V when the source is 12 V. Find the source needed for an output of 5 V.
Solution
1The ratio is 12 ÷ 3 = 4, so the source must be 4 × 5 = 20 V.
Answer20 V
Common slips: The 7.2 V turns the ratio upside down. The 36 V multiplies 12 by 3.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 4

Capacitors and inductors: behaviour, combination and energy

Questions 40–50. The capacitor voltage and inductor current are continuous. Combine them, and find their energy.

Question 40
Complete the statement: The current in a capacitor is given by …
Solution
1The current depends on the rate of change of voltage, not on the voltage itself.
2A steady voltage means zero current, so a capacitor behaves as an open circuit in DC steady state.
Answeri = C dv/dt
Question 41
Complete the statement: The voltage across a capacitor …
Solution
1This gives the first rule of transient work: the capacitor voltage just after switching is the same as just before.
Answercannot jump, as that needs infinite current
Common slips: A sudden jump in v would need dv/dt to be infinite.
Question 42
The voltage across a 10 μF capacitor rises uniformly from 0 to 20 V in 2 ms. Find the current.
Solution
1i = C ΔV ÷ Δt = 10 × 10⁻⁶ × 20 ÷ (2 × 10⁻³) = 0.1 A.
Answer0.1 A
Common slips: The 0.01 and 1.0 A are slips of a decimal place. The 100 A uses 2 μs for the time.
Question 43
Complete the statement: The voltage across an inductor is given by …
Solution
1The voltage depends on how fast the current changes.
2In DC steady state di/dt is zero, so the inductor behaves as a short circuit.
Answerv = L di/dt
Question 44
Complete the statement: The current through an inductor …
Solution
1This is the second rule of transient work: the inductor current just after switching is the same as just before.
Answercannot change instantly
Common slips: A sudden change in current would need an infinite voltage.
Question 45
The current in a 50 mH coil rises uniformly from 0 to 4 A in 20 ms. Find the voltage across it.
Solution
1v = L ΔI ÷ Δt = 0.05 × 4 ÷ 0.02 = 10 V.
Answer10 V
Common slips: The 4 V is the current alone. The 0.4 V and 100 V are slips of a decimal place.
Question 46
Complete the statement: Two equal capacitors C are connected in series. Their total capacitance is …
Solution
1In series, 1/C_T = 1/C + 1/C = 2/C, so C_T = C/2.
2The total is less than either capacitor, since the series connection acts like a larger distance between the plates.
AnswerC ÷ 2
Question 47
A 4 μF and a 12 μF capacitor are connected in series. Find the total capacitance.
Solution
1C_T = 4 × 12 ÷ (4 + 12) = 3 μF.
Answer3 μF
Common slips: The 16 μF is the parallel value. The 8 μF is the average. The 48 μF is the product.
Question 48
Complete the statement: The energy stored in a capacitor is …
Solution
1For an inductor the matching formula is ½ L I².
Answer½ C V²
Common slips: The energy is the area under the voltage–charge line, ½ Q V, which becomes ½ C V².
Question 49
Complete the statement: The voltage across a capacitor is doubled. The energy stored becomes …
Solution
1The energy is in proportion to V², so doubling V gives 2² = 4 times the energy.
Answerfour times larger
Question 50
A 100 μF capacitor is charged to 50 V. Find the energy stored.
Solution
1E = ½ × 100 × 10⁻⁶ × 50² = ½ × 10⁻⁴ × 2 500 = 0.125 J.
Answer0.125 J

CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 5

Transients: RC and RL circuits

Questions 51–67. Every first-order change runs from the starting value to the final value with one time constant.

Question 51
Complete the statement: A capacitor charged through a resistor from a supply V has a voltage of …
Solution
1The voltage starts at zero and rises towards V, quickly at first and then more slowly.
2The form V e-t/RC is the discharge.
Answerv = V (1 − e-t/RC)
Question 52
Complete the statement: The time constant of an RC circuit is τ = RC. After one time constant of charging, the voltage has reached …
Solution
11 − e⁻¹ = 0.632.
2After 5τ the capacitor is above 99% charged.
Answerabout 63% of its final value
Common slips: The 37% is what remains, e⁻¹.
Question 53
Complete the statement: The graph shows a capacitor charging. Point P is at t = τ. The capacitor voltage at P is …
Solution
1At t = τ, v = V(1 − e⁻¹) = 0.632 V.
Answer63% of V
Common slips: The 86% value is the voltage at t = 2τ, 1 − e⁻² = 0.865. The 37% is the part of V still to be gained.
Question 54
Complete the statement: A capacitor with an initial voltage V₀ discharging through a resistor has a voltage of …
Solution
1The voltage falls from V₀ towards zero.
2The rate of fall is in proportion to the voltage left, which gives an exponential curve.
Answerv = V₀ e-t/RC
Question 55
After a time of 5τ, how much of its initial voltage has a discharging capacitor lost?
Solution
1e⁻⁵ = 0.0067, less than 1%.
2In practice a circuit is considered settled after 5 time constants.
Answerlost over 99% of its voltage
Question 56
A 1 000 μF capacitor charged to 12 V discharges through 10 kΩ. Find the voltage after 10 s.
Solution
1τ = RC = 10 000 × 0.001 = 10 s.
2At t = τ, v = 12 e⁻¹ = 4.41 V.
Answer4.4 V
Common slips: The 7.6 V is 12 (1 − e⁻¹), the charging formula. The 1.6 V is the value at 20 s.
Question 57
Complete the statement: The current in an RL circuit connected to a DC supply V grows as …
Solution
1The inductor opposes the sudden rise of current.
2The time constant is L ÷ R: a bigger inductance makes the rise slower, and a bigger resistance makes it faster.
Answeri = (V/R)(1 − e-t/τ), with τ = L/R
Question 58
Complete the statement: At the instant a DC supply is switched on to an RL circuit, the inductor behaves as …
Solution
1The inductor current cannot jump, so it starts at zero and acts like a break.
2When the current becomes steady, di/dt is zero and the voltage across it is zero, like a wire.
Answeran open circuit; a long time later it behaves as a short circuit
Question 59
A coil of 20 Ω and 0.5 H is switched on to a 40 V DC supply. Find the time constant and the final current.
Solution
1τ = L ÷ R = 0.5 ÷ 20 = 0.025 s = 25 ms.
2Final current = V ÷ R = 40 ÷ 20 = 2 A.
Answer25 ms and 2 A
Common slips: The 40 ms uses R ÷ L upside down. The 0.5 A is wrong for the final current. The 25 s slips the unit.
Question 60
Complete the statement: A diode connected across a relay coil, to protect the transistor that switches it, is called a …
Solution
1When the switch opens, the diode lets the coil current go on flowing round a loop, and it dies away slowly instead of making a spike.
2The diode is connected the opposite way to the supply voltage.
Answerflywheel (freewheel) diode
Question 61
A coil of 2 H carries 0.5 A. A switch opens and the current falls to zero in 1 ms. Find the average voltage induced.
Solution
1v = L ΔI ÷ Δt = 2 × 0.5 ÷ 0.001 = 1 000 V.
2This shows why switching an inductive load without protection is dangerous.
Answer1 000 V
Common slips: The other values are slips of a power of ten.
Question 62
Complete the statement: For a first-order circuit, the response after switching is x(t) = …
Solution
1The quantity moves from its starting value to its final value along an exponential curve.
2All that is needed is the initial value, the final value and the time constant.
Answerx_final + (x_initial − x_final) e-t/τ
Question 63
A capacitor at 2 V is switched to a 10 V source through a resistor. Find the voltage just after switching.
Solution
1The capacitor voltage cannot change at once, so it keeps its value of 2 V.
2It then rises towards 10 V.
Answer2 V
Question 64
A capacitor at 2 V is switched to a 10 V source through 4 kΩ. C = 50 μF. Find the capacitor voltage after 0.2 s.
Solution
1τ = 4 000 × 50 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.2 s, so t = τ. v = 10 + (2 − 10) e⁻¹ = 10 − 8 × 0.368 = 7.06 V.
Answer7.06 V
Common slips: The 6.32 V forgets the initial 2 V. The 4.94 V and 9.26 V mix up the two terms.
Question 65
Complete the statement: For a capacitor in a network, the time constant is τ = R_th C, where R_th is …
Solution
1The capacitor sees the rest of the network as a Thévenin equivalent.
2Its resistance, found with the sources off, is the R in RC.
Answerthe resistance seen by the capacitor, sources off
Question 66
Complete the statement: A capacitor’s C is doubled and the source voltage is doubled. The time constant …
Solution
1τ = RC does not depend on the source voltage.
2The source changes the final value of the voltage, but not the rate at which it is reached.
Answerdoubles, as only C changes
Question 67
A 500 μF capacitor is connected across terminals a–b of the network. Find the time constant.
Solution
1With the source shorted, the capacitor sees 6 Ω ∥ 3 Ω = 2 Ω. τ = 2 × 500 × 10⁻⁶ = 1 ms.
Answer1 ms
Common slips: The 4.5 ms uses 9 Ω (resistors in series). The 3 ms uses the 6 Ω alone. The 1.5 ms uses the 3 Ω alone.

AC basics: sinusoids, phasors and impedance

Questions 68–76. Sinusoids become phasors; resistors, inductors and capacitors become impedances.

Question 68
Complete the statement: A sinusoidal voltage v = Vm sin(ωt + φ) has an angular frequency ω equal to …
Solution
1One cycle is 2π radians and there are f cycles each second, so ω = 2πf in rad/s.
2At 50 Hz, ω = 314 rad/s.
Answer2πf
Question 69
Complete the statement: The RMS value of a sine wave is …
Solution
1The RMS value is the DC value that gives the same heating in a resistor.
2The average of a full sine wave is zero, so it cannot be used.
Answerthe peak ÷ √2, about 0.707 of the peak
Question 70

Questions 77–85. The laws of DC apply with impedance. Power has real and reactive parts.

Question 77
Complete the statement: The impedance of a series RLC circuit is …
Solution
1The inductor and the capacitor are 180° apart in phase, so they cancel each other in part.
2The net reactance is X_L − X_C.
AnswerZ = R + j(X_L − X_C)
Question 78
Complete the statement: The magnitude of the impedance of a series circuit with resistance R and net reactance X is …
Solution
1R and X are at right angles, so they add as the sides of a right-angled triangle, not as plain numbers.
Answer√(R² + X²)
Question 79
A series circuit has R = 30 Ω, X_L = 50 Ω and X_C = 10 Ω, and is connected to 100 V. Find the current.
Solution
1Net reactance = 50 − 10 = 40 Ω. |Z| = √(30² + 40²) = 50 Ω.
2I = 100 ÷ 50 = 2.0 A.
Answer2.0 A
Common slips: The 1.1 A adds R, X_L and X_C (90 Ω). The 3.3 A uses R alone. The 1.5 A adds X_L and X_C (60 Ω).
Question 80
Complete the statement: The admittance Y of a circuit is …
Solution
1Admittance is in siemens.
2For parallel branches the admittances simply add, in the same way that resistances add in series.
Answerthe reciprocal of the impedance, 1 ÷ Z
Question 81
Complete the statement: A resistor and an inductor are in parallel across an AC supply. The total current is …
Solution

Question 85

Resonance, filters, matching and the transformer

Questions 86–100. Resonance, bandwidth, filters, conjugate matching and the ideal transformer.

Question 86
Complete the statement: A series RLC circuit is at resonance when …
Solution
1The reactances cancel, so the circuit looks purely resistive.
2The current is then at its maximum, V ÷ R, and is in phase with the voltage.
AnswerX_L = X_C, so the impedance is a minimum
Question 87
Complete the statement: At series resonance, the voltage across the inductor …
Solution
1The inductor and capacitor voltages are equal in size and opposite in sign, so they cancel in the loop, but each is Q times the supply voltage.
2A high-Q circuit can produce dangerous voltages.
Answercan be much greater than the supply voltage
Question 88
A series circuit has L = 0.1 H and C = 10 μF. Find the resonant frequency.
Solution
1f₀ = 1 ÷ (2π√(LC)) = 1 ÷ (2π√(10⁻⁶)) = 1 ÷ (2π × 10⁻³) = 159 Hz.
Answer159 Hz
Common slips: The 1 000 is ω₀ in rad/s, which is not a frequency in hertz. The 1 592 Hz slips a decimal place. The 6 283 Hz multiplies by 2π instead of dividing.
Question 89
Complete the statement: The Q factor of a series resonant circuit is …
Solution
1Q measures how sharp the resonance is.
2It is the ratio of the reactance of the inductor at resonance to the resistance, and it also equals the resonant frequency divided by the bandwidth.
AnswerQ = ω₀L ÷ R = f₀ ÷ bandwidth
Question 90
Complete the statement: A circuit with a higher Q has …
Solution
1Since BW = f₀ ÷ Q, a larger Q gives a smaller bandwidth.
2A radio tuner needs a high Q to select one station and reject its neighbours.
Answera narrower and sharper resonance
Question 91
A resonant circuit has f₀ = 1 kHz and a bandwidth of 100 Hz. Find its Q.
Solution

Answer10
Common slips: The 0.1 turns the ratio upside down. The 100 is the bandwidth itself.
Question 92
Complete the statement: A load receives the maximum power from an AC source when the load impedance is …
Solution

2The load is Z_L = R_th − jX_th, where the source is R_th + jX_th.
Answerthe complex conjugate of the source

Question 94

Solution

Answer0.707 of the input, a drop of 3 dB

Question 98
Complete the statement: For an ideal transformer, the voltage and current ratios are …
Solution
1The voltage follows the turns ratio, and the current follows its inverse, so that the power in equals the power out.
AnswerV₁ ÷ V₂ = N₁ ÷ N₂ and I₁ ÷ I₂ = N₂ ÷ N₁
Question 99
Complete the statement: A load Z_L on the secondary of an ideal transformer appears at the primary as …
Solution
1The voltage is multiplied by N₁ ÷ N₂ and the current is divided by it, so the ratio of voltage to current is multiplied by (N₁ ÷ N₂)².
2This is used to match a load to a source.
Answer(N₁ ÷ N₂)² Z_L
