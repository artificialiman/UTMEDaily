Engineering · Shared Core, Year 2

Engineering Mathematics III

Three operations carry this whole course: differentiate in more than one variable, integrate over a line, area or volume, and connect the two with a theorem. Learn the operations and the connecting theorems, and every problem is a short chain. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

Differentiate, integrate, connect

A function of several variables is differentiated one variable at a time. Its gradient points uphill; divergence and curl describe a vector field. Integrals add up a quantity along a curve, over a surface or through a solid. Green, Stokes and Gauss turn an integral on the boundary into an integral inside, and the other way round. Laplace and Fourier methods finish the course by turning differential equations and periodic signals into algebra.

CHAPTER 1

The Ten Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · Partial derivatives

One variable at a time

dz/dt = z_x x′ + z_y y′
Rule: Hold the other variables fixed. Add every path in the chain rule. An implicit derivative carries a minus sign.
Block 2 · Gradient

Uphill, and normal to the level curve

D_u f = ∇f · u
Rule: The direction must be a unit vector. The gradient is normal to a level surface, so it gives the tangent plane.
Block 3 · Maxima and minima

Zero gradient, then test

D = f_xx f_yy − f_xy²
Rule: D > 0 with f_xx < 0 is a maximum. D < 0 is a saddle. D = 0 means the test says nothing. With a constraint use ∇f = λ∇g.
Block 4 · Double integrals

Inside out, limits follow the region

∬ f dA = ∫∫ f dy dx
Rule: The inner limits may depend on the outer variable. If one order is hard, reverse it. Area is the integral of 1.
Block 5 · Polar, cylindrical, spherical

Match the shape

dA = r dr dθ ; dV = ρ² sin φ dρ dφ dθ
Rule: Circles and discs want polar. Cones and cylinders want cylindrical. Spheres want spherical. The Jacobian factor is never optional.
Block 6 · Divergence and curl

Spread and spin

curl F = 0 ⇒ F = ∇φ
Rule: Divergence is a scalar and curl is a vector. Zero curl on a simple region means a potential exists.
Block 7 · Line integrals and Green

Work along a path, area inside it

∮ P dx + Q dy = ∬ (Q_x − P_y) dA
Rule: For a conservative field use end points. Otherwise parametrise. Green needs anticlockwise travel.
Block 8 · Surface integrals and the big theorems

Flux out equals divergence inside

∯ F·n dS = ∭ ∇·F dV
Rule: dS = √(1 + f_x² + f_y²) dA for a graph. Stokes links circulation to the flux of the curl. The flux of a curl through a closed surface is zero.
Block 9 · Laplace transforms

Calculus becomes algebra

L{y′} = sY − y(0)
Rule: Transform, solve for Y, split into partial fractions, invert. The initial values come in with the transform.
Block 10 · Fourier series

A periodic signal as sines and cosines

b_n = (1/π) ∫ f sin nx dx
Rule: Even functions have cosines only, odd functions sines only. At a jump the series gives the average.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 1

Partial derivatives and the chain rule

Questions 1–10. Hold the other variables fixed, and add every path.

Question 1
Find ∂f/∂x for f = x2y + 3xy2.
Solution
1Treat y as a constant and differentiate in x.
2The derivative of x2y is 2xy; the derivative of 3xy2 is 3y2.
Answer2xy + 3y2
Common slips: Differentiating y as well gives x2 + 6xy, which is ∂f/∂y.
Question 2
Find ∂f/∂y for f = exy sin x.
Solution
1sin x does not contain y, so it is a constant multiplier.
2The derivative of exy in y is x exy.
Answerx exy sin x
Common slips: Dropping the inner factor x (writing exy sin x) forgets the chain rule on xy.
Question 3
Find ∂f/∂x at (1, 2) for f = ln(x2 + y2).
Solution
1∂f/∂x = 2x ÷ (x2 + y2).
2At (1, 2): 2 ÷ (1 + 4) = 2/5.
Answer25
Common slips: Using 2x ÷ (x + y) or leaving out the 2 gives 2/3 or 1/5.
Question 4
Find ∂²f/∂y∂x for f = x3y2.
Solution
1∂f/∂x = 3x2y2.
2Differentiate that in y: 6x2y.
Answer6x2y
Common slips: 3x2y2 is the first derivative; 6x3y is the derivative in y first, which is ∂f/∂y, not the mixed one.
Question 5
Find uxx + uyy for u = ex cos y.
Solution
1ux = ex cos y, so uxx = ex cos y.
2uy = −ex sin y, so uyy = −ex cos y.
3Sum: ex cos y − ex cos y = 0.
Answer0 (u is harmonic)
Common slips: Forgetting the minus sign from the derivative of cos y gives 2ex cos y.
Question 6
z = x2y with x = t2 and y = t3. Find dz/dt.
Solution
1dz/dt = zx x′ + zy y′ = 2xy · 2t + x2 · 3t2.
2Substitute x = t2, y = t3: 4t6 + 3t6 = 7t6.
3Check: z = t7, so dz/dt = 7t6.
Answer7t6
Common slips: Adding only one of the two paths gives 4t6 or 3t6.
Question 7
z = x2y. Use the total differential to estimate the change in z when (x, y) moves from (3, 2) by dx = 0.1 and dy = −0.2.
Solution
1dz = 2xy dx + x2 dy.
2At (3, 2): 12(0.1) + 9(−0.2) = 1.2 − 1.8 = −0.6.
Answer−0.6
Common slips: Using dy = +0.2 gives +3.0.
Question 8
The curve x2 + y2 + xy = 7 passes through (1, 2). Find dy/dx there.
Solution
1Let F = x2 + y2 + xy − 7. Then dy/dx = −Fx/Fy.
2Fx = 2x + y = 4 and Fy = 2y + x = 5 at (1, 2).
3dy/dx = −4/5.
Answer−45
Common slips: Leaving out the minus sign gives 4/5; swapping Fx and Fy gives −5/4.
Question 9
The radius r of a cylinder is measured with a 1% error and its height h with a 2% error. Find the largest percentage error in V = πr2h.
Solution
1ln V = ln π + 2 ln r + ln h, so dV/V = 2 dr/r + dh/h.
2Largest error: 2(1%) + 2% = 4%.
Answer4%
Common slips: Adding the percentages without the factor 2 gives 3%.
Question 10
f = x3 + 3xy2. Find x fx + y fy at (1, 1).
Solution
1fx = 3x2 + 3y2 = 6 and fy = 6xy = 6 at (1, 1).
2x fx + y fy = 6 + 6 = 12.
3Check with Euler: f is homogeneous of degree 3 and f(1, 1) = 4, so the sum is 3 × 4 = 12.
Answer12
Common slips: Using only fx + fy = 12 happens to match here, but only because x = y = 1.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 2

Gradient, directional derivative and tangent plane

Questions 11–20. The gradient points uphill and is normal to the level curve.

Question 11
Find ∇f at (1, 2, 3) for f = x2y + z.
Solution
1∇f = (fx, fy, fz) = (2xy, x2, 1).
2At (1, 2, 3): (4, 1, 1).
Answer(4, 1, 1)
Common slips: The z-component is 1, not 3: z is differentiated, not substituted.
Question 12
Find the directional derivative of f = x2 + y2 at (1, 2) in the direction of the vector (3, 4).
Solution
1∇f = (2x, 2y) = (2, 4) at (1, 2).
2Make (3, 4) a unit vector: (3, 4)/5.
3D = (2, 4) · (3/5, 4/5) = 6/5 + 16/5 = 22/5.
Answer4.4
Common slips: Skipping the division by 5 gives 22.
Question 13
Find the largest rate of increase of f = xy2 at (2, 1).
Solution
1∇f = (y2, 2xy) = (1, 4) at (2, 1).
2The largest rate is |∇f| = √(1 + 16).
Answer√17 ≈ 4.12
Common slips: The vector (1, 4) is the direction; its length is the rate.
Question 14
Find the unit vector in the direction in which f = x2 + 3y2 decreases fastest at (1, 1).
Solution
1∇f = (2x, 6y) = (2, 6) at (1, 1).
2Fastest decrease is along −∇f = (−2, −6).
3Its length is √40 = 2√10, so the unit vector is (−1, −3)/√10.
Answer(−1, −3)/√10
Common slips: Using +∇f gives the direction of fastest increase.
Question 15
Find the tangent plane to z = x2 + y2 at (1, 2, 5).
Solution
1zx = 2x = 2 and zy = 2y = 4 at (1, 2).
2z − 5 = 2(x − 1) + 4(y − 2).
3Rearranged: 2x + 4y − z = 5.
Answer2x + 4y − z = 5
Common slips: Check with the point: 2 + 8 − 5 = 5.
Question 16
Find the tangent plane to the sphere x2 + y2 + z2 = 14 at (1, 2, 3).
Solution
1Let F = x2 + y2 + z2. Then ∇F = (2x, 2y, 2z) = (2, 4, 6) at the point.
2The plane has equation 2(x − 1) + 4(y − 2) + 6(z − 3) = 0.
3Divide by 2 and simplify: x + 2y + 3z = 14.
Answerx + 2y + 3z = 14
Common slips: The gradient of a level surface is normal to it, not tangent.
Question 17
Find a vector tangent to the level curve of f = x2 − y through (2, 1).
Solution
1∇f = (2x, −1) = (4, −1) at (2, 1).
2The tangent is perpendicular to ∇f, so (a, b) with 4a − b = 0.
3Take a = 1: (1, 4).
Answer(1, 4)
Common slips: (4, −1) itself points across the curve, not along it.
Question 18
Find the unit vectors for which the directional derivative of f = x2 + y2 at (1, 1) is zero.
Solution
1∇f = (2, 2) at (1, 1).
2D = 0 when the direction u is perpendicular to (2, 2), so u = ±(1, −1)/√2.
Answer±(1, −1)/√2
Common slips: Using (1, 1)/√2 gives the largest rate, not zero.
Question 19
A temperature field is T = 100 − x2 − 2y2. At (1, 1), find the rate of change of T when moving towards the point (3, 2).
Solution
1∇T = (−2x, −4y) = (−2, −4) at (1, 1).
2The direction is (3 − 1, 2 − 1) = (2, 1); the unit vector is (2, 1)/√5.
3D = (−4 − 4)/√5 = −8/√5.
Answer−8√5 ≈ −3.58
Common slips: A negative sign means the temperature falls in that direction.
Question 20
Use the linear approximation of f = √(x2 + y2) at (3, 4) to estimate f(3.03, 3.96).
Solution
1f(3, 4) = 5.
2fx = x/f = 3/5 and fy = y/f = 4/5.
3Δf ≈ (3/5)(0.03) + (4/5)(−0.04) = 0.018 − 0.032 = −0.014.
4Estimate: 5 − 0.014 = 4.986.
Answer4.986
Common slips: Using fx = 3 and fy = 4 forgets the division by f.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 3

Maxima, minima and Lagrange multipliers

Questions 21–30. Find where the gradient vanishes, then test; with a constraint, set ∇f = λ∇g.

Question 21
Find the stationary point of f = x2 + y2 − 4x + 6y and its value.
Solution
1fx = 2x − 4 = 0 gives x = 2; fy = 2y + 6 = 0 gives y = −3.
2f(2, −3) = 4 + 9 − 8 − 18 = −13.
3fxx = 2 > 0 and D = 4 > 0, so it is a minimum.
Answer(2, −3), minimum value −13
Common slips: The value is f at the point, not the coordinates.
Question 22
Classify the stationary points of f = x3 − 3x + y2.
Solution
1fx = 3x2 − 3 = 0 gives x = ±1, and fy = 2y = 0 gives y = 0.
2fxx = 6x, fyy = 2, fxy = 0, so D = 12x.
3At (1, 0): D = 12 > 0 and fxx = 6 > 0, a minimum. At (−1, 0): D = −12 < 0, a saddle.
Answer(1, 0) is a minimum; (−1, 0) is a saddle
Common slips: D < 0 means a saddle whatever the sign of fxx.
Question 23
Find and classify the stationary point of f = xy − x2 − y2.
Solution
1fx = y − 2x = 0 and fy = x − 2y = 0 give x = y = 0.
2fxx = −2, fyy = −2, fxy = 1, so D = 4 − 1 = 3 > 0.
3D > 0 and fxx < 0: a maximum, with f(0, 0) = 0.
Answer(0, 0), a maximum
Common slips: Writing D = fxx fyy + fxy2 gives 5, which has no meaning here.
Question 24
Find D = fxx fyy − fxy2 for f = x2 − y2 at (0, 0) and classify the point.
Solution
1fxx = 2, fyy = −2, fxy = 0.
2D = (2)(−2) − 0 = −4 < 0.
AnswerD = −4: a saddle point
Common slips: A saddle is neither a maximum nor a minimum.
Question 25
For f = x4 + y4 find D at (0, 0). What can be concluded?
Solution
1fxx = 12x2, fyy = 12y2, fxy = 0, all zero at the origin, so D = 0.
2The second-derivative test is silent when D = 0.
3But f ≥ 0 everywhere and f(0, 0) = 0, so the origin is a minimum.
AnswerD = 0: test inconclusive; it is a minimum by inspection
Common slips: D = 0 does not mean a saddle.
Question 26
Find the largest value of xy when x + y = 10.
Solution
1Set up L = xy − λ(x + y − 10): y = λ and x = λ, so x = y.
2x + y = 10 gives x = y = 5.
3xy = 25.
Answer25
Common slips: A product with a fixed sum is greatest when the parts are equal.
Question 27
Find the smallest value of x2 + y2 when x + y = 2.
Solution
12x = λ and 2y = λ, so x = y.
2x + y = 2 gives x = y = 1.
3x2 + y2 = 2.
Answer2
Common slips: 2 is the value of the function; the point is (1, 1).
Question 28
Find the distance from the origin to the line 3x + 4y = 25.
Solution
1Minimise x2 + y2: 2x = 3λ and 2y = 4λ, so x = 1.5λ and y = 2λ.
2Substitute: 3(1.5λ) + 4(2λ) = 12.5λ = 25, so λ = 2.
3The closest point is (3, 4); its distance is √(9 + 16) = 5.
Answer5
Common slips: The minimum of x2 + y2 is 25, which is the distance squared.
Question 29
A closed box has surface area 24. Find the largest volume.
Solution
1Maximise V = xyz with 2(xy + yz + zx) = 24.
2Lagrange: yz = 2λ(y + z), xz = 2λ(x + z), xy = 2λ(x + y). Dividing the equations shows x = y = z.
36x2 = 24 gives x = 2, so V = 8.
Answer8 (a cube of side 2)
Common slips: An open box would have 5 faces and a different answer.
Question 30
Find the largest and smallest values of f = x + y on the circle x2 + y2 = 2.
Solution
11 = 2λx and 1 = 2λy, so x = y.
22x2 = 2 gives x = ±1, so the points are (1, 1) and (−1, −1).
3f(1, 1) = 2 and f(−1, −1) = −2.
Answerlargest 2, smallest −2
Common slips: Both points satisfy the constraint, so both must be tested.

Questions 31–40. Integrate the inner variable first; the region fixes the limits.

Question 31
Evaluate ∫01 ∫02 xy dy dx.
Solution
1Inner integral in y: ∫02 xy dy = x · 2 = 2x.
2Outer integral: ∫01 2x dx = 1.
Answer1
Common slips: The inner variable is y: x stays outside.
Question 32
Evaluate ∫02 ∫03 (x2 + y) dy dx.
Solution
1Inner: ∫03 (x2 + y) dy = 3x2 + 9/2.
2Outer: ∫02 (3x2 + 9/2) dx = 8 + 9 = 17.
Answer17
Common slips: Integrating x2 as a constant over y gives 3x2, not x2y2/2.
Question 33
Evaluate ∫01 ∫0x y dy dx.
Solution

2Outer: ∫01 x2/2 dx = 1/6.
Answer16
Common slips: The upper limit x of the inner integral turns y into x.
Question 34
Evaluate ∫01 ∫x1 e^{y2} dy dx by reversing the order.
Solution

2Inner: ∫0y e^{y2} dx = y e^{y2}.
3Outer: ∫01 y e^{y2} dy = (e − 1)/2.
Answere − 12 ≈ 0.859

Question 35
Find the area between y = x2 and y = x.
Solution
1They meet where x2 = x, at x = 0 and x = 1, with the line above the parabola.
2Area = ∫01 (x − x2) dx = 1/2 − 1/3 = 1/6.
Answer16

Question 36

Question 37

2The integral separates: (∫02 x dx)(∫02 y dy) = 2 × 2 = 4.

Question 38

2Area = 6.
3Average = 18 ÷ 6 = 3.

Question 39
Reverse the order of ∫01 ∫02x dy dx and evaluate it as the area.
Solution

Question 40
Find x̄ for the triangle with corners (0, 0), (2, 0), (0, 2), uniform density.

2∬ x dA = ∫02 x(2 − x) dx = 4 − 8/3 = 4/3.
3x̄ = (4/3) ÷ 2 = 2/3.
Answer23

Questions 41–50. Choose the coordinates to fit the shape, and keep the Jacobian factor.

Question 41
Find the area of the disc of radius 3 using polar coordinates.
Solution

2Inner: 9/2. Outer: 2π × 9/2 = 9π.
Answer9π

Evaluate ∬ e^{−(x2 + y2)} dA over the unit disc.
Solution

2∫01 r e^{−r2} dr = (1 − e−1)/2.
3Multiply by ∫02π dθ = 2π: π(1 − e−1) ≈ 1.986.

Question 43
Evaluate ∬ (x2 + y2) dA over the unit disc.

2∫01 r3 dr = 1/4, and the angle gives 2π.

Question 44
Find the area enclosed by the cardioid r = 1 + cos θ.

2The cos θ term integrates to 0 and cos2 θ gives π, so ½(2π + π).
Answer3π2
Common slips: The area in polar form is ½ ∫ r2 dθ, not ∫ r dθ.
Question 45
Evaluate ∭ xyz dV over the box 0 ≤ x ≤ 1, 0 ≤ y ≤ 2, 0 ≤ z ≤ 3.

2∫ x dx = 1/2, ∫ y dy = 2, ∫ z dz = 9/2.
3Product: (1/2)(2)(9/2) = 9/2.
Answer92
Common slips: Each integral uses its own limits, not the same ones.
Question 46
Find the volume of the tetrahedron bounded by x + y + z = 1 and the coordinate planes.
Solution

2Innermost: 1 − x − y. Next: ∫01−x (1 − x − y) dy = (1 − x)2/2.
3Outer: ∫01 (1 − x)2/2 dx = 1/6.

Question 47

2∫02 r3 dr = 4, and the angle gives 2π.

Question 48

Question 49

Question 50

CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 6

Divergence and curl

Questions 51–60. Divergence is a scalar, curl is a vector; zero curl means a potential.

Question 51
Find div F at (1, 2, 3) for F = (x2, y2, z2).
Solution
1div F = ∂P/∂x + ∂Q/∂y + ∂R/∂z = 2x + 2y + 2z.
2At (1, 2, 3): 2 + 4 + 6 = 12.
Answer12
Common slips: Each component is differentiated by its own variable only.
Question 52
Find div F at (1, 1, 1) for F = (xy, yz, zx).
Solution
1∂(xy)/∂x = y, ∂(yz)/∂y = z, ∂(zx)/∂z = x.
2div F = x + y + z = 3 at (1, 1, 1).
Answer3
Common slips: Differentiating every component in x gives the wrong terms (y, 0, z).
Question 53

Questions 71–80. Flux through a closed surface equals the divergence inside; circulation equals the flux of the curl.

Question 71

3Check with the divergence theorem: div F = 3 and V = 4π/3, so 3V = 4π.
Answer4π
