Engineering · Shared Core, Year 2

Electrical Machines Basics

Every machine in this course converts energy through a magnetic field. A flux, a winding and a motion appear in each, and the same few relations fix the voltage, the force and the losses. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

Flux, winding, motion

A current in a winding sets up flux in an iron path. A changing flux induces voltage in a winding, and a current in a field gives a force on a conductor. The transformer keeps the iron still. In the DC, induction and synchronous machines the windings or the field move, and electrical power becomes mechanical power or the reverse.

CHAPTER 1

The Ten Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · Magnetic circuits

Ohm’s law for flux

Φ = mmf/S
Rule: Reluctances in series add. A short air gap carries most of the reluctance. Loss in iron depends on frequency and thickness.
Block 2 · Induction and force

Voltage from motion, force from current

e = Blv ; F = BIl
Rule: The angle is measured from the field. Aiding series coils add 2M, opposing coils subtract it.
Block 3 · Ideal transformer

Ratio of volts, inverse ratio of amps

Z′ = a²Z
Rule: Impedance changes by the square of the ratio. Flux is inversely proportional to frequency at fixed voltage.
Block 4 · Practical transformer

Tests give the circuit

η = out/(out + losses)
Rule: Open circuit gives core loss, short circuit gives copper loss. Maximum efficiency is where the two are equal.
Block 5 · DC generators

Emf less armature drop

V = E − IaRa
Rule: In shunt and compound machines the armature carries the load current plus the field current. Wave winding doubles the voltage.
Block 6 · DC motors

Back emf sets the current

N ∝ Eb/Φ
Rule: A starter limits the first surge. A weaker field raises the speed. A series motor needs a load.
Block 7 · Induction motors

Slip splits the air-gap power

Pcu2 = sPag
Rule: Torque is air-gap power over synchronous speed. Torque varies as the square of the voltage.
Block 8 · Synchronous machines

Speed locked to frequency

Ns = 120f/P
Rule: Work per phase. Power follows sin δ and fails past 90°. An over-excited motor supplies lagging vars.
Block 9 · Three-phase systems

Line and phase

P = √3 VLILcos φ
Rule: Star gives √3 in voltage, delta gives √3 in current. Two wattmeters give the power and the power factor.
Block 10 · Losses, starting, protection

Where the energy goes

tap k gives k²
Rule: Starting current is six times full load. Star-delta cuts it to a third, and so cuts torque to a third. Insulation life halves per 10 K.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 1

Magnetic circuits

Questions 1–10. Flux, reluctance, inductance and core loss.

Question 1
A coil of 500 turns carries 2 A on a closed iron ring of mean length 0.4 m. Find the magnetomotive force and the magnetising force.
Solution
1mmf = NI = 500 × 2 = 1000 A-turns.
2H = NI/l = 1000/0.4 = 2500 A/m.
Answermmf 1000 A-turns; H = 2500 A/m
Question 2
Iron of relative permeability 2000 carries a magnetising force of 400 A/m. Find the flux density.
Solution
1B = μ0μrH.
2= 4π × 10−7 × 2000 × 400 = 1.005 T.
Answer1.005 T
Common slips: Permeability of free space is 4π × 10−7 H/m. Leaving it out gives a flux density 800 000 times too large.
Question 3
The flux density is 1.2 T over a core section of 20 cm2. Find the flux.
Solution
1Convert the area: 20 cm2 = 20 × 10−4 m2.
2Φ = BA = 1.2 × 20 × 10−4 = 2.4 mWb.
Answer2.4 mWb
Common slips: 1 cm2 is 10−4 m2, not 10−2.
Question 4
Find the reluctance of an iron path 0.5 m long, section 10 cm2, relative permeability 1500.
Solution
1S = l/(μ0μrA).
2A = 10−3 m2, so S = 0.5/(4π × 10−7 × 1500 × 10−3).
3= 2.65 × 105 A-turns/Wb.
Answer2.65 × 105 A-turns/Wb
Question 5
An mmf of 1000 A-turns acts on a magnetic circuit of reluctance 2 × 105 A-turns/Wb. Find the flux.
Solution
1Φ = mmf/S, the magnetic form of Ohm’s law.
21000/(2 × 105) = 5 mWb.
Answer5 mWb
Question 6
A core of mean length 0.5 m, section 10 cm2 and μr = 2000 has an air gap of 1 mm. Find the current in a 1000-turn coil that produces a flux of 1 mWb. Neglect fringing.
Solution
1Iron reluctance = 0.5/(4π × 10−7 × 2000 × 10−3) = 1.99 × 105.
2Gap reluctance = 10−3/(4π × 10−7 × 10−3) = 7.96 × 105.
3Total S = 9.95 × 105 A-turns/Wb; the two are in series so they add.
4mmf = ΦS = 10−3 × 9.95 × 105 = 994.7 A-turns.
5I = 994.7/1000 = 0.995 A.
Answer0.995 A
Common slips: The gap is only 0.2% of the length yet carries 80% of the reluctance. Air has μr = 1.
Question 7
A coil of 500 turns is wound on a core of reluctance 5 × 105 A-turns/Wb. Find the inductance.
Solution
1L = N2/S.
2= 5002/(5 × 105) = 0.5 H.
Answer0.5 H
Question 8
An inductance of 0.5 H carries 4 A. Find the stored energy.
Solution
1W = ½LI2.
2= 0.5 × 0.5 × 16 = 4 J.
Answer4 J
Question 9
A core loses 40 W to hysteresis and 30 W to eddy currents at 50 Hz and a given flux density. Find the total at 60 Hz with the flux density unchanged.
Solution
1Hysteresis loss varies with f: 40 × 60/50 = 48 W.
2Eddy-current loss varies with f2: 30 × (60/50)2 = 43.2 W.
3Total = 48 + 43.2 = 91.2 W.
Answer91.2 W
Common slips: Do not scale both losses by the same factor. Lamination thickness controls the eddy-current part.
Question 10
An air gap of 2 mm must carry a flux density of 1 T. Neglect the iron. Find the mmf, and the current for a 200-turn coil.
Solution
1H = B/μ0 = 1/(4π × 10−7) = 7.96 × 105 A/m.
2mmf = Hg = 7.96 × 105 × 2 × 10−3 = 1591.5 A-turns.
3I = 1591.5/200 = 7.96 A.
Answermmf 1591.5 A-turns; I = 7.96 A

Question 18
Two coils have L1 = 0.4 H, L2 = 0.9 H and M = 0.3 H. Find the coupling coefficient.

The practical transformer

Questions 31–40. Tests, regulation, efficiency and paralleling.

Question 31
R1 = 2 Ω, R2 = 0.02 Ω and a = 10. Find the total resistance referred to the primary.
Solution
1R01 = R1 + a2R2.
2= 2 + 100 × 0.02 = 4 Ω.
Answer4 Ω
Question 32
An open-circuit test on a transformer reads 230 V, 0.5 A, 60 W. Find the core loss, the working and magnetising currents.
Solution
1Core loss = wattmeter reading = 60 W.
2cos φ0 = 60/(230 × 0.5) = 0.522.
3Iw = 0.5 × 0.522 = 0.261 A.
4Im = 0.5 × sin φ0 = 0.5 × 0.853 = 0.427 A.
Answercore loss 60 W; Iw = 0.261 A; Im = 0.427 A
Common slips: The open-circuit test is done at rated voltage on the low-voltage side. Copper loss in it is negligible.
Question 33
A short-circuit test at rated current reads 20 V, 10 A, 100 W. Find the equivalent resistance, impedance and reactance referred to the side tested.
Solution
1Z01 = 20/10 = 2 Ω.
2R01 = P/I2 = 100/100 = 1 Ω.
3X01 = √(22 − 12) = 1.732 Ω.
AnswerZ = 2 Ω; R = 1 Ω; X = 1.732 Ω
Common slips: The short-circuit test uses a low voltage; core loss is then negligible and the wattmeter shows copper loss.
Question 34
Using R01 = 1 Ω and X01 = 1.732 Ω, find the approximate regulation of a 230 V transformer at 10 A and power factor 0.8 lagging.
Solution
1Voltage drop ≈ I(R cos φ + X sin φ).
2= 10 (1 × 0.8 + 1.732 × 0.6) = 18.4 V.
3Regulation = 18.4/230 = 8.0%.
Answer≈ 8.0%
Common slips: At leading power factor the X term is subtracted, and the regulation can be negative.
Question 35
A 50 kVA transformer has core loss 400 W and full-load copper loss 800 W. Find the full-load efficiency at power factor 0.8.
Solution

2Losses = 0.4 + 0.8 = 1.2 kW.
3η = 40/(40 + 1.2) = 97.09%.
Answer97.09%

For the same transformer find the efficiency at 0.75 of full load, power factor 0.8.
Solution

2Copper loss varies as the load squared: 800 × 0.752 = 450 W.
3η = 30/(30 + 0.4 + 0.45) = 97.24%.
Answer97.24%

Question 39
A 100 kVA distribution transformer has core loss 1 kW and full-load copper loss 1.5 kW. Its daily load is full load at unity power factor for 6 h, half load at unity for 10 h, and no load for 8 h. Find the all-day efficiency.

Question 40
A 500 kVA transformer with 4% impedance and a 250 kVA transformer with 5% impedance are in parallel on a 600 kVA load. Find each share.
