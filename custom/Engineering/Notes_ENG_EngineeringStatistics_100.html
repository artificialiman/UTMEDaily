Engineering · Shared Core, Year 2

Engineering Statistics

Statistics turns scattered measurements into a decision. Summarise the data, describe its randomness with a distribution, then ask how far a sample can be trusted. Learn the summary, the distribution and the test statistic, and each problem becomes a short calculation. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

Summarise, model, infer

Engineering data vary from one measurement to the next. The first job is to summarise a set of values by its centre and spread. The second is to model the variation with probability and a distribution such as the binomial, Poisson or normal. The third is to use a sample to say something about the whole process, with a confidence interval or a test. Regression and control charts then apply the same ideas to relationships and to production.

CHAPTER 1

The Ten Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · Descriptive statistics

Centre and position

mean = Σx / n
Rule: Combine means by weight. An outlier moves the mean more than the median. Coding changes the spread only by the multiplier.
Block 2 · Spread

Variance and standard deviation

s² = SS / (n − 1)
Rule: Sample variance divides by n − 1. Independent variances add. The coefficient of variation compares spreads of different scales.
Block 3 · Probability rules

Add, multiply, complement

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)
Rule: Take the complement for “at least one”. Series multiplies reliabilities; parallel multiplies failures.
Block 4 · Conditional and counting

Given, Bayes, arrangements

P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)
Rule: Rare conditions give mostly false positives. Count combinations when order is irrelevant.
Block 5 · Discrete distributions

Binomial and Poisson

C(n, x) pˣ (1 − p)ⁿ⁻ˣ
Rule: Binomial needs a fixed number of trials. Poisson counts events; scale the mean to the length or time.
Block 6 · Continuous distributions

Density and area

∫ f(x) dx = 1
Rule: Total area is 1. The exponential is memoryless, with mean 1/λ. E(X²) − [E(X)]² gives the variance.
Block 7 · The normal distribution

Standardise and look up

z = (x − μ) / σ
Rule: Add variances for sums. Use a continuity correction when a normal replaces a binomial.
Block 8 · Sampling and intervals

How far a mean can be trusted

SE = σ / √n
Rule: Four times the sample halves the width. Use t when σ is estimated. Round a sample size up.
Block 9 · Hypothesis tests

Statistic against critical value

z = (x̄ − μ₀) / SE
Rule: The significance level is the Type I error rate. Not rejecting is not proof. A p-value below α means reject.
Block 10 · Regression and quality

Fit, correlate, control

b = S_xy / S_xx
Rule: r² is the share of variation explained. Control limits for means use σ / √n. C_p compares tolerance width with 6σ.

Question 1

Solution

Find the median of 3, 9, 1, 7, 5, 11.
Solution
1Put the values in order: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11.
2With six values the median is the mean of the middle two: (5 + 7) ÷ 2 = 6.

Question 3

Solution

2The mode is the value that appears most often.
Answer5

Question 31
P(A ∩ B) = 0.2 and P(B) = 0.5. Find P(A | B).
Solution
1P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) ÷ P(B).

Answer0.4
Common slips: Dividing by P(A) instead gives P(B | A).
Question 32
Machine A makes 60% of parts, with 2% defective. Machine B makes 40%, with 5% defective. Find the overall probability that a part is defective.
Solution
1P(D) = P(A)P(D | A) + P(B)P(D | B).
20.6 × 0.02 + 0.4 × 0.05 = 0.012 + 0.020 = 0.032.
Answer0.032
Common slips: Averaging 2% and 5% gives 3.5%, ignoring the shares.

Answer0.375
Common slips: The answer is not 0.6: the defect rate of A is lower than of B.
