Engineering · Machining and Forming

Workshop Practice II

Every machining answer follows one chain: cutting speed gives spindle speed, spindle speed and feed give time, and force and power come after. Learn the chain once and each machine is a small variation. One hundred problems, every step shown.

ORIENTATION

One chain, many machines

A lathe, a mill, a drill and a grinder all cut by the same rule: the edge of the tool must travel past the work at a set speed. From that speed come the spindle speed, the feed rate and the time of the cut. Forming and welding have their own rules, and each is a single product of a few quantities.

CHAPTER 1

The Eleven Blocks

One card per block, with the rule and the trap to avoid.

Block 1 · Holding and tapers

Hold, support, then taper

tan α = (D − d) / 2L
Rule: Three jaws centre round work; four jaws take square or off-centre work. A long slender bar needs a support. The compound slide is set to the half angle.
Block 2 · Lathe calculations

Finish, power and life

Ra = f² / 32r
Rule: Halve the feed and the roughness falls to a quarter. Power is force × speed ÷ 60 000. Cutting speed shortens tool life most.
Block 3 · Milling

Speed, direction and removal rate

N = 1000 v / πD
Rule: Up milling cuts thin to thick. Climb milling needs a table without backlash. Removal rate is width × depth × feed.
Block 4 · Indexing and gears

Divide the turn, size the blank

crank = 40 / N
Rule: One crank turn is 9° on a 40:1 head. Blank diameter is m(z + 2). A hexagon is 1.155 times its flat size across corners.
Block 5 · Shaping

Feed on the return

ratio = cutting angle / return angle
Rule: The tool cuts on the forward stroke only. The table is fed during the return. Cutting time is strokes needed ÷ strokes per minute.
Block 6 · Grinding

Hard work, soft wheel

v = πDN / 60
Rule: A shiny face and burning mean a glazed wheel. Surface speed falls as the wheel wears but never exceeds the printed limit in rpm.
Block 7 · Drilling and location

Smaller drill, higher speed

N = 1000 v / πD
Rule: Halve the diameter and the speed doubles. A jig guides the tool. 3-2-1 location removes all six freedoms.
Block 8 · CNC

Position, speed, correct

F = f_z × z × N
Rule: G90 is from program zero, G91 from the last point. G00 never goes into material. F changes unit with G94 and G95. Backlash is lost at reversal.
Block 9 · Welding

Heat per millimetre

H = ηVI / v ; Q = I²Rt
Rule: Slower travel puts in more heat. Double the current in a spot weld and the heat is four times larger.
Block 10 · Press work

Cut, bend, draw

F = πD t τ
Rule: The die is made to the blank size for blanking. Bend allowance uses the neutral axis. A draw has a limit near 2 for D ÷ d.
Block 11 · Heat treatment

Slower cooling, softer steel

quench > air > furnace
Rule: Quenching makes steel hard and brittle; tempering reduces the brittleness; furnace cooling gives the softest steel.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 1

Lathe: work holding, tool angles and tapers

Questions 1–12. Hold the work, support it if it is slender, then cut the taper by the half angle.

Question 1
Complete the statement: A three-jaw chuck holds a round bar running true without any adjusting, because …
Solution
1A spiral groove (the scroll) moves all three jaws in or out at the same time, so the bar centres itself.
2Its accuracy depends on the wear of the scroll and jaws, so work needing high accuracy is turned in one setting.
Answerthe three jaws are moved together by one scroll
Question 2
Complete the statement: A square bar must be turned round about its own centre. The chuck needed is …
Solution
1Three jaws cannot grip a square section properly: they bear on corners and the bar can slip.
2A four-jaw chuck has independent jaws, so square, rectangular and off-centre pieces can be clamped and then set true with a dial indicator.
Answera four-jaw chuck, each jaw set by hand
Question 3
Complete the statement: A bar of 20 mm diameter sticks 300 mm out of the chuck and bends away from the tool, so the cut tapers. The usual remedy is to …
Solution
1The bar acts like a cantilever.
2The bend under the cutting force grows with the cube of the overhang, and 300 ÷ 20 is a ratio of 15, far beyond the ratio of about 5 that a chuck alone can hold.
3A support at the free end removes the overhang.
4A deeper cut increases the force, and speed does not change the stiffness.
Answersupport the free end with a live centre or a steady rest
Question 4
Complete the statement: When the rake angle of a turning tool is increased, the cutting force becomes …
Solution
1A larger positive rake lets the chip curl away easily, so the force and the heat fall.
2The wedge of metal behind the edge gets thinner, so the edge is more likely to chip.
3Hard materials are cut with small or negative rake for that reason.
Answersmaller, but the edge is weaker
Question 5
Complete the statement: A tool with too little clearance angle will …
Solution
1Clearance keeps the flank of the tool from touching the freshly cut surface.
2Without it the flank rubs, the friction heats the tool and the work, and the finish is poor.
3Too much clearance leaves a thin, weak edge, usually 5 to 10° is used.
Answerrub on the work, heat up and wear on its flank
Question 6
In the diagram, name the angle γ between the tool face and the vertical dashed line.
Solution
Answerrake angle
Common slips: The angle α below the flank is the clearance angle.
Question 7
Complete the statement: The compound slide is chosen for taper turning when the taper is …
Solution
1The slide can be set to any angle, but its travel is short and it is fed by hand, which leaves a poorer finish.
2Long, gentle tapers are turned by tailstock set-over or with a taper attachment.
Answershort and steep, and fed by hand
Question 8
Complete the statement: A taper has large diameter D, small diameter d and length L. The compound slide is set to the angle α where …
Solution
1The slide is set to the half angle, because the tool moves only one side of the axis.
2The change of radius is (D − d) / 2 over the length L.
3Leaving out the 2 gives the full included angle in the wrong place.
4Adding the diameters has no meaning here.
Answertan α = (D − d) / (2L)
Question 9
A taper has D = 40 mm, d = 30 mm and L = 100 mm. Find the angle to which the compound slide is set.
Solution
1tan α = 10 / 200 = 0.05, so α = 2.86°.
Answer2.86°
Question 10
Complete the statement: Tailstock set-over is best used for tapers that are …
Solution
1Moving the tailstock sideways tilts the bar, so the tool cuts a taper along it.
2It only suits a small angle, since the centres are no longer in line.
3It cannot be used when the work is held in a chuck.
Answerlong and gentle, turned between centres
Question 11
Complete the statement: A bar of length L is turned between centres. Only a length l of it carries the taper, from D to d. The tailstock set-over is …
Solution
1The bar is tilted by the half angle of the taper, so the end moves sideways by L times its tangent, and tan α = (D − d) / (2l).
2The simpler form (D − d) / 2 is only right when the taper runs the full length of the bar (l = L).
AnswerS = L (D − d) / (2 l)
Question 12
A taper has D = 40 mm and d = 30 mm over a taper length of 100 mm. The bar is 200 mm long. Find the set-over.
Solution
1S = 200 × (40 − 30) ÷ (2 × 100) = 10 mm.
Answer10 mm
Common slips: The 5 mm is (D − d) / 2, which forgets the bar is longer than the taper. The 20 mm leaves out the 2. The 2.5 mm turns the length ratio upside down.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 2

Lathe: threads, surface finish, power and tool life

Questions 13–24. Finish depends on the square of the feed; power is force times speed; tool life falls steeply with speed.

Question 13
Complete the statement: During thread cutting, the carriage is driven by …
Solution
1The lead screw gives a fixed, accurate movement for each turn of the spindle.
2The feed rod, which is smooth and gives only plain feeds, is not accurate enough for threads.
Answerthe lead screw, through a closed half-nut
Question 14
A double-start thread has a pitch of 2 mm. Find its lead (the distance the nut moves in one turn).
Solution
1Lead = pitch × number of starts = 2 × 2 = 4 mm.
2For a single-start thread lead and pitch are equal.
3A lathe must be set to the lead, not the pitch, or the thread will have the wrong rate of advance.
Answer4 mm
Question 15
The lead screw of a lathe has a pitch of 6 mm. A thread of pitch 1.5 mm is to be cut. How many turns must the work make for each turn of the lead screw?
Solution
1In one turn of the lead screw the carriage moves 6 mm.
2The tool must move 1.5 mm for each turn of the work, so the work turns 6 ÷ 1.5 = 4 times.
Answer4 times
Common slips: The 0.25 is the ratio upside down. The 7.5 adds the two pitches.
Question 16
Complete the statement: To make a turned surface smoother, the operator should use …
Solution
1The tool leaves a row of ridges, one for each turn of the work.
2A smaller feed makes the ridges closer together; a larger nose radius makes each ridge shallower.
3Both changes lower the roughness.
Answera smaller feed and a larger nose radius
Question 17
The theoretical roughness is Ra = f² ÷ (32 r). If the feed f is halved, by what factor does the roughness change?
Solution
1Roughness depends on the square of the feed: (1/2)² = 1/4.
2This is why a finishing cut uses a very small feed, and why the small feed is more effective than a bigger nose radius, which only enters once.
Answerfalls to one quarter
Question 18
A tool of nose radius 0.8 mm cuts with a feed of 0.2 mm/rev. Find the theoretical roughness Ra.
Solution
1Ra = 0.2² ÷ (32 × 0.8) = 0.04 ÷ 25.6 = 0.00156 mm = 1.6 μm.
Answer1.6 μm
Common slips: The 16 and 0.16 are slips of a decimal place in the change from mm to μm. The 3.1 μm comes from using 16 instead of 32 in the formula.
Question 19
Complete the statement: The power used at the cutting tool is found from …
Solution
1Power is force times speed, in the same way as for a lifting or pushing force.
2The depth of cut and feed enter through the force, not directly.
Answercutting force × cutting speed
Question 20
Cutting force is in newtons and cutting speed in m/min. By what number is Fc × v divided to give the power in kW?
Solution
1N × m/min is divided by 60 to give watts (N × m/s).
2Watts are divided by 1 000 to give kilowatts.
360 × 1 000 = 60 000.
Answer60 000
Common slips: Dividing by 1 000 only gives a result in kN·m/min; dividing by 60 only gives watts.
Question 21
A cutting force of 1 500 N acts at a cutting speed of 60 m/min. The machine is 75% efficient. Find the motor power needed.
Solution
1Power at the tool = 1 500 × 60 ÷ 60 000 = 1.5 kW.
2The motor must supply more than this because of the losses: 1.5 ÷ 0.75 = 2.0 kW.
Answer2.0 kW
Common slips: The 1.1 kW multiplies by the efficiency instead of dividing. The 1.5 kW forgets the losses. The 3.0 kW would be the answer for an efficiency of 50%.
Question 22
Complete the statement: Taylor’s tool life equation links …
Solution
1The equation is V Tⁿ = C.
2V is the cutting speed, T the tool life in minutes, and n and C are constants for the tool and work material.
Answercutting speed and tool life
Question 23
Which increase shortens tool life the most, if each is raised by the same percentage?
Solution
1Speed has the largest effect, since heat at the edge rises sharply with it.
2Feed comes next and depth of cut has the smallest effect.
3If production must go up, it is cheaper in tool life to increase depth and feed before speed.
Answercutting speed
Question 24
A tool lasts 60 min at a certain speed, with n = 0.25. The speed is raised by 20% (a factor of 1.2). Find the new tool life.
Solution
1V Tⁿ = C gives T₂ = T₁ ÷ 1.2^(1/n) = 60 ÷ 1.2⁴ = 60 ÷ 2.07 = 29 min.
Answer29 min
Common slips: The 50 min divides by 1.2 only. The 72 min multiplies by 1.2. The 12 min divides by 5.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 3

Milling: cutting direction, speed and removal rate

Questions 25–33. Spindle speed comes from the cutting speed; the removal rate is the product of width, depth and feed.

Question 25
Complete the statement: In up (conventional) milling, the chip …
Solution
1The tooth enters the work at the surface moving against the feed, rubs before it bites, and cuts a chip that grows to full thickness as it leaves.
2In down milling it is the other way round.
Answerstarts thin and ends thick
Question 26
Complete the statement: Down (climb) milling should be avoided on a machine with …
Solution
1In down milling the cutter pulls the work forward in the direction of the feed.
2If there is backlash, the table jumps forward into the gap, the cutter digs in and may break or ruin the work.
3A rigid machine with a ball-screw drive is safe.
Answerbacklash in the table feed screw
Question 27
In the diagram, the cutter in B moves, at the point of contact, in the same direction as the feed. Name this type of milling.
Solution
1In A, the cutter moves against the feed.
Answerdown (climb) milling
Question 28
Complete the statement: The cutting speed of a milling cutter is the speed of …
Solution
1It is the speed at which the cutting edge travels past the work, in m/min.
2It depends on the work material and the cutter material, not on the machine.
3The spindle speed is then set from it.
Answerthe cutter edge at its outer diameter
Question 29
A cutter of diameter D (mm) is to run at cutting speed v (m/min). Write the spindle speed N (rev/min).
Solution
1The cutter edge travels πD mm in one turn.
2In one minute it must travel 1 000 v mm.
3So N = 1 000 v ÷ πD.
AnswerN = 1000 v / (π D)
Question 30
A cutter of 80 mm diameter is to run at a cutting speed of 25 m/min. Find the spindle speed.
Solution
1N = 1 000 × 25 ÷ (π × 80) = 99.5 rev/min.
Answer99 rev/min
Common slips: The 9.9 and 990 are slips of a decimal place. The 313 comes from leaving out π.
Question 31
Complete the statement: The metal removal rate in milling is …
Solution
1In one minute the table moves a length equal to the feed.
2The volume removed is the cross-section of the cut (width × depth) times that length.
Answerwidth of cut × depth of cut × table feed
Question 32
The depth of cut is doubled and the table feed is halved. The width of cut is unchanged. By what factor does the removal rate change?
Solution
1The rate depends on the product of depth and feed.
2Doubling one and halving the other gives 2 × ½ = 1.
3Changing one variable at a time is easy; with two changes the product must be checked.
Answeris unchanged
Question 33
A cut is 50 mm wide and 4 mm deep, at a table feed of 320 mm/min. Find the metal removal rate.
Solution
150 × 4 × 320 = 64 000 mm³/min.
Answer64 000 mm³/min
Common slips: The 6 400 and 640 are slips of a decimal place. The 1 067 is the correct value per second (64 000 ÷ 60) with the wrong unit.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 4

Indexing, gear blanks and hexagons

Questions 34–44. On a 40:1 head, one crank turn is 9°. Gear and hexagon sizes follow from simple geometry.

Question 34
To divide the work into N equal parts by simple indexing on a 40:1 head, write the number of crank turns.
Solution
1One full turn of the work needs 40 turns of the crank.
2One of N divisions needs 40 ÷ N turns.
Answer40 ÷ N turns
Common slips: The 360 ÷ N is the angle of each division in degrees, not the crank turns.
Question 35
A gear blank needs 18 divisions on a 40:1 head. Find the crank movement.
Solution
140 ÷ 18 = 2 and 4/18, so 2 full turns and 4 holes on a circle with 18 holes (or any multiple, such as 4 holes of 18).
Answer2 full turns and 4 holes on an 18-hole circle
Common slips: The fraction must be counted on a circle whose hole number matches the denominator; on a 20-hole circle the same 4 holes would give 4/20.
Question 36
To turn the work through θ degrees on a 40:1 head, write the number of crank turns.
Solution
1One crank turn gives 9°.
2So θ degrees need θ ÷ 9 turns.
Answerθ ÷ 9 turns
Common slips: The 360 ÷ θ is the number of equal divisions, not the crank turns.
Question 37
On a 40:1 head, the crank is moved by one hole on an 18-hole circle. Find the angle the work turns through.
Solution
1One hole is 1/18 of a crank turn, and a crank turn is 9°: 9 ÷ 18 = 0.5°.
2Angles that are a whole number of half degrees can be set directly on the 18-hole circle.
Answer0.5°
Common slips: The 9° is the whole turn.
Question 38
The work is to be turned through 47° on a 40:1 head. Find the crank movement.
Solution
147 ÷ 9 = 5.222 turns.
24 turns and 14 holes give 43°.
Answer5 turns and 4 holes on an 18-hole circle
Common slips: The 0.222 of a turn is 4/18, so 4 holes on the 18-hole circle. 5 turns and 2 holes would give 46°. On a 20-hole circle the 4 holes would give 5.2 turns, which is 46.8°.
Question 39
A spur gear of module m has z teeth. Write its outside diameter.
Solution
1The pitch circle diameter is m × z.
Answerm (z + 2)
Common slips: The addendum, which is one module, adds to the radius on each side, so 2m is added to the diameter. m z alone is the pitch circle diameter.
Question 40
Complete the statement: For a standard tooth, the addendum and the dedendum are …
Solution
1The part of the tooth above the pitch circle is 1 m.
2The part below is 1.25 m, so that the tip of the mating tooth has a small clearance at the root.
3The whole depth is 2.25 m.
Answer1 m and 1.25 m
Question 41
A gear has module 2.5 mm and 24 teeth. Find the blank outside diameter.
Solution
1D = m (z + 2) = 2.5 × 26 = 65 mm.
Answer65 mm
Common slips: The 60 mm is the pitch circle diameter (2.5 × 24). The 62.5 mm uses z + 1. The 67.5 mm uses z + 3.
Question 42
A hexagon has a distance A across the flats. Write the distance across the corners.
Solution
1Across corners = A ÷ cos 30° = 1.155 A.
Answer1.155 A
Common slips: The 0.866 is A × cos 30° (the wrong way). The 1.414 is the ratio for a square, not a hexagon.
Question 43
A hexagon is cut by turning the work through 60° between flats. On a 40:1 head, find the crank turns.
Solution
160 ÷ 9 = 6.667, which is 6 and 2/3 turns.
2On a 27-hole circle this is 6 turns and 18 holes.
Answer6 and 2/3 turns
Common slips: Using 6 turns only would give 54°.
Question 44
A hexagon 30 mm across flats is milled from round bar. Find the smallest bar diameter.
Solution
1The corners of the hexagon lie on a circle of 30 ÷ cos 30° = 34.64 mm.
2A bar of 30 mm would leave the corners unfinished.
Answer34.6 mm in diameter
Common slips: The 26 mm is 30 × cos 30°, which is too small. The 60 mm is twice the flat size.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 5

Shaping and the quick-return stroke

Questions 45–50. Cutting is on the forward stroke only. The quick return saves time but does not change the cutting speed.

Question 45
Complete the statement: A shaper cuts metal during …
Solution
1The tool cuts on the forward stroke.
2On the return stroke the tool head lifts on its hinge and the tool slides back clear of the work, so no metal is removed.
3That idle stroke is why the machine has a quick return.
Answerthe forward stroke of the ram only
Question 46
Complete the statement: On a shaper, the table feed is applied …
Solution
1The table moves sideways by one feed at the end of each cutting stroke.
2Moving it while the tool is cutting would drag the tool across the work and break it.
Answerduring the return stroke, clear of the work
Question 47
A flat surface 150 mm wide is shaped with a feed of 1.5 mm per stroke at 60 double strokes per minute. Find the cutting time.
Solution
1Strokes needed = width ÷ feed = 150 ÷ 1.5 = 100.
2Time = 100 ÷ 60 = 1.67 min.
Answer1.67 min
Common slips: The 0.60 min comes from dividing 60 by 100 (upside down). The 2.50 min comes from 150 ÷ 60, which ignores the feed. The 100 min treats 100 strokes as 100 minutes.
Question 48
Complete the statement: The quick-return ratio of a shaper is …
Solution
1The ratio is more than 1 because the return stroke takes less time.
2Shapers usually have a ratio between 3:2 and 2:1.
Answercutting-stroke time ÷ return-stroke time
Question 49
The crank of a quick-return mechanism turns at constant speed. The cutting stroke uses 220° of the turn and the return stroke uses 140°. Find the ratio.
Solution
1Because the crank speed is constant, the times are in the same ratio as the angles: 220 ÷ 140 = 1.57.
2A ratio of 1.00 would mean there is no quick return at all.
Answer1.57 : 1
Common slips: The 0.64 is the ratio upside down.
Question 50
A shaper makes 30 double strokes per minute with a stroke of 250 mm. The cutting stroke uses 220° of the 360° crank turn. Find the average cutting speed.
Solution
1One cycle takes 60 ÷ 30 = 2 s.
2The cutting part is 2 × 220/360 = 1.222 s.
3Speed = 0.25 m ÷ 1.222 s = 0.2045 m/s = 12.3 m/min.
Answer12.3 m/min
Common slips: The 7.5 m/min spreads the cut over the whole cycle (0.25 × 30). The 15.0 m/min counts both strokes. The 19.3 m/min is the return speed, found with 140°.
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 6

Grinding: wheel marking, speed and dressing

Questions 51–59. Match the wheel to the work: hard work needs a soft wheel. Surface speed is πDN.

Question 51
Complete the statement: In the wheel marking A 60 K 5 V, the letter K gives the …
Solution
1The marking is read in order: A = aluminium oxide abrasive, 60 = grit size (medium), K = grade (medium hardness), 5 = structure (spacing of grains), V = vitrified bond.
2Grade is how firmly the bond holds the grains.
Answerhardness (grade) of the wheel
Question 52
Complete the statement: To grind a very hard steel, the wheel chosen should be …
Solution
1Hard work dulls the grains quickly.
2A soft-grade wheel releases dulled grains so fresh sharp ones come forward.
3A hard-grade wheel keeps the dull grains, which rub, heat and burn the work.
4The rule is: hard work, soft wheel; soft work, hard wheel.
Answera soft grade, so dulled grains break away
Question 53
A hardened tool steel is to be finish-ground. Choose the most suitable wheel marking.
Solution
1Steel needs aluminium oxide (A).
2Finish grinding needs a fine grit (80).
3Hard work needs a soft grade (H).
4C 24 T has silicon carbide, a coarse grit and a hard grade, so it is wrong on all three.
5A 24 T keeps aluminium oxide but is coarse and hard.
6C 80 H is fine and soft, but silicon carbide suits cast iron and non-ferrous metals, not steel.
AnswerA 80 H 5 V
Question 54
Complete the statement: The maximum speed printed on a grinding wheel …
Solution
1A wheel can burst when run too fast, because the stress in the wheel rises with the square of the speed.
2The limit is part of the wheel and applies to every size.
Answermust never be exceeded by the machine spindle
Question 55
A grinding wheel wears from 300 mm to 240 mm diameter at the same spindle speed. By how much does the surface speed change?
Solution
1v = πDN, so v is in proportion to D.
2240 ÷ 300 = 0.8, a fall of 20%.
3As the wheel wears, the operator may raise the rpm to bring the surface speed back, but never above the maximum rpm marked on the wheel.
Answerfalls by 20%, in proportion to the diameter
Common slips: The 36% comes from squaring (0.8²).
Question 56
A wheel of 300 mm diameter runs at 1 800 rpm. Find its surface speed.
Solution
1v = π × D × N ÷ 60 with D in metres: π × 0.3 × 1 800 ÷ 60 = 28.3 m/s.
Answer28.3 m/s
Common slips: The 14.1 m/s uses the radius. The 56.5 m/s doubles the diameter. The 1 696 is the speed in metres per minute, written with the wrong unit.
Question 57
Complete the statement: Dressing a grinding wheel …
Solution
1A diamond or a dressing tool is passed over the face.
2It breaks off dull grains and clogged metal, so the wheel cuts freely again.
Answerclears the face and exposes sharp grains
Question 58
Complete the statement: Truing differs from dressing because truing …
Solution
1Truing makes the wheel round and the face straight, or cuts a profile on it.
2Dressing sharpens it.
3One pass of a diamond often does both.
Answercorrects the wheel shape and runout
Question 59
Complete the statement: A wheel grinding hardened steel has a shiny face and the work shows burn marks. The wheel is …
Solution
1A shiny face means the grains are worn flat and are not breaking away, which is glazing.
2The wheel rubs instead of cutting, so the work heats and burns.
3Dressing restores the cut.
4If glazing returns, the grade is too hard for this work, so a softer one is needed.
5A loaded wheel looks dull and clogged with metal.
6Imbalance causes vibration, not burning.
Answerglazed; dress it, then try a softer grade
CHAPTER 2 · BLOCK 7

Drilling, jigs and fixtures

Questions 60–64. A smaller drill turns faster for the same rim speed. Jigs guide; fixtures hold.

Question 60
A 20 mm drill is replaced by a 10 mm drill at the same cutting speed. How does the spindle speed change?
Solution
1N = 1000 v ÷ (πD), so N is in inverse proportion to D.
2Half the diameter means double the speed.
Answerdoubles
Common slips: Quadrupling would only be right if N depended on D squared.
Question 61
A 10 mm drill cuts at 31.4 m/min with a feed of 0.15 mm per revolution. Find the time to drill a hole 30 mm deep.
Solution
1N = 1000 × 31.4 ÷ (π × 10) = 1 000 rpm.
2Feed rate = 0.15 × 1 000 = 150 mm/min = 2.5 mm/s.
3Time = 30 ÷ 2.5 = 12 s.
Answer12 s
Common slips: The 0.2 s is 30 ÷ 150 = 0.2 min with the unit changed to seconds. The 24 s comes from halving the rpm. The 200 s is 30 ÷ 0.15, which forgets to multiply by the rpm.
Question 62
Complete the statement: A drilling jig differs from a fixture because the jig …
Solution
1A jig holds the work and guides the tool, so each hole lands in the same place without marking out.
2A fixture holds and locates the work but does not guide the tool; the tool is set to the work with a set block or gauge.

Question 66

Question 67

Solution

Question 69
Complete the statement: G02 and G03 differ in that …

Question 70

Question 71

Question 73

Question 75

Question 76

Question 81

Question 82

Welding: arc heat input and spot welding

Questions 83–88. Heat input is energy per millimetre; spot-weld heat grows with the square of the current.

Question 83
Complete the statement: The heat put into a weld, for each millimetre of its length, rises when …
Solution
1Heat input = arc power × efficiency ÷ travel speed.
2A slow torch leaves more heat in each millimetre.
3Too much heat widens the weld zone and softens the metal near it.
Answerthe travel speed falls
Question 84
Complete the statement: An arc efficiency of 0.8 means …
Solution
1The rest of the energy is lost to the air, the spatter and the radiation.
2Manual metal arc is about 0.8; submerged arc is about 0.95.
Answer80% of the arc energy enters the work
Question 85
A weld is made at 25 V and 200 A, travelling at 240 mm/min, with an arc efficiency of 0.8. Find the heat input.
Solution
1Power = 25 × 200 = 5 000 W; with efficiency 4 000 W.
2Speed = 240 ÷ 60 = 4 mm/s.
3Heat input = 4 000 ÷ 4 = 1 000 J/mm = 1.0 kJ/mm.
Answer1.0 kJ/mm
Common slips: The 1.25 leaves out the efficiency. The 0.8 applies the efficiency twice. The 16.7 divides by 240 without changing minutes to seconds.
Question 86
Complete the statement: In resistance spot welding, the heat comes from …
Solution
1The sheets are pressed between copper electrodes and a large current passes through them.
2The contact between the sheets has the highest resistance, so it gets hottest and melts into a nugget.
Answercurrent passing through the resistance of the joint
Question 87
In spot welding, the current is doubled and the time is unchanged. By what factor does the heat produced change?
Solution
1Heat = I²Rt.
2Doubling I multiplies the heat by 2² = 4.
Answerbecomes four times larger
Common slips: Eight times would need the time to double as well.
Question 88
A spot weld passes 8 kA through a joint resistance of 100 μΩ for 0.2 s. Find the heat generated.
Solution
1Heat = I²Rt = 8 000² × 0.0001 × 0.2 = 64 000 000 × 0.0001 × 0.2 = 1 280 J.
Answer1 280 J
Common slips: The 6 400 J forgets the time. The 0.16 J uses I × R × t, without squaring the current. The 320 J is the heat at 4 kA.
